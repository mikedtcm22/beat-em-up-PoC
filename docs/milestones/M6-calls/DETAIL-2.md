## What the stages show (item 1: is the quilting there?)

The dense mesh (`04_parts`) has the puffer's folds as shallow valleys. Measured on the 19.5k mesh with a signed dihedral (`valley_diag.py`, scratch): of 30,239 edges 8,803 are concave; valley edges over 20 degrees in runs of 0.25 m number 2,771, over 28 degrees 2,488, over 38 degrees 2,237. Over 28 degrees they are nearly all the feet of the plates (the steel's own creases already ink those); the jacket's folds and the sleeve's bands are the 10-22 degree valleys, and they are real but soft: a fold's dihedral spreads over several edges of a few degrees each. So the jacket reads as a lumpy puffer, not as the concept's stitched channels: the model has the folds, not the stitching. A `geometry_quality detailed` regeneration (Tripo 50-85 credits) would give a denser, sharper sculpt, but the folds would stay folds; the stitched channels the concept draws are in no mesh we have. Not spent.

## Calls

**E-1 Valleys as their own ink pass (`crease.valley`, zone `valley_deg`).** Concave edges only, at a lower angle and a longer chain than the ridges: the cloth zone asks 11 degrees and 0.35 m, the steel none (its ridges at 30-58 degrees already draw the plates, and a valley at a plate's foot a centimetre from its ridge would double the line into a thick one). / One lower angle for the ridges and valleys together. / It would ink every sculpt bump on the steel as a scribble; the sign tells a fold from a bevel. 22 degrees (0.25 m) found almost nothing on the jacket; 16 a few; 10-11 with 0.3-0.35 m gives the diagonal fold lines at the hem and the sleeves (jacket ink 0.0002 in M6, 0.098 in the pilot, 0.0795 now with thinner lines).

**E-2 Zones that switch ink off: `scarf`, `helmet`.** The valley pass on the whole cloth zone scribbled the scarf (a first look at 11 degrees showed hatching over the face cloth). A `scarf` zone (slot 44, x within 0.2, in front, z 1.3-1.68) and a `helmet` zone (z over 1.68) come after `cloth` in the list (the last zone wins) with `valley_deg` 181; the scarf's crease is off too. / Raising the valley angle for the whole cloth. / Then the jacket loses its folds; the scarf and the helmet are the only smooth cloth.

**E-3 The helmet back is purple (`palette.regions`: `helmet_back`, `helmet_top`).** The first jacket view showed the helmet's back and sides in steel patches (the texture's grey there); the concept's helmet is purple with a steel visor. Two boxes paint slot 44 over faces of slots 45 and 46 only (the visor, in front of y 0.02 below z 1.71, stays). / Merging the patches with a larger island area. / They were not islands: the steel covered half the helmet. Tested: the helmet's top is at least 95% purple.

**E-4 Colour borders follow the texture: a palette slot per texel in the mask's B (`texel_swatch`).** Of the four options in the brief this is the first, with the decode made safe at a border: the shader reads B with one `texelFetch` (no filter, no mip), so a code is never interpolated, and the swatch is looked up in the atlas with the texel's slot (colour, damage class and gloss all follow). The labels are made in the bake: the Lab-nearest slot of the dense texture's colour (blurred 1 px), allowed only among the slots of the texel's dense face and its neighbours, then a 3 x 3 majority twice, then the island padding. Measured: 205k of 233k texels are decided by the texture; 1.1% of all texels differ from their face's swatch; the colour edge of the scarf, the visor frame and the knee pads follow the texture at 4 mm instead of 20 mm steps. / (a) A separate small swatch texture: a second sampler for the same job. (b) Subdividing the border faces: each border face split into ten is 2,000 to 4,000 triangles over the 15k budget, and the decimator would take them back. (c) The face-only labels with a better filter: the stair is the face size. / (a) costs a texture and a binding; (b) costs the budget; the mask is already on UV2 and already loaded.

**E-5 Opt-in by data, not by a uniform the art sets: the mask's alpha is the marker.** The brief prefers a uniform or metadata flag. The art reaches the material through `rig_paint.gd` (not mine), which passes only `detail_sharp`, so a flag on the art would need a change there (listed in requests). Without a flag, a code in B is not enough: the hero's mask has a grey in B. The mask that carries codes is an RGBA PNG whose alpha is 128/255 (`pipe_detail.CODE_MARK`); every other mask is opaque RGB. The shader takes a code only where alpha is under 0.9, so the Punk, the hero, the Kicker, the Thug and Ox render as before (test: no other mask has alpha under 0.999). A first marker of 254/255 was dropped by Godot's import (the loader treats an alpha of 250 and over as opaque and imports RGB), which showed as a render identical to the face swatches: the A/B is visible (v_d against v_c differ in 1.1% of texels), and 128 survives. / A flag on the art in `rig_paint.gd`. / Not my file; the marker costs one texel's alpha and a uniform switch (`use_texel_swatch`, the Tuning A/B) already lets the lead turn it off.

**E-6 The fade from texel to face swatch (`texel_swatch_fade`, tunables 1.0 to 3.0 texels per pixel).** A nearest-sampled border at 5-10 m crawls when the camera moves (a texel is under a pixel). Past 1 texel per pixel (about 3 m for the Bruiser's 4 mm texels) the colour fades to the face's swatch, fully at 3 (about 10 m), where the pilot's look held with no speckle; the damage class switches at half the fade. / No fade (exact everywhere). / Uncertain at 5-10 m and one number to turn off. / Not checked in motion, only in stills (the sheets at 5 m show the same silhouette and borders as the pilot's); the tunables are the A/B if the lead sees crawling.

**E-7 Line weight 10.1 mm to 7.8 mm.** A line is `width_m` + 1.7 `ramp_m` across at the shader's cut (the 0.15 contour of a ramp): the pilot's 0.005 + 1.7 x 0.003 is 10.1 mm. Tried: 0.002/0.002 (5.4 mm: the chest's plate steps vanished through the stroke cleanup), 0.003/0.002 (6.4 mm), 0.004/0.0025 (8.2 mm); shipped 0.0035/0.0025 (7.8 mm) with the valley lines at 0.003 (6.4 mm). I did not measure the Punk's stroke width in numbers: his lines come from the texture's strokes, cleaned and blurred, and at 2.5 m the sheets show the Bruiser's lines in the same weight as his vest's outlines. / The Punk's weight by measurement. / The Punk's mask has no crease field to compare with; stated here as a judgement from the stills, for the lead to look at.

**E-8 Item 4, the purple on the steel and the knee pads: nothing more.** After E-4 the purple on the steel is the texture's own: the large purple oval on each forearm guard is the purple sleeve cuff between the elbow and the guard (the concept has it), and the chest's V is the scarf. A per-zone island merge (`island_zones` with 0.012 m2 over the forearms and knees) was built and merged 127 more faces, but the patches that remain are not islands under that area, so it changed nothing a player would see; removed again. / Keeping it. / Dead code.

**E-9 The 20k variant is not shipped; sheet for the ruling.** `palette.budget_tris` 20000 (the body takes 20000 - 2 x 1350 fists - 700 reserve = 16,600) builds in the same chain; the art's `triangle_budget` has to be 20000 too or `SkinnedArt.check()` fails and the primitives show (a first recording showed purple blocks). `ab_bruiser_20k.png`: at 1 m and 2.5 m the 20k has slightly cleaner guard and plate shapes (the bevels of the steps) and the same lines and colours; at 5 m no difference. Ship 15k. / 20k as the default. / SPEC section 10 says 15k for an enemy, and the difference is a few pixels of silhouette; to switch, the config value, the art's `triangle_budget`, the roster's `budget` and the strain/A9 ratchets (a new mesh) all move.

**E-10 No ratchet moved.** The GLB is byte-identical to the pilot's (the mesh depends on none of the new keys), so A6/A7/A9, strain and the roster rows are as FB-DETAIL left them. `tests/test_m6_feedback_detail.gd`: the plate-ink floor (0.2) still passes at 0.2094 with the thinner lines (it was 0.322); not changed. Twin assertions added on the visible path: codes on every texel and a few per cent differing from the face, other masks unmarked, the decode and the shader's strings, the material's switch, the helmet, the line weight and the quilting config.

**E-11 Perf.** `tools/perf_compare.sh --force --vsync on --runs 3` of `aca0c10` against HEAD, the acts "Look: Bruiser at 1 m" (the Bruiser filling the view, the worst case for the shader) and "Look: heavies beside a Punk", on the shared machine at a load of about 4.4 (above the tool's bar of 2.0, so `--force`; vsync on, so frame times follow the display): heavies mean 16.16 to 16.26 ms, p95 21.25 to 21.21; 1 m p95 19.80 to 20.36 ms with a worst frame of 27.0 to 24.7 (the run-to-run range is wider than the delta); renderer counters identical (draws 31.04, objects 41.04, prims the same). One `texelFetch` and a second atlas read on the Bruiser's fragments only; Punk and the rest pay the branch on a uniform. No cost that the noise shows; not a quiet-machine number.

## Rollout recipe, part 2 (what each new key changes, and start values for the Kicker and the Thug)

New keys, all inside `palette.detail_bake`; absent = the old behaviour.

| Key | What it changes | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `crease.valley` `{angle_deg: 999, min_chain_m, width_m, ramp_m, spacing_m}` + a zone's `valley_deg` | draws concave edges (folds, quilting) at a lower angle than the ridges; 999 = off outside the zones that ask | one more kd-tree pass over the texels of those zones (the palette step about 10 s to about 11 s) |
| `texel_swatch` `{blur_px: 1.0, passes: 2, fill_px: 3, rings: 1}` | writes the slot per texel in B and makes the PNG RGBA with alpha 128/255 | the PNG is RGBA and 274 KB (the Punk's, RGB, is 96 KB); the shader fetches and reads one more atlas texel on that art |
| crease `width_m`, `ramp_m` | the line weight: `width_m + 1.7 * ramp_m` across | none |

Steps for a character, after the pilot's recipe (steps 1-8 of FB-DETAIL.md stay):

1. **Turn on `texel_swatch` first.** It needs no tuning: `blur_px` 1.0, `passes` 2, `rings` 1. Check the report: `texel_swatch.differ_from_face_share` should be 0.5-3% (the Bruiser 1.1%); over 8% means the texture is noisy and the ring or majority needs more (raise `passes`, or `blur_px` 1.5); under 0.2% means the faces were already clean. Import by hand (`godot --headless --import --quit`): the .import is unchanged and a changed PNG may not reload.
2. **Line weight**: `width_m` 0.0035, `ramp_m` 0.0025 for a 1.8 m character; 7.8 mm across is the heaviest line to keep at 1 m. For a slimmer model (the Kicker, the Thug) start at `width_m` 0.003, `ramp_m` 0.002 (6.4 mm).
3. **Valleys** only where the cloth is thick (a jacket, a coat, padding): `valley` with `min_chain_m` 0.35 and a zone `valley_deg` 11-16 on the cloth's slot; **off** (181) on the face, the scarf and the helmet or hat, and absent on steel. For the Kicker's and the Thug's thinner cloth start at 14-16 degrees and `min_chain_m` 0.3; if the crease report's `texels_on_a_crease` barely moves against the pilot-only build, the cloth has no valleys and it is a model without folds (leave it).
4. **Regions for a texture that painted a part the wrong colour** (the Bruiser's helmet): `palette.regions` boxes with `only_from`, as the Punk's do. Look from behind and from the side: the guard pose hides the back of the body and the jacket, and the texture's mistakes are there. The new act "Look: Bruiser jacket" (`tests/bot/acts/detail2_acts.gd`) turns a character 0, 90 and 150 degrees at 1.6 m; copy its subject id for another character.
5. **A thinner raw model** (the Kicker, the Thug): few valleys, shorter creases; the colour borders are where the gain is (their cloth blocks, belts and shoes are small, so texel swatches fix the 2 cm steps that are bigger than a belt buckle). Use `rings` 1.
6. **Check** every other character's `--check` is still YES (the shared files `pipe_detail.py`, `blender_palette.py` changed behind keys), then the character's own test file (copy `tests/test_m6_feedback_detail.gd`'s `test_the_mask_carries_a_palette_slot_per_texel` and `test_no_other_mask_has_codes`, adding the new id to the unmarked list's exceptions).
7. **Do not** raise `budget_tris` to 20000 for the look: the 20k sheet shows a few pixels of silhouette for 33% more triangles.

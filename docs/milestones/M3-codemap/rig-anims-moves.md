# M3 codemap: humanoid rig, placeholder art and animation, move data

Mapped on `m3/enemy-ai` at `3f5a866` (M2 merged). All paths are relative to the repo root. Every `file:line` points at the declaration. This area is **Milestone 1 placeholder art plus Milestone 2 ragdoll**: every enemy in M3 is a `HumanoidRig` built in code, its animations are procedural, and its attacks are `MoveData` resources generated by `tools/gen_moves.gd`.

## 0. What matters most for M3

1. **One rig for everyone.** The player, the dummies and every M3 enemy use `HumanoidRig` (`game/actors/humanoid_rig.gd`). Per actor you can change only `height` (uniform scale), four colors, `stance` and `wrapped_hands`, all **before `build()`**. There is no per-archetype mesh shape (a Bruiser can't be wider, a boss can't lose the cap) without editing `PlaceholderBody.parts()`.
2. **A new attack is four edits in shared files**: the generator (`tools/gen_moves.gd`), the move id list (`PlaceholderAnims.MOVE_IDS`), the recipe dispatch (`PlaceholderPoses._recipe()`) and a recipe function. On top come the test tables that pin every move (`tests/test_move_data.gd`, `tests/test_humanoid_rig.gd`) and the ARCHITECTURE table. All of these are append-at-the-end lists, so they are **merge hot spots** (section 16). The lead should land a scaffold commit before any parallel build.
3. **The cheapest sound way to author enemy attacks is the `dummy_jab` pattern** (`placeholder_poses.gd:1279`). Give the enemy move its own id, reuse a player recipe's strike pose (and follow-through), and replace only the wind-up with a big telegraph pose. Plus one small engine addition: a **held wind-up** (`MovePoses.windup_hold_at`, section 15.2). Without it, a 24-36 frame startup interpolates slowly from the wind-up into the strike and reads mushy.
4. **Enemy moves must not join the player-centric rig test tables** (`MOVES` in `test_humanoid_rig.gd:55`). Those tests demand EyePoint steadiness, the relaxed stance, first-person framing and a strike pose held through the active frames. Give enemies their own table and boxer-only checks.
5. **Scaling works** through `CombatActor.body_height` (`combat_actor.gd:60`, copied into `rig.height` at `combat_actor.gd:1477`). Hurtboxes, rig points, ragdoll bodies and masses all scale. **Hitbox radii, offsets and arc numbers do not scale**, and every strike pose aims at a 1.8 m target's head, so a big rig punches high (measured in section 11).
6. **Enemy grabs on the player need new animations.** `held` bends the victim over and drops its head to about 1.24 m. On the player, the first-person lens rides EyePoint, so it would sink into the holder's chest. See section 13.
7. **Rig build costs about 10 ms**, of which about 8.4 ms is the named animation library. Spawning six enemies in one tick costs about 60 ms. Cache the library per (scale, stance) or stagger the spawns (section 17).

### 2.6 Invariants

- **Time:** nothing advances by itself. The AnimationPlayer runs in MANUAL mode (`:829`). The owner passes sim dt. Tests rely on `advance(FRAME)` stepping exactly one frame.
- **Determinism:** after `advance()`, rig points, hurtboxes (written straight to the PhysicsServer, `:1005`) and attachment nodes all show the same pose. Hit detection reads `get_point_transform()` (animation FK) only.
- **Cross-fades are the rig's own** (`_start` `:843`, `_apply_fade` `:862`). The AnimationPlayer always plays one clip at full weight. Never call `anim_player.play()` with a blend from outside.
- **Every animation keys every bone except Root, plus a Hips position track** (`PlaceholderAnims.make` `placeholder_anims.gd:373`). A hand-made Animation that omits tracks leaves stale bones behind.
- **Do not scale the rig Node3D** (or any ancestor). Hurtbox shapes and ragdoll `PhysicalBone3D`s are under it, and Godot/Jolt physics does not support scaled bodies or shapes. Size comes only from `height`.

## 3. `PlaceholderBody` (`game/actors/placeholder_body.gd`): the body layout

- **Axis convention** (`:8-16`): every bone's rest rotation is the identity, so pose Euler angles (degrees, YXZ) read the same on every bone. **+x** swings a hanging limb forward and tips the spine, neck and head **back**. Elbows flex with +x, knees with **-x**, and +x on a foot points the toes up. **+y** turns toward the character's left. **+z** swings a hanging limb toward +x (out for right limbs, across for left ones) and tips the spine to the left. `euler_basis(deg)` `:404`.
- `REF_HEIGHT := 1.8` `:19`. `BONE_NAMES` `:22` (Godot humanoid names, parents first); `BONE_PARENT` `:30`; `BONE_REST` `:45` (Hips at 0.975 m).
- **Rig points:** `POINT_BONE` `:72` and `POINT_OFFSET` `:91`. `EyePoint` (Head), `HeadTopPoint`, `ChestPoint` (Chest), `Left/RightFistPoint` (Hand), `Left/RightElbowPoint` (LowerArm), `Left/RightKneePoint` (LowerLeg), `Left/RightFootPoint` (**Toes**, the ball of the foot), `WeaponGrip` and `WeaponTip` (both RightHand; the tip is 0.7 m along the hand's -Z), `HoldPoint` (Chest, 0.55 m forward).
- **Meshes:** `class Part` `:147`, `parts(wrapped_hands)` `:198` (static, the same for every actor except the hands); `_gloved_hand` `:257` (enemies); `_wrapped_hand` `:271` (player). `enum Shape` `:136`. `enum Paint { BODY, PANTS, ACCENT, SKIN, DARK }` `:141`: one material per paint per rig (`humanoid_rig.gd:720`), and DARK is fixed (`_DARK_COLOR` `humanoid_rig.gd:106`). A test pins **4-6 flat colors per rig** (`tests/test_humanoid_rig.gd:532`).
- **FP hiding:** `FP_HIDDEN_BONES` `:121` and `FP_HIDDEN_PARTS` `:124` (player only).
- **Planting:** `SOLE_BONES`/`SOLE_OFFSETS` `:128-134`; `pose_globals(pose, hips_offset, scale)` `:411`; `lowest_sole(globals, scale)` `:427`.
- **Hurtboxes:** `class HurtSpec` `:169`; `hurt_specs()` `:389`. HEAD sphere r0.15 at Head + (0, 0.11, -0.01). TORSO capsule r0.2 from Spine to UpperChest. LEGS capsules r0.1 from UpperLeg to Foot. **The capsule length is fixed at the rest length** (`humanoid_rig.gd:807-811`); radii scale with height.
- **Ragdoll specs:** `RAGDOLL_MASS := 75.0` `:287` (it scales with the cube of the height); `enum RagdollJoint` `:290`; `class RagdollSpec` `:306` (`cone(axis, center, swing, twist)`, `hinge(lower, upper)`); `ragdoll_specs()` `:350`. **Joint limits are body data**: the upper-arm cone is `swing_deg = 100` about a down-forward-out center (`:370-372`), the knee hinge runs -130..0 and the elbow 0..150.

## 4. `PlaceholderAnims` (`game/actors/placeholder_anims.gd`): animations from poses

- `FRAME_S := 1/60` `:13`. `MAX_STEP_DEG := 90` `:17`: consecutive keys whose angles differ by more than 90° get in-between keys (`_subdivide` `:423`), so 360° spins and long swings follow the authored direction.
- **`ANIM_IDS`** `:20` (40 named ids); **`LOOPING`** `:33` (idle, the walks, block, hold, held, downed_*); **`CHARGE_ANIMS`** `:41` (`haymaker -> haymaker_charge`); **`FALL_JOIN_S`** `:45`; **`MOVE_IDS`** `:54` (26 move ids with a recipe); `warn_on_fallback` `:51`.
- `class PoseKey` `:64` (time, pose, resolved hips); `_k()` `:75`.
- **`build_library(scale, stance)`** `:88`: one `Animation` per `ANIM_IDS` entry. It is built per rig at `build()` and **costs about 8.4 ms** (section 17).
- **`build_named(id, scale, stance)`** `:98`: one big `match` (`:103-290`). Each case sets `length` and `keys` from `PlaceholderPoses` builders over the stance pose `g`. Unknown ids warn and hold the stance (`:288`).
- **`build_move(move, scale, mirrored, from_charge, stance)`** `:303`, the key layout of every move clip:

  ```
  t = 0                         guard            (windup when from_charge)
  t = windup_at * startup       windup           (only if startup > 0)
  t = startup                   strike           (first active frame)
  t = startup + active          strike_end or strike   (held: strike pose through the active frames)
  t = startup+active+follow_at*recovery   follow (optional)
  t = total                     finish or guard
  ```
  Length is exactly `total_frames() / 60`. `windup_at` and `follow_at` are clamped to 0.05..0.95 (`:322`, `:332`).
- `windup_time(move, poses)` `:329`; `_poses_for(move, stance)` `:335`, which calls `PlaceholderPoses.move_poses(move.get_anim(), stance)` or the fallback; `fallback_is_kick(move)` `:351` and `_fallback_poses` `:357`. **An id without a recipe still animates** as a generic jab or front kick, chosen by its first hitbox's point (with a push_warning).
- **`make(keys, length, loop, scale)`** `:373` sorts the keys (a later key at the same time wins), resolves hips, subdivides, then writes one rotation track per animated bone and one Hips position track (`(rest + hips) * scale`).
- **`resolve_hips(pose)`** `:413`: `HIPS_POS` if present, otherwise the offset that **puts the lowest of six sole points on y = 0**, plus `HIPS_NUDGE`.

**How a recipe is authored:** `_recipe` passes `g` (the guard, with the stance's arms in the relaxed stance) or `wg` (the weapon carry, for pipe ids). The recipe builds `windup` and `strike` as `merge(g, {...})`, so every bone it doesn't list keeps the guard's angle. It returns `_mp(g, windup, strike)` with optional `follow`, `strike_end`, `finish` and `windup_at`. Clinch moves ignore `g` and start from `hold()` (`_knee()` `:1849`). Air moves start from `_air_guard(base)` (legs tucked, `HIPS_POS` zero). Strike poses are tuned against the **"Strike reach" envelopes** (ARCHITECTURE "Moves" table, tested at `tests/test_humanoid_rig.gd:98-130`) and against **first-person framing** (`tests/fp_view.gd`).

Stale comment: `placeholder_poses.gd:1271` (and `test_humanoid_rig.gd:2070`, `test_combat_integration.gd:614`) still say `dummy_jab` has "18 frames of startup". It has had 24 since Game-boy's second playtest.

## 8. Adding a new move end to end (checklist)

1. **Data:** in `tools/gen_moves.gd:_build_moves()`, call `_add(&"<id>", "<Name>", Vector3i(startup, active, recovery), damage, TIER, REACTION, knockback, [_sphere(&"<Point>", r)] or [_arc(...)] or [])`, then set the flags on the returned MoveData (`magnetism = false`, `blocked_recoil_frames`, `unblockable`, `free_lunge`, `hitstun`, `max_targets`). Name tunable literals as constants at the top of the generator (`:60-81`). Run the generator (section 7.3) and commit the new `.tres`.
2. **Animation id:** append `&"<id>"` to `PlaceholderAnims.MOVE_IDS` (`placeholder_anims.gd:54`). The rig's `has_anim()` reads it.
3. **Recipe dispatch:** add `&"<id>": return _<id>(g)` (or `(wg)` for a weapon carry) to `PlaceholderPoses._recipe()` (`placeholder_poses.gd:1154`).
4. **Recipe:** write `static func _<id>(g: Dictionary[StringName, Vector3]) -> MovePoses`, the boxer-authored key poses. If it doesn't start from the stance, add the id to `_HOLD_MOVES` `:152`, `_WEAPON_MOVES` `:153` or `_AIR_MOVES` `:157` (they only matter for the relaxed stance, `_relax` `:1122`), or build the start pose inside the recipe as `_knee()` does.
5. **Tests:** add the id to every table in `tests/test_move_data.gd` (TABLE, SPHERE_POINTS or ARCS or NO_HITBOX, NO_MAGNETISM, EXPECTED_BRANCHES, plus the blocking rule) and either to `MOVES`/`_reach_rows()` in `test_humanoid_rig.gd` (player moves) or to a new enemy table (section 15.4). Add a dedicated telegraph test for enemy moves.
6. **Docs:** add a row to the ARCHITECTURE "Moves" table (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:145-170`) and a paragraph if it is special (as for `dummy_jab` at `:172`).
7. **Look at it:** in first person from the defender's side (Movie Maker capture). Tests alone don't show whether a telegraph reads.

Camera offsets are a **player** feature. Enemy moves leave them at 0, which `test_kick_camera_offsets` already expects for non-kicks.

## 11. Scaling a rig (Bruiser, Boss)

**How:** set `body_height` (and `body_radius`, which does **not** follow) on the actor. `_ensure_body()` sizes the capsule and the rig from it (`combat_actor.gd:1466-1477`). Every other `body_height` reader (get-up room checks `:1043`, `:1202`; flying-body impact `:1357`; dummy room checks) already uses it.

**What scales correctly (by `height / 1.8`):** bone rests and meshes (`humanoid_rig.gd:708`, `:743`, `:754`); rig points (`:339`, `:788`), so EyePoint, HoldPoint, WeaponGrip/Tip and the strike points all move out; hurtbox radii and capsule lengths (`:802-811`); the Hips position track in every animation (`placeholder_anims.gd:407`); ragdoll shapes, offsets and masses (`ragdoll.gd:196-205`, mass by the cube of the scale); flash and `hurt_point` math (`_scale` everywhere).

**Measured** (headless, idle boxer rig at 1.8 m against 2.2 m, scale 1.22):

| Point / strike | 1.8 m (y, z) | 2.2 m (y, z) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EyePoint | 1.656, -0.139 | 2.024, -0.170 |
| Head bone | 1.551 | 1.896 |
| HoldPoint | 1.195, -0.561 | 1.460, -0.686 |
| ChestPoint | 1.250 | 1.528 |
| `jab_2` RightFistPoint at strike | 1.568, -0.748 | **1.917**, -0.914 |
| `pipe_swing_3` WeaponTip at strike | 1.593, -0.874 | **1.946**, -1.069 |
| `front_kick` RightFootPoint at strike | 1.195, -0.776 | 1.460, -0.949 |
| `shove` LeftFistPoint at strike | 1.160, -0.679 | 1.417, -0.830 |

**What breaks or needs authoring:**
1. **Strike heights.** Poses are angles, so a scaled jab lands at 1.92 m. The player's HEAD hurtbox is a 0.15 m sphere centered at about 1.66 m (1.51-1.81). A r0.14 fist at 1.92 just grazes it at 2.2 m and misses at about 2.3 m. **Big-rig strikes must aim down** (spine and neck bent forward, arms lower) **or target the torso and legs** (the overhead smash, charge and ground pound do naturally). Keep the Bruiser near 2.0-2.1 m.
2. **Reach.** Strikes reach 1.22x further, so the AI's attack ranges and step-in distances must come from the rig scale (or from measured strike points), not from the 1.8 m numbers in ARCHITECTURE.
3. **Hitbox numbers don't scale.** `HitboxSpec.radius/offset` and `arc_radius/arc_height` are absolute (`hit_detector.gd:106-160`), so author them per archetype in the generator.
4. **Grab geometry.** `held` is authored for a 1.8 m victim under a 1.8 m holder's HoldPoint (`humanoid_rig.gd:46-49`, `placeholder_poses.gd:289-293`). A 2.2 m holder's HoldPoint is 0.27 m higher and 0.12 m further out, so its `hold` fists no longer rest on a 1.8 m victim's shoulders.
5. **First-person hold view and stomp** are tuned for 1.8 m victims. That is camera and player area; it matters only if the player can grab or stomp a big enemy.
6. **The node scale is off limits** (section 2.6).
7. Ragdoll joint limits are angles, so they are size-independent: fine.

## 13. Grabs: the held and thrown animations, and enemies grabbing the player

**Today (the player grabs an enemy):** the holder plays `grab` then `hold` (`player.gd:992`, `:537`). The victim calls `CombatActor.start_grabbed(by)` (`combat_actor.gd:467`): it plays `held` with `Tuning.grab_hold_blend_s`, becomes a ghost, and `_pin_to_holder()` `:1409` pins its origin on the floor below the holder's HoldPoint (`_pin_spot()` `:1401`), facing the holder, eased in over `grab_pin_ease_s`. Release: `release_from_grab(push, stagger)` `:487` plays `stagger_*` or `idle` with `grab_release_blend_s`, then `_leave_hold_under_hips()` `:1432` (+ `carry_fade`). A throw or a knee-KO launches out of the hold (`_launch` `:1002`; THROWN plays `launched`, and the ragdoll takes over at once).

**Pose geometry:** `hold()` `placeholder_poses.gd:268` bends the holder forward with both fists low and wide on the victim's shoulders. `held()` `:294` bends the victim over at the waist, arms hanging and head forced down, so the head sits about 0.35 m in front of the holder at y 1.24.

**M3 (Bruiser grab and headbutt, boss grab slam; the player is the victim):**
- The code path is generic (`start_grabbed` is on CombatActor), but **`held` on the player's rig would sink the first-person lens to about 1.24 m, into the holder's chest and arms.** `FirstPersonCamera` takes its position from EyePoint and its rotation from the look (`first_person_camera.gd:3-5`, `:262-266`). This needs new animation ids (the camera mapper owns the camera side):
  - `hold_collar` (holder upright, both fists at the victim's collar or chest, at HoldPoint distance) and `held_collar` (victim upright, leaning a little toward the holder, arms up at the holder's wrists; a struggle loop for the mash-out, where a faster loop on each mash press reads as "breaking free"). The victim's origin stays at `_pin_spot()`, so author `held_collar` for an origin 0.55 x holder-scale in front of the holder. The upright torsos then touch; both bodies are ghosts while held, so the capsules don't push.
  - **Headbutt** as a move recipe that starts and ends in `hold_collar` (like `_knee()` starts in `hold()`): the wind-up tips the neck and head back (+x on Neck and Head) and the spine back; the strike snaps them forward (-x), the hips driving in. Use **no hitbox** (applied by code to the held victim, like `throw` and `NO_HITBOX`) or a `&"Head"` bone sphere.
  - **Grab slam** (boss): a holder move starting in `hold_collar` (wind-up: arms up with the victim, at most about 140° on the upper arms, see G7; strike: a deep crouch slam). The victim needs a `held_lift` (the `held_collar` pose with `HIPS_POS` raised) during the wind-up, then gets a KNOCKDOWN by code, which plays `knockdown_*`. This is the most pose work of all the M3 attacks. A cheaper fallback: the victim stays in `held_collar` and is "slammed" by the reaction alone. That reads weaker.
- Any new named id goes into `ANIM_IDS` (and `LOOPING` if it loops), gets a `build_named` case, and is added to the ARCHITECTURE "Animation ids" list. The test copy in `test_humanoid_rig.gd:35` (`ANIM_IDS`) only checks the ids it lists, so a new id doesn't break it.

## 14. Weapon grip: a Pipe Thug holding the pipe

- `WeaponGrip` sits in the right fist (`placeholder_body.gd:84`, `:103`); `WeaponTip` is 0.7 m further along the hand's -Z (`:104`). Swing hitboxes use `WeaponTip`, so the pipe mesh never affects hits (`weapon.gd:3-5`).

- **Player-only weapon logic** lives in `player.gd` (`_weapon` `:99`, durability `:586`, throw `:574`, set switching `:347`). **`Weapon` has no `drop()`**: the only detach paths are `throw_from()` `:75` and `break_apart()` `:91`. The ground physics (`_set_ground_physics` `:95`) is private. The Pipe Thug's "drops the pipe on knockdown" needs a small public `drop(velocity)` in `weapon.gd`.
- **Carry pose:** pipe recipes start and end in `weapon_guard()` (`placeholder_poses.gd:244`, right hand up by the shoulder, pipe resting back). The thug's idle and walks are the plain boxer `guard()`, where the right fist at the chin points the pipe back over the shoulder. So every swing blends guard to carry over the `play_move` blend, and back over `locomotion_blend_s`. That is acceptable in graybox. A dedicated look needs either a `STANCE_WEAPON` (stance is baked at build, and after the drop the empty fist would stay in the carry pose) or a runtime `set_stance()` on the rig that rebuilds the `""` library and clears `"moves"`. That is a `humanoid_rig.gd` change, about 10 lines.
- Don't reuse `pipe_swing_*` MoveData for the thug. Their startups are 7-9 frames, they have magnetism, and the player's weapon set is built around them. Make `thug_pipe_1..3` with recipes that reuse `_pipe_swing_1..3(wg)` strikes and follow-throughs (section 15).

## 15. M3: the cheapest sound way to author the enemy attacks

### 15.1 The pattern

For every enemy attack:
1. **Its own move id** (separate F1 tuning, separate test rows, and the `get_anim() == id` contract kept).
2. **Its own recipe function that reuses the nearest player recipe** for the strike, `strike_end`, follow and finish (`var m := _pipe_swing_1(wg)`), and **replaces the wind-up** with a big, readable telegraph pose, as `_dummy_jab()` does. Only genuinely new motions (jump kick, sweep, smash, charge, pound, headbutt, slam) need new strike poses.
3. **`start_move(move, 1, &"root", true)`** from the enemy, so the whole startup is WINDUP (the rear warning and the interrupt rules hang off it). Startup is **at least 18 frames for grunts and at least 30 for the Bruiser and boss** (SPEC §7.4). The enemy applies `Tuning.windup_mult` as `TrainingDummy._jab_move()` does (`training_dummy.gd:321`). Hoist that helper into the enemy base.
4. **`magnetism = false`** (no tracking, so dodges work). Lunges are applied by the enemy at the right frame through `set_lunge(dir, distance, frames)` (`combat_actor.gd:458`) from the `_on_move_frame()` hook (`:573`), **not** with `free_lunge`. `_plan_lunge` (`:605`) spreads `free_lunge` evenly over the **startup**, which would slide the body forward through the whole telegraph.
5. Telegraph readability is judged from the **player's lens**: write a test like `test_dummy_jab_telegraphs_its_wind_up` (`test_humanoid_rig.gd:2069`) per move, then capture.

### 15.2 The one engine addition: a held wind-up

`build_move` keys guard, wind-up (`windup_at`), then strike at `startup`, so with a 24-36 frame startup the body creeps from the wind-up into the strike over 10-18 frames. That's slow and mushy, and it leaves no moment where the threat "holds". Add to `MovePoses` (`placeholder_poses.gd:30`) a `windup_hold_at: float = -1.0` (or a `windup_end` pose) that `build_move` (`placeholder_anims.gd:313-315`) keys as a second wind-up key at `windup_hold_at * startup`. Mirror it in `MovePoses.mirrored()` `:48`. For example, cock by 0.5, hold to 0.8, and drive into the strike over the last 20% (5-7 frames). Keep `windup_at` within **0.4-0.6**, because `test_play_move_length_and_strike_key_timing` wants a key there (`test_humanoid_rig.gd:996-1001`). A zero-change hold adds no `_subdivide` keys. A "tremble" (a small `add()` delta on the hold key) adds life cheaply. This is one owner's change (lead scaffold), about 10 lines and a test.

## 17. Measured costs

Headless, on this machine (`godot --headless`, a scratch script outside the repo):
- `HumanoidRig.build()`: **8.7-14.9 ms** per rig (7 rigs; the first is the slowest). `PlaceholderAnims.build_library(1.0, boxer)` alone: **8.4 ms**. `ragdoll_specs()`: 0.03 ms. Meshes and materials add GPU pipeline work on a windowed run (the flash shader is pre-warmed once, `humanoid_rig.gd:498`).
- First `play_move()` of a frame triple: about 0.44 ms (the clip build, cached per rig after that).
- `advance()`: about 0.03 ms per tick per rig (60 ticks: 1.9 ms).
- **Implication:** six enemies spawned in one tick cost about 60 ms of build, a visible hitch. Either spawn one per tick or **cache the named `AnimationLibrary` statically per (scale, stance)** in `HumanoidRig._build_animation()` (`:824`). The named clips are read-only after the build, so one library can be added to many AnimationPlayers. The `"moves"` library could be shared the same way, since its keys encode everything. The head-curve cache (`_head_curves` `:173`) stays per rig. Also each rig's **first ragdoll** builds about 20 `PhysicalBone3D`s and joints (`Ragdoll.setup` `ragdoll.gd:181`), a hitch on the first knockdown. It could be pre-built at spawn time.

## 18. Gotchas (numbered for cross-reference)

- **G1, stance and height are baked at `build()`.** Set them in `_configure_rig()`; `rig.height` is overwritten by `body_height` (`combat_actor.gd:1477`).
- **G2, `get_anim() == id` is a contract** (ARCHITECTURE `:141`, `test_move_data.gd:334`). `MoveData.anim` aliasing works in code, but the test fails. Prefer own-id recipes that reuse player recipes.
- **G3, enemy chains through `auto_next` or branches force FINISHER tiers** (`test_move_data.gd:466`) and borrow the player's chain semantics (ComboParser). Let the AI start each hit itself.
- **G4, one move, one strike pose, one hit per victim.** Multi-hit attacks are several moves.
- **G5, `free_lunge` slides the body during the startup** (`combat_actor.gd:605-607`), which is the whole telegraph for an enemy. Use `set_lunge()` from `_on_move_frame()`.
- **G6, hitboxes and arcs are absolute meters** and don't scale with `height`. Arcs aim by `get_forward()`, not by the animated hips.
- **G7, ragdoll joint limits.** A STAGGER starts a partial ragdoll from the on-screen pose, and the physics joints snap anything past `PlaceholderBody.ragdoll_specs()` (upper-arm cone swing 100° about a down-forward center, so a forward raise above about **140°** is out, and `_pipe_swing_3`'s 170° wind-up is out; knees -130..0, elbows 0..150). Keep enemy wind-ups inside, or widen the spec knowing what that means.
- **G8, bones a pose leaves out are at rest, not at the stance.** Always `merge(g, {...})`.
- **G9, auto-planting** puts the **lowest** sole point on the floor (`resolve_hips` `placeholder_anims.gd:413`). Sweeps, deep lunges and kicks with a low outstretched leg can float the support foot. Use `HIPS_POS` or `HIPS_NUDGE`.
- **G10, `windup_at` and `follow_at` are clamped to 0.05-0.95**, and the rig test wants a key at 40-60% of the startup.
- **G11, a non-looping animation holds its last pose.** The owner must return to locomotion.
- **G12, the play_move clip cache is keyed by anim id plus frames**, not by move id or recipe content. A recipe edited at runtime (it never is) wouldn't rebuild.
- **G13, `Reaction` and `HitTier` are stored as ints** in the `.tres` files: append enum values only.
- **G14, keep moves in the flat `game/combat/moves/` folder.** Tuning (`tuning.gd:258`) and the tests list only that directory.
- **G15, the generator overwrites every `.tres`.** Edit the values in `gen_moves.gd`; a parse error exits 0, so check for the "wrote" line.
- **G16, `held` on the player buries the first-person lens** (section 13).
- **G17, no rig `Node3D` scaling** (physics shapes under it).
- **G18, `thrown` is built but not played** (THROWN uses `launched`). Don't assume a victim of a slam shows `thrown`.
- **G19, the stale "18 frames" comments** about `dummy_jab` (`placeholder_poses.gd:1271`, `test_humanoid_rig.gd:2070`, `test_combat_integration.gd:614`). It is 24.

# WP4 build brief (lead's design decisions, draft)

Drafted by the working session on 2026-10-23 for the WP4 builder. The player-ragdoll default (decision 2) awaits Game-boy's answer.

WP4: partial ragdoll and finisher ragdoll (Milestone 2, phase 2). Read docs/milestones/M2-plan.md "WP4", SPEC §6 "Hit reactions" and the tier table's "Victim reaction" row, the HumanoidRig class doc (game/actors/humanoid_rig.gd, "Skeleton modifiers (limitation)"), ARCHITECTURE "Time model", "Physics and visual layers", "Humanoid rig" and "Playback notes".

Lead's design decisions (follow them; if one proves unworkable, stop and explain why in your result instead of improvising a different design):
1. Which reactions: STAGGER (heavy hits, the finisher shove, guard breaks, perfect-block staggers) blends a PARTIAL ragdoll over the animation: a PhysicalBoneSimulator3D simulating the upper body (spine up, head, arms; the hips and legs stay animated), influence in the spec's 0.3-0.6 range, with an impulse at the contact point along the hit, fading back to the animation over the stagger. Every off-feet reaction (LAUNCH, KNOCKDOWN, THROWN, and a KO) switches to a FULL ragdoll with an impulse at the contact point: these bodies already fly in M1; the spec's "finishers" row is read as "a body knocked off its feet". Flinches (light) stay animation-only (additive flinch).
2. Who: enemies and training dummies. The PLAYER keeps animation-only reactions by default (a tunable bool, default off): its first-person camera rides the pure-animation EyePoint and its visible arms and legs must never flop in its own view. (The lead is confirming this default with Game-boy.)
3. Simulation stays authoritative and deterministic. The CombatActor's CharacterBody3D flight, landing, flying-body impacts (a flying body still knocks standing enemies down), DOWNED/GETUP/KO timing, hurtboxes, rig points and get_point_transform() keep the pure-animation FK and the existing code paths. The ragdoll is a visual layer: it starts from the pose on screen and the bones' current velocities plus the impulse, and its hips are held to where the simulation says the body is (a soft, tunable spring or pin), so the ragdoll and the sim body never drift apart. On get-up, the actor's origin moves to the ragdoll hips' floor position (x/z only, collision-checked, so it never ends inside a wall or another body), and the get-up animation blends from the ragdoll pose (snapshot the post-ragdoll pose as the cross-fade source). KO: the body goes limp and settles instead of freezing.
4. Rig path, as the HumanoidRig class doc says: after skeleton.advance() has run the modifiers, the visual followers (meshes and attachments) read the post-modifier global poses; hurtboxes, rig points and get_point_transform() keep the pure-animation FK. With no ragdoll active the visuals must be bit-for-bit what they are now (every existing rig and first-person view test must still pass).
5. Time: the ragdoll respects each actor's sim time scale (TimeController.scale_for(actor)): frozen during that actor's hitstop (no falling through the freeze; the finisher's impulse lands when its hitstop ends or is held until then), slowed during the finisher slow-mo (0.3x) and the debug/cheat time scale. Engine.time_scale stays 1 (contract). For example: on a scale change from a to b, scale bone linear/angular velocities by b/a and set gravity_scale = b^2, and use a custom integrator to hold the bones still at scale 0. Prove it with a test.
6. Physics layers: the ragdoll bones collide with the world (and, if cheap and stable, with other ragdolls), never with hurtboxes, the player body, enemy bodies or hitbox queries. If a new layer is needed, add it to CombatTypes and project.godot and document it in ARCHITECTURE's layer table (the lead agrees to that contract change).
7. Stability: no explosions, no jitter, no limbs through the floor or walls, bodies come to rest (sleep) within a tunable time; joint limits that read as a human body (knees and elbows bend one way, the neck and spine limited). Damping and solver settings tuned for Jolt at 60 Hz.
8. Tunables (TuningData group "Ragdoll"): partial influence, partial fade time, partial impulse scale, full impulse scale, hips spring strength/damping, get-up blend time, rest/sleep time, the player toggle, and anything else that is feel. No feel numbers in scripts.
9. Performance: physical bones are built once per rig (in build() or lazily on the first ragdoll), kept inactive when not simulating; 6 enemies on screen must stay cheap.

Tests (tests/test_ragdoll.gd): hurtboxes and get_point_transform() unaffected by an active ragdoll (identical to the animation FK); a staggered dummy's upper body visibly deviates from the animation and returns to it; a launched dummy ragdolls, comes to rest, gets up and stands at its hips' spot with no overlap; a KO'd dummy settles limp and stays; the hitstop freeze and slow-mo scale the ragdoll; a flying ragdolled body still knocks a standing dummy down; the player never ragdolls with the default; no NaN or runaway velocity after many hits in a row; all existing tests still pass.

Captures: add ragdoll acts to the combo bot or a ragdoll bot (tests/bot/): a hook stagger, the uppercut launch, the haymaker, the spinning heel hitting two dummies, the push kick into a second dummy (flying body knockdown), the throw, a KO on a low-health dummy, a guard break stagger, each in first person and side view. Record them and judge them from the frames: bodies give way on staggers, fly and land believably on finishers, never explode, jitter or sink, and get up cleanly.

Owned files: game/actors/humanoid_rig.gd (the ragdoll and the post-modifier visual path), a new game/actors/ragdoll*.gd if you split it out, game/actors/combat_actor.gd (reaction hooks and the get-up relocation only), game/actors/placeholder_body.gd (physical bone shapes and joints from the placeholder layout, if needed), game/combat/combat_types.gd and project.godot (a ragdoll layer only, if needed), the "Ragdoll" group in game/autoload/tuning_data.gd, tests/test_ragdoll.gd, tests/bot/ ragdoll acts, the ARCHITECTURE.md "Humanoid rig" playback notes and layer table.

# M3 judgement calls: P3-bruiser (the armored heavy)

Calls the P3-bruiser builder made where the plan left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked (no grey-block feel questions): the default that changes least was taken. First-person sheets are from `tools/capture_bot.sh` (local, never committed); counts are headless runs of `tests/bot/crowd_bot.tscn` acts.

## Armor

1. **The haymaker's armor window counts from the moment the Bruiser is back on its feet, not from the hit.** While it is flying, down or getting up (and dead) the clock waits; `bruiser_armor_break_s` stays 2.0. *Alternative:* count from the hit. *Why not:* the haymaker's knockdown is the flight, `downed_frames` 75 and the get-up 30 (about 1.75 s), so a window from the hit would be gone before the Bruiser stood up, and "light hits flinch it then" would never be seen. The plan says the window is what the haymaker's payoff is.
2. **While the armor is broken every hit does its ordinary reaction to the Bruiser** (a jab flinches, a hook staggers, the uppercut launches). *Alternative:* only the light hits lose their absorption, with the heavy and finisher rules left on. *Why not:* with the flags left on, the uppercut would still stagger and never launch it; broken armor should mean the Bruiser is a body like any other for a moment.
3. **A second haymaker restarts the window, and a killing hit never starts it.** *Alternative:* a break that cannot be extended. *Why not:* the second haymaker has to do something visible, and the window is short.
4. **A perfect block staggers a Bruiser that was blocked (`_parry_staggers()` true, written out and tested).** The lead asked to log it. *Alternative:* the boss's rule (an ordinary recoil, no stagger). *Why not:* the plan gives that to the boss only; a parry is the player's answer to the smash, which a plain block only drains (60 of the guard's 100, no recoil, no punish window), and a staggered Bruiser can be grabbed.
5. **`bruiser_attack_cooldown_s` is 4.0 (S0 seeded 2.2).** Against a player who does nothing (god mode, "Bruiser cadence", 40 s, three seeds each): 7 attacks at 2.2, 5 at 3.5, 5 at 4.0, 4-5 at 5.0 (an attack is a smash or a grab; the headbutt's wind-up is the same attack). At 4.0 it attacks about every 6.4 s for 15, about 1.25 times the damage rate of a Punk at its locked 5.0 (two Punks: 10-11 wind-ups in 40 s, 6 per hit). Among five Punks against a passive player ("Bruiser among Punks, passive", three seeds each at 3.5, 5.0 and 8.0): the Bruiser attacks twice in about 20 s whatever the value, because the director's cap, gap and hit grace queue it; the player's time to KO was 19-23 s (R12 asks 12 or more) and the worst two seconds 18-27 (30 or less). *Alternatives:* 2.2 (the seed: it binds least, 7 attacks, the heaviest hitter attacking most often); 5.0 (the Punk's, the plateau's far edge, no more threat than a Punk); 8.0 like the Thug (a Bruiser attacks about once in a short fight). *Why 4.0:* below about 4 s it hardly binds (the cooldown counts through the recover and the walk back), it is mid-plateau between 3.5 and 5.0 so it sits on no edge, and it keeps the heavy a little more dangerous than a grunt, which is all the cooldown can do in a crowd.
6. **A light hit only ever braces the Bruiser while it stands with no move running.** The `brace` clip (0.36 s: the chest pushes out, the chin drops) plays then. In a wind-up or strike the move's own pose stays: a pose swap would break the telegraph, and no additive layer exists. *Alternative:* never play it (the flash, spark and clank are the whole read). *Why not:* the plan lists it ("maybe `brace`") and the pose file owes the clip. **A cost:** the base calls the locomotion every tick (`_stop()`, `_animate_legs()`), which would end the clip at once, so `bruiser.gd` overrides both private methods to leave the rig alone while `_brace_frames` counts down. *Alternatives:* set the base's `_taunt_frames` (posturing resets it on its own and ends the clip, and other states never count it down), or edit `enemy_actor.gd` (not mine). If the base renames those two, the brace silently stops showing; `test_bruiser.gd` (the brace test) is the alarm.
7. **`_tick_control()` is overridden (it counts the armor break and the brace, then calls `super`).** The base's note says archetypes override hooks and never the control tick; this is CombatActor's own per-tick hook and the override only decrements two counters first. *Alternative:* a timestamp of physics frames. *Why not:* it would not stop for hitstop and slow-mo, and the haymaker has the longest hitstop there is.

## The smash

8. **The wind-up is both fists over the head, held, with the shoulders shrugged.** The upper arms are limited to about 140 degrees by the ragdoll's cone (a stagger starts a partial ragdoll from the pose on screen), which puts a 2.05 m body's fists level with its head. The shoulder bone's own cone (36 degrees) was free: shrugging both 28 degrees brings the fists to 2.36 m against a 2.29 m head top, so they read as overhead. *Alternative:* the upper arms at 158 degrees (fists 2.2 m, past the cone by 7 degrees: the joint test fails); fists at head level (reads as a guard).
9. **The raise ends at 42% of the startup, not the dispatcher's 50%, and the arms yaw 32 degrees outward.** The shared first-person check wants the head not hidden by the rising arms at every frame at 2.5 m. With yaw 12-26 the forearms cross the face on the third frame (one cell of the 48-wide grid); at 32 and more they pass. *Alternative:* a different test threshold. *Why not:* it is the shared check's rule.
10. **The downswing passes through a wide arc, which the sheets show as a short T.** From the raised V to the fists together in front of the chest the arms swing out and down for the last three frames (the two poses are far apart and the clip blends between them). *Alternative:* an in-between key (the recipe has none) or a narrower raise. *Why not:* the first is an engine change, the second brings the face-hiding blip back; three frames of an overhead chop reads fine.
11. **The strike ends at the player's chest and head (fists 1.46 m high, 1.04 m ahead, 0.3 m either side), not at the height the angles would give.** The poses are angles, so on a 2.05 m body an arm out at the shoulder passes over a 1.8 m player's head: the spine and neck bend forward and the fists end low (`test_the_strike_aims_down...`). `reach_m` is 1.3 and the rig measures 1.32. *Alternative:* the fists at head height (a head hurtbox 1.66 m, 0.15 m): the head would be the only part it meets, and the fists would pass the lens.
12. **The smash is blockable.** The plan says so and the guard test shows 60 of 100 drained, the chip 2.25, no recoil. *Alternative:* unblockable like the grabs and the pound. *Why not:* a heavy that cannot be blocked leaves only a dodge; the parry (call 4) and the block's drain are the answers.
13. **Knockback (0, 3, 4) m/s.** A knockdown that carries the player a body length and lifts him a little. *Alternative:* the haymaker's (0, 4, 8). *Why not:* 15 damage should not throw further than the player's own finisher, and the first-person sheets show the get-up where it started.
14. **The shared check's "striking hand in view at the held key" does not apply at 1.0 and 1.2 m.** The raised fists leave the top of the lens at arm's length (as the Thug's pipe does, P2-thug call 6), so `test_enemy_moves_bruiser.gd` keeps the head-in-view lines there and the full shared check at the ring (2.5 m), and tests the fists overhead directly. *Alternative:* a lower raise that keeps the fists in view at 1.2 m. *Why not:* it would no longer read as overhead from the ring, which is where the player decides.

## The held Bruiser (the player grabs it)

15. **A held Bruiser stands 0.3 m further out (`bruiser_held_pin_extra_m`, in its own `_pin_spot()`).** At the ordinary pin its head was 0.08 m from the lens and covered 83% of the view (a held 1.8 m dummy: 0.24 m, 33%), a black wall of cap seen from above (first sheet of "Grab the staggered Bruiser"). At 0.3 m it is 0.35 m and 42%; at 0.5 m 0.45 m and 41% (no better, and further from the knee). The knee and the throw still land (tested). *Alternatives:* the upright `held_collar` pose for the Bruiser (the player's own held look: a different pose and the player's fists would grip its belly); a change in `player.gd` or the camera (not mine in phase 3; a request below).

## Armor that reads (A6.1)

16. **The grey spark is the block spark's art, tinted (`armor_spark_grey` 0.55), 0.24 m wide.** *Alternative:* a new PNG (`tools/gen_fx.gd`, `assets/`, ASSETS.md: not mine). *Why not:* a white star with a black outline times 0.55 reads grey, and a tint is one number F1 can edit. The white disc around it in the sheets is the existing contact flash, not mine.
17. **The clank is the block clack pitched down to 0.6 with the swing's whoosh; no thud, no grunt.** *Alternative:* a new sample. *Why not:* the plan says existing pools; the pitch is `armor_clank_pitch`.
18. **The combat log line is drawn grey (`HudStyle.MUTED`)** on top of the `ARMOR` flag that was already in `describe()` and `add_hit()`. *Alternative:* the flag alone. *Why not:* a grey line among the white and yellow ones is the cheapest way to see which jabs bounced.
19. **The armor tunables (`armor_spark_m`, `armor_spark_grey`, `armor_clank_pitch`, `armor_clank_db`) are in the Bruiser subgroup although the boss shares them.** The rule is one owner per subgroup; the boss builder reads them and adds none.
20. **The deep purple jacket (0.34, 0.10, 0.50) with steel-grey accents (0.74, 0.76, 0.82).** The Punk is teal, the Kicker lime, the Thug olive, the dummy orange, the player blue; no red. Steel on the cap, wraps and shoes reads as plating, which is what armor is. The boss takes charcoal and orange.

## The lens (A6.3) and the measurements

21. **Lens clearance of the smash and the headbutt landing on the player is 0.1 m** (twice the first-person near plane, 0.05 m). Measured from the real first-person camera over the whole move and the 30 ticks after: the smash 0.24 m at nearest and 52% of the view at most, the headbutt 0.14 m and 73% (the cap going into the face is the headbutt). *Alternative:* P2-grab's 0.25 m held-view figure. *Why not:* that is for a hold at arm's length; a strike lands, and what matters is that nothing is cut by the near plane or comes back through the lens after the hit (`_pull_strike` snaps the striker to its stance after the hitstop, as the plan says; no pose retraction is visible in the sheets).
22. **Six Bruisers at once miss R8's soft p95 by 3 points (53% against 50%).** The Punks' marginal misses were 51-57% (slice gate), and the Bruiser's body is 14% larger. *Alternative:* a wider posture ring for heavies. *Why not:* the ring is locked from the gate; it is a soft metric; flagged for the crowd reviewer.
23. **Spawn hitch measured windowed** (`-- --act="Six Bruisers, observed"`, six Bruisers one every 24 ticks, no Movie Maker, three runs, machine load 4 to 7 on 8 CPUs with other builders running): 26.3, 25.4 and 25.1 ms against R15's 33 ms, frame time p95 11.7-11.8 ms against 16.7. About what the Punks measured (26-31.5 ms); no shared perf fix is needed for the Bruiser.

## What the first-person sheets showed

- **"Bruiser shrugs off jabs"** at 1.2 m: the grey star on the purple chest for each jab, no flinch, the arms rising out of the top of the lens for the wind-up, four jabs absorbed in the middle of it, the smash landing on schedule (34 frames from the first to the last, hitstop on the Bruiser did not stretch it), the player flung down.
- **The smash from 3 m**: both fists straight up either side of the head, held about 12 frames, then down together in front of the belly. Reads as an overhead from afar.
- **"Hook staggers the Bruiser"**: WHAM and a red burst, the torso rocks back and the head dips out of the wind-up; the uppercut's KAPOW with speed lines staggers it with its arms flung wide instead of launching it; back to its guard.
- **"Haymaker breaks the armor"**: CRUNCH, it flies several metres and lies spread out for about a second, gets up; the jab then flinches it, and once the window is over the jab bounces off again.
- **"Grab the staggered Bruiser"**: the held view is a bent-over body, a cap and shoulders in the lens, the player's wrapped fists on its shoulders, a knee (SMAK) and the throw.
- **"Bruiser grab: mash free" / "headbutt"**: arms spread wide at the edges of the lens, pulled in, the MASH P / K! bar filling, the grey cap above the chest; free, its arms fly out and it staggers back; unmashed, the cap drops into the lens (THUD) and the player goes down.
- **"Bruiser winds up behind"** (corner crops): the top-left burst on the Bruiser's first wind-up frame, about 30% larger than the Punk's top-right one (`rear_warning_heavy_scale`), both inside the corner, gone on the hit.
- **"Bruiser 1v1"** with the real AI: it walks in with its fists up and its head and shades in view, squares up, steps in, the arms go up and fill the top of the view, THUD.

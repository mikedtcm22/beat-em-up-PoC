# M3 judgement calls: P1-instruments

Calls the P1-instruments builder made where the plan left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked: the default that changes least was taken.

## The oracle

1. **The widget is found by method, not by class.** The oracle scans the tree for any node with `corner_state(side)` (rescanning every 60 frames while none is found). *Alternative:* `is RearWarning`, or a `rear_warning` property of the HUD. *Why not:* P1-hud is not merged, and a class reference would not compile until it is; the contract (§2.9) names the method.
2. **The oracle keeps its own model of the warning, end rule included.** It does not only compare the corner at the wind-up's first frame: it re-derives, from Part B §B1.3, when each warning ends and checks the widget every frame (count per side, and `heavy`). *Alternative:* check the corner once, next frame (the readability-first plan's wording). *Why not:* R10 lists an end lag, and "the warning ends on the wrong moment" is the risk the plan names for the warning; a start-only check cannot see it.
3. **Latency and end lag are measured, and each WARN-CHECK line is `ok` only if the side, the latency (at most 1 frame) and the end lag (at most 1) all hold.** One line per wind-up carries all four R10 numbers (`lat=`, `end=`, `why=`). *Alternative:* a line per number. *Why not:* `check_m3_log.py` (P4) wants "the lines all say ok" and one line per wind-up.
4. **A false positive is an episode of burst the model cannot explain (a side lit with fewer live entries, net of recently ended ones still inside their allowed lag), counted once, however long it lasts.** A burst with nobody winding up prints its own `FAIL ... unexplained` line; one during a wind-up marks that wind-up wrong-side. *Alternative:* count every frame. *Why not:* one stuck burst would read as hundreds.
5. **A wind-up that lived one tick or less is not required to have lit.** The widget runs on the render frame, so a hit in the same frame as the start leaves it nothing to show. *Alternative:* demand it. *Why not:* a false FAIL on every instant interrupt.
6. **While the player's health is 0 (and `rear_warning_hide_in_ko`) the model expects nothing, and a wind-up that starts then expects none for its whole life.** The plan says "hides it while health is 0": whether a wind-up still live at the revive reappears is not stated, and the window (a wind-up of about 0.4 s against a revive of 1.5 s or more) never overlaps in practice.
7. **A missing widget fails every corner wind-up and passes the negative controls.** *Alternative:* skip the check (no widget, no verdict). *Why not:* a warning act that ran with the HUD's widget absent must not pass; `widget_found` says why.
8. **The oracle ticks at physics priority 1000 (after every actor).** An export (`late_priority`), a bot setting. The widget updating on the render frame then shows up as latency 1, which is within R10.

## The autopilot

9. **It always faces the nearest enemy, wind-up or not** (the lead's note: "faces the nearest posturing enemy"), and turns to a seen wind-up in preference. *Alternative:* turn only to wind-ups. *Why not:* it would never fight. *Consequence, documented:* a wind-up behind it is only a surprise (and the quick turn only fires) when something nearer stands in front, so the quick-turn test places a decoy.
10. **Skill defaults, all exports:** reaction 10 frames, turn 540 degrees a second, guard up when the strike is 6 frames away, dodge at 8, 45 frames between dodges, P P P P then K K K, mash every 3 frames. *Alternatives:* a slower turn (270 was the first value). *Why not:* at 270 a wind-up from 135 degrees could not be faced before the strike, and the bot looked helpless; the player's own quick turn is 900 degrees a second.
11. **It dodges away from the threats' mean side** (stick +x is right, threats on the left), with a seeded coin toss when they are dead ahead. The first version had the sign backwards; a test pins it.
12. **It walks up to the nearest enemy within 6 m and stops at 1.3 m, punching within 1.9 m** (all exports). The enemy AI of P1-ai closes to its own strike distance, so the exact numbers only matter for a posturing enemy on the ring; act authors may tune them (R13's "waves cleared in 90 s" needs it to reach enemies at all).
13. **It reads the block arc, the view angle, the behind angle and the dodge and quick-turn lengths from `Tuning`**, so a retune of those cannot desynchronise it. Only its own skill knobs are node exports.

## The probe

14. **The instruments have no `class_name`**, like `fp_view.gd` and the bots; tests `preload` them. *Alternative:* `class_name`. *Why not:* the export excludes `tests/*`, and nothing under it may be needed at runtime.
15. **The probe owns an oracle (created in `attach()`) and forwards `start_act` and `end_act`.** *Alternative:* P1-int attaches both. *Why not:* R10 belongs in `readability.json` next to the others, and two attach calls are two chances to forget one.
16. **Enemies are the group `ai_enemy`** (an export, `attacker_group`, switches to `enemy` for dummies); the autopilot's default is `enemy`. *Alternative:* `enemy` for both. *Why not:* the training room's dummies are not under the director, so counting them would break R1 whenever a bot spars.
17. **R1 also counts a holder (`is_holding(player)` or GRABBING) and reports the frames over the cap, with the cap read each frame**, so a cap changed in F1 mid-act is judged correctly; the hard verdict is "no frame over the cap".
18. **R2 compares consecutive wind-up starts only when they are different enemies, and the pass line is `ceil(director_min_windup_gap_s x 60 - 0.001)` frames** (A2.10 renamed Part B's `token_grant_gap_frames`). Under slow-mo the gap in physics frames only grows, so it cannot cause a false fail.
19. **R3's "attack" is the run of moves between one `attack_started` with chain step 1 and the next**, so a chain link (no wind-up of its own, A2.14) belongs to the telegraph before it. A wind-up that ends interrupted ends the attack. Exempt: a body impact, and any hit while its attacker holds the player (`grabbed` to `released` or `grab_escaped`). A hit by an attacker with no record at all is unwarned. The floor is `windup_min_frames_heavy` for an attacker with `heavy`, else `_grunt`. *Alternative:* the move's own startup. *Why not:* it is the telegraph actually shown that matters, and a startup can be shortened by a hit-stop bug the probe would then miss.
20. **R4 is sampled every 3 frames, not 10** (Part B says every 10th for the lens metrics). An 18-frame wind-up gets one sample at 10 and "over half" is meaningless on one; at 3 it gets six. A wind-up is in view when most of its samples had the chest inside the lens, and occluded when most of its samples had another enemy's mesh nearer than the chest by more than 5 cm. Only other `ai_enemy`s' meshes occlude (walls and crates are the room's). R8 stays at every 10 frames.
21. **R5 and R6 count only `AiState.POSTURE` enemies**; walking-in (APPROACH) ones would pull the ring's 10th percentile under 2.2 m by design. R5's denominator is frames with at least one live enemy.
22. **R7's share is over frames with two or more standing enemies; the raw frame count is kept too.** "Standing" is everything but DOWNED, LAUNCHED, THROWN, GRABBED and KO, so a get-up pop counts (the plan allows 0.5% of them).
23. **R9 is built from the enemies' own `wants_token()` the frame before a token appears**, not from the director's `debug_waiting()`. *Alternative:* the director read API. *Why not:* the skeleton and the stub answer it differently from the real one, and a probe that trusts the director's list cannot catch the director being wrong.
24. **R11 uses the hit's own damage** (not blocked; reaction KNOCKDOWN, LAUNCH or THROWN; attacker not in group `boss`), and also records the attack's chain total (`max_knockdown_attack_damage_non_boss`, informational) since the data rule of §2.10 is about the chain.
25. **R12 applies only to an act tagged `{passive = true}`**, and KO time runs from the act's start. *Alternative:* detect that the player never acted. *Why not:* a tag is explicit and costs one argument.
26. **R13's stuck test is displacement, not distance to the slot:** an ENTER or APPROACH enemy (with a slot more than 0.75 m away) that moved under 0.3 m in 3 s. The director reslots every 1.5 s, which would reset a progress-toward-the-slot measure. "Waves cleared" is the live count falling from above 0 to 0, within the act's first 90 s (the spawner has no event for it in the contract); R13's wave row only applies to an act of 90 s or more.
27. **R14 prefers a counter on the audio director and falls back to polling it.** The preferred read is `AudioDirector.windup_voices_stolen`; without it, the probe snapshots `_windup_of` and `_voices[i].playing` each frame and counts a voice that was playing a live wind-up's cue and is now reassigned while that attacker is still in WINDUP (an interrupted wind-up cuts its own voices, and one that finished playing is a free voice, so neither counts). With neither it reports null and the verdict says not measurable. See "requests".
28. **R15's frame times have the probe's own tick time taken out**, and the verdict is n/a headless or when recording (Movie Maker's frame time is the encoder's). A spawn is a new `ai_enemy` instance id first seen after the act began. The lens sample costs about 7 ms with six enemies and 40 meshes in view, which is why its own time is subtracted; `lens_every = 0` turns it off for a clean perf run.
29. **Verdict rows are `ok`, `FAIL` (hard), `miss` (soft) or `n/a`** (nothing measured, or the row does not apply to this act). A row with nothing to measure is never a pass and never a failure, so a short act does not fail R13's waves. `Probe.hard_ok()` is false only on a `FAIL`; the hard rows asserted in `test_slice_crowd.gd` should also check the row is not `n/a` where the act is supposed to produce it.
30. **`readability.json` goes to the folder of `--write-movie`**, else `--probe-out=<dir>`, else `user://`. *Alternative:* an argument only. *Why not:* `capture_bot.sh` already passes the movie path, so every recording lands its report beside it with no change to the script. `write_on_exit` exists because bots end with `get_tree().quit()`.

## The tests

31. **The edge rules of R3, R11 and R12 are tested with events emitted by hand, not with the stub's real attack.** Once P1-ai lands, `EnemyActor.windup_move()` floors every wind-up at 18 frames, so a test that built a 5-frame "unwarned" wind-up on a stub would start to fail the day it merged. The one real-attack R3 test uses the sparring jab (24 frames), which holds either way.
32. **The oracle has its own widget double with controllable faults** (mirrored, late, lingering, always lit, ignores `heavy`), instead of waiting for `RearWarning`. It reuses `ViewGeometry` for the rule; the rule itself is pinned by `test_view_geometry.gd`'s hand table, and the oracle test places attackers by hand-computed bearings.
33. **A scratch bot (autopilot against stub enemies in the training room, with the probe and oracle attached) was recorded to look at the autopilot in first person and was not committed:** the plan lists no bot among this package's files, and P1-int owns the crowd bot's acts.

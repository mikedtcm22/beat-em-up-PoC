# M3 judgement calls: P1-director

Calls the P1-director builder made where the plan left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked (no grey-block feel questions): the default that changes least was taken.

1. **Wind-ups are paced at the wind-up, not at the grant.** `director_min_windup_gap_s` (0.35 s, A2.10) is enforced in `may_start_windup()`; grants are limited only by the cap. *Alternative:* also pace the grants by the same gap, as Part C's `director_min_attack_gap_s` did. *Why not:* A2.10 says the wind-up gap replaces it, and R2 is about wind-up starts, which a grant gap can't guarantee (two enemies granted a second apart can arrive together). The cost: a volley of three grants becomes three wind-ups 0.35 s apart, with the waiting ones squared up at their reach, and the gate may want a grant gap too.

2. **`may_start_windup()` says no to an enemy that is not a token holder** (and to anyone while a menu is open, the AI is frozen or the player is closed). The S0 skeleton answered true to all. *Alternative:* true for non-holders, so a forced attack always goes. *Why not:* "no token, no go" makes the director the one gate, and `force_attack()` doesn't call it anyway (it skips the token); the director still records a forced wind-up through `windup_started`, so a holder's next start is spaced from it.

3. **The gap compares with 1 ms of slack** (`_CLOCK_SLACK_S`). Twenty-one additions of 1/60 may come out a hair under 0.35, which would make the gap 22 ticks some runs and 21 others. With the slack it is exactly 21 ticks at 60 Hz, never fewer. *Alternative:* an integer tick counter. *Why not:* the director's clock is sim time (slow-mo stretches the gap with it, tested), which a tick count can't be.

4. **Revocation has two strengths.** A *hard* closed player (missing, `health <= 0`, KO, downed, flying, GRABBED, in the revive grace) takes back every token whose attack hasn't started, as Part C says. A player who is only **invulnerable** (a dodge, a get-up) withholds new tokens but takes none back. *Alternative:* Part C's literal "closed" including `is_invulnerable()`. *Why not:* a dodge would strip every ENGAGEing enemy of its token each time, re-queueing them all and losing their place in line; a wind-up begun during the last frames of an i-frame window lands after it anyway (the minimum wind-up is 18 frames).

5. **Menus and `ai_frozen` hold the fight the same way.** While `GameInput.menu_open` (A2.2) **or `Tuning.ai_frozen`** no token is granted and unstarted ones are taken back. `ai_frozen` is not in the director's brief, but "stands still and asks for nothing" is the same hold and a frozen enemy with a stale token would otherwise sit squared up for 4 s. *Alternative:* leave `ai_frozen` to the enemies. *Why not:* the F4 "Freeze AI" checkbox and the scripted warning acts toggle it at will; a token surviving the toggle is a surprise.

6. **An attack that has started always keeps its token, however the player's state changes.** "Started" is WINDUP, ATTACK or GRABBING, *any* running move (`current_move != null`, so a Kicker's jump kick under AIRBORNE counts), or the player in the enemy's grip (`player.holder == enemy`: an enemy that grabs the player closes the player to everyone else, but not to the holder). And the cap counts those attackers whether or not they hold a token (a bot's forced attack uses up a place). *Alternative:* count tokens only. *Why not:* R1 is "enemies in WINDUP or ATTACK at once", and a token taken from a wind-up would free a place for a second attacker while the first is still swinging.

7. **The director prunes holders that are reacting** (an actor state other than FREE with no move running) **as well as dead, gone and freed ones.** P1-ai releases its token on a hit itself ("tokens released on every exit path" is lens L4); this is the backstop, so a forgotten release costs one tick instead of a stuck cap. *Alternative:* trust the enemies and only prune the dead. *Why not:* one missed exit path (a room switch, an F4 removal, a get-up) freezes a place in the cap for the full 4 s timeout.

8. **The token timeout only applies to an attack that has not started.** After `director_token_timeout_s` a holder that is still just standing there loses its token. A holder in its wind-up or strike never does. *Alternative:* revoke after the timeout whatever it is doing (Part C's wording). *Why not:* same as 6.

9. **Slots are given to every live enemy, ENTER, APPROACH, POSTURE, RECOVER, REACT and token holders included, and the slots are computed before the grants in a tick.** A holder keeps its slot (so `slot_for()` answers for an enemy that has just been granted a token and RECOVER can walk back to it at once, and an enemy granted on its very first tick still has one). *Alternative (my first version, and Part C's "the posturing set"):* only tokenless enemies are spread. *Why not:* the circle then re-spread (n versus n-1) with every token that came or went: the other enemies shuffled by 6-12 degrees at every attack. Found with a throwaway walk-in simulation, fixed.

10. **Seats: an enemy that has a slot sits at its claimed angle, not at its current position.** The arrangement is the L1-optimal rotation (try each enemy as the one that sits exactly on its slot, under each cyclic shift: exact, n^3 with n <= 12, and a settled crowd is a fixed point), then a greedy closest-pair match, as Part C allows. The "current angle" of an enemy that was seated last time is that seat (plus its own jitter), so an enemy walking through the player's near side, or knocked across the room, does not flip the assignment (my first version used the positions: six Punks walking in from one alley made three slot jumps of 7 m while they crossed). A new enemy sits where it stands. *Alternatives:* the circular mean of the angles (ill-defined for a clump of six enemies at the same doorway, and its noise would turn the whole arrangement); the Hungarian method (nothing here needs the global optimum); positions only, as Part C words it (the churn above). The cost: an enemy pushed far from its seat is not re-seated by the director; its own stall detection calls `report_stalled()`. *Final review:* a new enemy now weighs only `director_newcomer_weight` (0.1) against a seated one in the arrangement, and the seated ones choose their slots first in the match, so a spawn (the boss's call-in above all) never turns the ring or hands a seated enemy the seat across the player; with two enemies the two mirrored arrangements cost the same and the first one tried won, which gave the boss the far seat (fix-final-enemy-ai-ring-and-tokens.md, call 8).

11. **A Kicker also turns the whole arrangement** until a slot is exactly behind the look. Part C says only that Kickers take the slots nearest behind first; with an even spread by the crowd's own rotation, one alone could end up at the player's side. *Alternative:* no rotation. *Why not:* the Kicker's job is to be in the blind arc, and for n = 1 or 2 "nearest behind" could be 90 degrees off. The cost: the arrangement swings round as the player turns (at the next reassignment, so every `director_reslot_s`), pulling the others with it by up to half a step. *Superseded at the final review:* measured in a fought mixed six the cost was far more than half a step: with this turn and the Kickers taking the rearmost seats first (Part C), the other five were re-seated at every reslot, often across the player, and a mixed crowd postured 0.4-0.8 of 5.8 against the six Punks' 1.4-1.8, with APPROACH covering over half the lens in 12 to 34 of 388 samples (0 to 5 for six Punks). Both are gone: the enemies that are not Kickers keep their stable minimum-movement ring, and each Kicker has a seat of its own as near directly behind the look as it can be, at least `director_flank_gap_deg` from every other seat (fix-final-enemy-ai-ring-and-tokens.md, call 9). Swapping seats with the occupant alone, or only dropping the turn, was measured and does not pass.

12. **A slot that cannot be snapped falls back to the raw ring point**, not to the navmesh's nearest point. If every angle fails (no navmesh polygon within the tolerance anywhere on the circle), the closest point could be across the room (or the origin of an empty map). The raw point is what P1-ai steers at with no navmesh, so its straight-line fallback handles it. Also no query is made unless the map is synced **and** has a region (a synced empty map, as the training room has, answers `(0,0,0)`).

13. **Hash-based jitter and radius.** Each enemy's angular jitter and ring radius come from a hash of its name (`String.hash()`), so they are stable across runs and ticks (a settled crowd does not shuffle) and cost no RNG draws (the roll stays the one random stream, so a test's sequence doesn't depend on how many enemies spawned). *Alternative:* the seeded RNG, drawn per enemy at spawn. *Why not:* the order of draws would then depend on spawn order.

14. **Only the director's own script runs the loop** (`get_script() == EnemyDirector`). `tests/fixtures/stub_director.gd` (S0's) inherits `_physics_process`, and S0's fixture doc says a test's lists are whatever it sets; with the real loop running the fixture would grant tokens and move slots under P1-hud's and P1-instruments' tests. *Alternative:* ask S0 to add `set_physics_process(false)` to the fixture. *Why not:* it's S0's file, and doing it in the director needs no change anywhere else. (If a real subclass is ever wanted, it can call the loop itself.)

15. **`report_stalled(enemy)` is a public addition to the API** (not in 2.6). Part C's P1-ai says its stall detection "asks the director for a new slot" and 2.6 has no call for it. It nudges that enemy's angle by `director_slot_retry_deg` (alternating sides, growing) and triggers a reassignment. *Alternative:* P1-ai reads `slot_for()` again, expecting a new slot. *Why not:* `slot_for()` is a getter. P1-int wires the call (duck-typed, `has_method`, until both are merged).

16. **No new tunables.** Every number is S0's seed, kept at its default (nothing in a headless run argues for a change: the gate retunes the gap and the cap from first-person sheets). The two slacks (`_CLOCK_SLACK_S`, `_COST_SLACK_DEG`) and the hash resolution are numeric tolerances, not feel numbers.

17. **No anti-flinch-lock rule beyond the gap and the cap.** Part C's goal says "no flinch-lock" and the pacing is the gap. A harder rule (no wind-up starts while the player is in hitstun, or a short grace after he is hit) would be one `if` in `may_start_windup()`. *Alternative:* add it now. *Why not:* it changes feel the plan didn't ask for, and R12's "passive time to KO" needs a real crowd to tell. If the slice gate's god-mode sheets show a chain of hits with no gap for the player to act, this is the first thing to try (open risk in the final message).

# M3 judgement calls: fix-final-lens-floor-and-warning-size

Calls the fixer of package "lens-floor-and-warning-size" (final review findings final-crowd-01 and final-warn-01) made where the brief left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked: the default that changes least was taken. Both fixes are presentation only; neither is a spec change (final-warn-01 restores SPEC section 8's "larger for the Bruiser and boss"; final-crowd-01 is a bug, and the belly-slab view question stays Game-boy's).

## final-crowd-01: the lens inside the street in a face-down knockdown

1. **A hard `max` on the lens y, not a soft knee.** `FirstPersonCamera._view_with()` sets `lens.y = max(lens.y, floor + cam_floor_clearance_m)`. A max against a floor that is a constant over the knockdown is 1-Lipschitz: the lens never moves more in a tick than the free lens does, so the clamp cannot add a pop of its own. The test asserts exactly that (the clamped lens's tick-to-tick step never exceeds the free lens's by more than a millimeter, on every case). It kinks the lens's speed where it meets the floor, which reads as the head meeting the street; at 60 Hz that is a one-frame change in a motion of 3 to 4 m/s. *Alternative:* a smooth maximum with a blend width (a second tunable). *Why not:* it lifts the lens above the floor's clearance before the edge, and the face-up fall, whose free lens is already 0.116 m (1.4 cm under the 0.13 clearance), would be lifted by more than the 2 cm the brief allows once the knee reaches it; it also costs a second tunable for a smoothness the max already has.
2. **One tunable, `cam_floor_clearance_m`, 0.13 m, range 0 to 0.5, in the "Camera" group.** The near plane (0.05 m, set in `FirstPersonCamera._init()`) plus 0.08 m of margin, which also covers the screen shake's 4 cm (`shake_max_offset_m`) so the real lens, shake included, stays above the near plane (the test measures `global_position.y`, not only the view). *Alternative:* make it near plane plus a margin constant. *Why not:* it is a feel number (it decides how low the face-down view sits); F1 can move it. `tuning/default.json` is not regenerated here: the lead does it (the default-preset test in `test_tuning_panel.gd` fails until then, by design of the brief).
3. **Where the floor comes from: the player's own interpolated height while it is on the floor; a world-layer ray straight down from `EyePoint` in the air; nothing otherwise.** On the floor (every state of a knockdown after the landing) the capsule stands on the floor, which is the floor whatever the pose does to the eye, and a plain read costs nothing. In the air (a launch or throw) the player's origin is above the floor, so the floor is found by a ray from `EyePoint`, never from the lens (a lens already in the box sees no surface: the ray starts inside it). With no surface within the ray's 4 m, or a player outside the tree (a rig test's loose player), the answer is `-INF` and the lens is as it was. The 4 m reach is not a feel number: the clamp only matters within 0.13 m of the floor. *Alternative:* always the ray. *Why not:* a ray every frame for the whole game to find the floor under a standing player, and a floor that would jump where the eye passes over a crate's lip.
4. **The clamp is in the static `_view_with()`, so `view_from_eye()` includes it.** The brief allowed `_follow()`. The rig tests judge the "real" view through `view_from_eye()` (test_ragdoll, test_combat_integration, test_grab_view, test_launch_from_hold); putting the clamp where they cannot see it would have left them judging a lens inside the floor. The cost is a physics query from a static function: guarded (`is_inside_tree()`, a null `get_world_3d()`), and only in the air. All of the rig tests pass unchanged (see the suite result below).
5. **The test measures the free lens with a second camera, not with arithmetic.** `test_a_knockdown_from_the_side_or_behind_keeps_the_lens_above_the_floor` runs two cameras on the same player, one updated with the clearance set to -10 (no floor), so both smooth the eye alike and differ only by the floor; the lift and the no-pop check compare them. A comparison against `view_from_eye()` itself would include the camera's own smoothing lag (0.15 to 0.3 m in a fall). The real knockdown is a Kicker's sweep (90 and 180 degrees) and a Bruiser's smash (135 and minus 135) on the real player in the real test room, and from the front for the face-up case (1.3 and 1.4 cm lift, under the brief's 2 cm). Without the clamp the four face-down cases fail at -0.045 m and -0.046 m over the floor (the review's -0.044 and -0.045); the face-down cases also assert that the free lens was under 0 m, so the scenario cannot pass by not reproducing the bug.
6. **First-person stills confirm it** (`captures/fix/kd`, not committed): a Kicker's sweep from behind, a Bruiser's smash from behind-left and a Kicker's sweep from the front, recorded with the crowd bot's first-person camera. Face-down, the street fills the lower half of the view with its grid, the road paint and the curb, the horizon at about the middle, where the review's sheets show the background colour and the undersides of curbs; the lens's minimum height over the three is 0.131 m (shake included) and 0 frames fall below the floor top; the descent, the hold and the get-up show no pop. Face-up the view is as before with the lens 1.4 cm higher.
7. **Not done: the floor reference is not smoothed.** The floor under a grounded player is the capsule's own height, which does not jump; in the air the ray can see a box's top where the eye passes over its lip. *Alternative:* damp the reference like the eye. *Why not:* it would let the lens sit under a rising floor for a few frames, the bug itself, and the case (the eye over a curb's lip a hand's width from the floor in the air) is not reachable in the test room's launches.

## final-warn-01: the heavy burst no larger than the normal one

8. **The brief's rule as written, applied to steady and pop alike.** The normal burst is `min(base x size, edge / heavy_scale)`, the heavy one that times `heavy_scale`; `burst_rect()` multiplies by the pulse and clips to the corner as it did, so a heavy pop is never smaller than a normal pop (`heavy_scale >= 1`, both sizes monotone in it). The steady defaults are unchanged (160 and 208 px at 1080p) and so is the default pop (heavy clipped to 216, normal 200). *Alternative:* cap the pop's pre-heavy size too (`edge / heavy_scale` over steady x pulse). *Why not:* it would take the normal pop away at the defaults (160 x 1.25 = 200 is over 166), which is a default change the brief rules out. One consequence stays: where the heavy scale is below the pulse's growth (under 1.25 at the default pulse) and the normal burst is at its cap, both pops clip to the corner and are equal; the test asserts the strict ordering at the default scale and at 2.0, and `>=` at the others.
9. **Logged in P1-hud.md (items 20 and 21):** the new rule, and that the slider's upper range saturates (the normal burst stops growing at about 1.04, the plan's binding 20% x 20% corner makes it inherent: M3-plan.md line 166), with the alternative of narrowing the `@export_range` rejected (its ceiling depends on `rear_warning_base_px` and `rear_warning_heavy_scale`). The range is not narrowed; the doc comment of `rear_warning_size` and `rear_warning_heavy_scale` in `tuning_data.gd` says so.
10. **Tests on the real canvas size.** `test_ui.gd` sweeps `burst_rect()` on 1920 x 1080 and `test_rear_warning.gd` lights a normal burst behind-left and a heavy one behind-right (real wind-ups through the HUD) and sweeps the slider's whole range (read from the property's hint: 0.25 to 3.0 in 0.05 steps) at the pop and steady: the heavy one larger, both inside the reserved corner; and the default sizes (160, 208, pop 200 and 216). The headless viewport is square and never reaches the 216 px clamp, which is why the old tests missed it. The existing "inside the reserved corner at every size" test needed a tolerance of 0.001 px: a normal burst now ends on a fractional size (the corner over 1.3), and a rect's corners are 32-bit floats, so a burst flush with the corner came out a millionth of a pixel past it. The recording at size 1.5 (`captures/fix/warn_size15`) shows the heavy burst clearly larger than the normal one, where the review's sheet showed them equal.

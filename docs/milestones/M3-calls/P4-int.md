# M3 judgement calls: P4-int (whole-fight integration)

Calls the P4-int builder made where the plan left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked (no grey-block feel questions): the default that changes least was taken. Measurements are headless or Movie Maker runs of the crowd bot's acts (`tests/bot/crowd_bot.tscn`, `-- --act=<title>`); R15 is windowed, not recording, with the machine at a load average of 3 to 4 (other builders, nothing of mine in parallel). The bot prints its event lines twice (live, then the whole log again), so counts are of the de-duplicated lines.

## The done-when test (`tests/test_m3_done_when.gd`)

1. **Each archetype winds up at its own strike range, not at 1.5 m.** Punk 1.0 m, Kicker 2.6 m (its jump kick), Bruiser 1.25 m, Thug 1.0 m, boss 2.55 m (its pound). Every wind-up therefore ends in a hit, and the corner is checked "from the wind-up's first frame to the contact", as the done-when says. *Alternative:* the slice act's 1.5 m for everyone. *Why not:* a Punk's jab reaches 1.05 m, so at 1.5 m it whiffs and the warning ends on the whiff, which proves a different clause. The Thug is at 1.0 m (the test and the act), not at its engage distance of 1.18 m: its forehand swings across the body and at 1.15 m it whiffed on the left and behind spots (the player's hurtboxes are not symmetric: arms in the guard) and landed on the right; the first recording of the act had those two warnings end on a whiff, which is another clause, so it was re-recorded at 1.0 m.
2. **All five archetypes are in the positive cases, three in the controls.** The lead named the Punk, Kicker and Bruiser; the Thug and the boss cost a few hundred frames and have the same code path, so they are in too (a hand-written table of 5 x 3 corners). The negative controls (50 degrees and -50 degrees in view at strike range, 135 and 180 degrees out of range at the reach plus the 1 m margin plus half a metre) run for the three named archetypes only (12 wind-ups): 25 more would have added about 10 s to a suite that already takes two minutes.
3. **The expected corners and distances are written in the test, and the bearing is checked twice.** The placement is worked out from the look's yaw by hand (`forward = (-sin, -cos)`), then the player's own `look_bearing_deg()` must agree within 0.6 degrees, and the corner expected for 135, -135 and 180 degrees is a literal. Nothing is derived from `ViewGeometry.warning_corner()`.
4. **The widget is read by a sampler that runs after everything else in the frame** (physics priority 1000), so a frame's row is the corners as the HUD shows them at its end, after its own `windup_started` and `hit_landed` handlers. "Lit from the first frame" is the row of the frame `windup_started` fired in; "to the contact" is every row before the frame `hit_landed` fired in; the contact's own row is dark; and nothing relights up to 30 frames later (a chain's links have no wind-up of their own, A2.14). *Alternative:* assert inside the event handlers. *Why not:* the HUD's handlers run first or last depending on connection order, and the test would then depend on it.
5. **The soak's player takes real damage, with his health refilled every frame, not god mode.** God mode sets `hit.damage` to 0 before the hit event fires, so R11 ("no non-boss knockdown over 15") is "max 0" in every god-mode act and proves nothing (the slice test and my five acts are all god mode). The refilled player never dies (a KO would hold the director) and the soak sees real numbers: 3 knockdowns, worst 15 (the Bruiser's smash or headbutt, the 15% rule's limit), and the test asserts there was at least one knockdown to judge. *Alternative:* god mode, as the slice test. *Why not:* R11 measured nothing.
6. **The soak is 1500 frames of the six archetypes' mix at the crowd bot's surround points, waves off, and a second test puts the waves on with the autopilot** (god mode) and asserts `alive_count()` is at most `wave_max_alive` on every frame, at least two waves start, and nobody is stuck. "Alive never over 6 from waves" needs the waves to be on, which the mixed soak is not. The waves test is a second test, not part of the soak, because the six placed by hand would already be six alive.
7. **The Debug toggles are put back by hand.** `Tuning.reset_to_default()` restores the preset, and `ai_frozen` and `god_mode` are not in it: the warning tests' frozen AI leaked into the next test (no wind-ups at all, "R2 was not measured"). The file sets both to false in `before_each` and `after_each`. (Found here; `test_slice_crowd.gd` never sets them, so it is not affected.)

## The log checker (`tools/check_m3_log.py`)

8. **It reads the bot's log, not the probe's JSON.** It checks the WARN-CHECK lines (all `ok`), that each wind-up the rule demands a corner for has `lat` 0 or 1 *and* the HUD's own `W+` line for that side (both for "both") at the wind-up's frame or the next, that a wind-up the rule leaves dark has no `W+`, that token holders never exceed the cap, that no READABILITY row says `FAIL (hard)` and that the log has no `SCRIPT ERROR` or `ERROR:` line. The `W+` cross-check is a second path to the same fact: the oracle's `lat` is the oracle's own reading of the widget, the `W+` line is the widget's own signal. *Alternative:* trust `lat`. *Why not:* one source can be wrong in the same way as the thing it checks.
9. **Four things the first runs taught it.** (a) The bot prints its event lines twice: a repeated line is read once. (b) A `WARN-CHECK` for a wind-up that began after its act's READABILITY block is skipped (the bot runs on 45 frames before the next act, the probe is off, and the oracle prints `ok`, `lat=-`). (c) A corner the rule demands that never lit is a failure unless the wind-up was over within a frame (its `WU-` line), the oracle's own exemption. (d) A hand-over of a token in one physics frame prints `TOK+` before `TOK-` (tree order), which read as a fourth holder: the holders are counted when the frame's last line is read. The cap defaults to `attacker_cap` of `tuning/default.json`. *Alternative:* count line by line. *Why not:* it failed a good run twice (f1216 and f1334 of the perf act). `tests/test_check_m3_log.gd` pins each rule on a log written in the test (skipped where python3 is missing).

## The acts (`tests/bot/acts/crowd_acts.gd`)

10. **"Mixed six" is Punk, Kicker, Thug, Bruiser, Punk, Kicker at the six surround points in that order**, one every 24 frames: ahead (east alley), left (doorway), right (side alley), behind (west alley), behind-left, behind-right. The Kicker, which wants the blind side, and the Bruiser start behind or beside him. *Alternative:* a random order. *Why not:* the plan names the order and a repeated measurement needs a fixed one.
11. **"Mixed six, fight" uses the held-back autopilot (the slice's `fight()`), with Punks topping the six up.** *Alternative:* the autopilot that walks up to enemies. *Why not:* it kills the Thug and the Kickers before they attack and the act would show a player beating a crowd, not the crowd's fight. `keep_alive(6)` can only top up with one archetype, so after a few kills the mix is Punks (the plan's `keep_alive(count, id)` signature).
12. **"Waves to the boss" uses the full-speed autopilot (it walks up), `boss_wave_every` 2 and ends 6 s after the boss's adds are in.** (Scope, corrected by the final review fixes: with `boss_wave_every` 2 the table gives wave 1 (two Punks), wave 2 (four Punks) and then the boss, so **this act is evidence of the waves' mechanics and of the boss's arrival and call-in only**. It plays no mixed wave (the table's waves 3 to 5: Punk, Kicker, Thug and Bruiser together) and the boss is beaten before it attacks. The mixed waves and the boss wave fought in a crowd are the acts "Mixed waves" and "Boss wave, held back" and the waves test's second case in `tests/test_m3_done_when.gd`; see `fix-final-instruments-and-evidence.md`.) Held back it took minutes to clear a wave; at full speed the boss wave starts at about 25 s and the phase 2 adds are called at about 43 s (the cap is 120 s). The boss never gets to attack: the autopilot walks up to it as it comes in and beats it in APPROACH (one boss wind-up in the whole act, at f3012, after the act's end). That is the open risk P3-boss logged ("the boss is easier than a boss should be"), seen again here. It was a starvation, not difficulty: the boss never reached POSTURE between chains and so never asked for a token; fixed at the final review (it wakes in place and is served first), after which `boss_health` and the cadence are real knobs, still unchanged. The act itself would now see the boss wind up before the act's end (the numbers shift; re-record if the sheets are re-judged).
13. **The warning act's boss throws its pound from 2.55 m, not its charge.** The charge from behind-right at 4 m would put the boss's capsule inside the parked car (`boss_acts.gd` only does the left side), and the pound has the same heavy burst and a short reach. The charge's long reach from behind is already in "Boss charge behind".
14. **The perf act's sampler is a coroutine of its own beside the act**, so it times the six spawns (the hitch of a spawn is what the plan asks about) as well as the fight, and it logs one line per enemy that appears. The engine's `TIME_PROCESS` and `TIME_PHYSICS_PROCESS` monitors in it include the probe's lens sampling and the autopilot (7 ms every tenth tick) and read high; the frame lines and the probe's R15, which takes its own cost out, are the measure. (Corrected by the final review fixes: those two monitors are not the last frame's. Each holds the **maximum single process frame or physics tick of the last second**, so a window can show a figure from the second before it, and the first windows show a startup spike (a physics reading of 93 ms beside a worst wall frame of 23 ms). The act's third column, "bodies" (`PHYSICS_3D_ACTIVE_OBJECTS`), was **always 0**: the Jolt server does not report it, so every PERF line said "0 bodies" while 26 enemies went down and the act said nothing of ragdoll load. The columns are now labelled as the second's maxima, and the bodies are counted directly: the simulating bodies of every running, not-asleep ragdoll plus the awake pipes. The frame lines and the probe's R15 are the only measure of cost, as this call already said.) A windowed run is not 60 Hz (about 105 frames a second here, two or three frames to a physics tick), so the frame list has long and short frames and the p95 of my own sampler (22 ms) is not R15's (13 ms).

## The crowd bot (`tests/bot/crowd_bot.gd`)

15. **`--seed` starts every archetype's numbering at 7n, not only the Punk's** (`_fresh_serials()`, from the catalog: Punk, Kicker, Thug, Bruiser, Boss), so a repeated measurement of an act with a Kicker or a Bruiser (each enemy's choices are seeded by its name) is repeatable without the act's own `_seed_names()`. With `--seed=0` nothing changes (the numbering starts at 0 as before).
16. **`_reset` frees the pipes lying on the floor (group `thug_pipe`), not the one the player holds.** The Thug's drop is a rigid body that outlives its Thug; a later act would find it lying in the street (and the LB cue up). A pipe in the player's hand is never touched: freeing it would leave the player's `_weapon` dangling.
17. **`keep_alive(count, id := &"punk")`** (the plan's signature): the top-up spawns `id` from the next surround point. Reset to the Punk at every act.

## The LB pick-up cue

18. **The HUD's cue asks the player: a read-only `PlayerCharacter.pickup_target() -> Weapon`.** It returns what LB would take this frame (an available weapon within `pickup_range`) only when LB would do that: on his feet, alive, nothing in his hands, and no enemy in the grab's reach (LB grabs a standing enemy first, so a cue that promised the pipe would lie). It changes nothing and `_grab_action()` is untouched, so the simulation is the same. *Alternatives:* (a) the HUD repeats the distance test itself (a second copy of `_find_weapon()`'s formula that would drift); (b) a billboard above the pipe in the world (the pipe is below the lens, which is the problem; a marker above it would be in the view, but it is art and spec of its own); (c) show whenever a pipe is in reach, even with an enemy to grab (simpler, wrong half the time in a crowd). The one line in `player.gd` is the integration fix (see the list below).
19. **A small panel low in the view ("LB", "PICK UP"), breathing, tunable in "Pick-up cue" under "Grab and weapons"**: `pickup_cue_enabled`, `pickup_cue_size` 1.0, `pickup_cue_opacity` 0.95, `pickup_cue_pulse_s` 0.9. It sits where the pipe lies (below the centre, 250 to 340 px under it at 1080p), clear of the top corners the rear warning owns and of the MASH prompt (a held player can pick nothing up). In the sheets it reads at the lower middle without covering the action (the Punk's torso passes behind it). *Alternative:* a larger, centred prompt. *Why not:* the plan says small, and at the centre it would sit on the crosshair.

## Performance (R15), measured windowed and fixed

20. **The spawn hitch passed 33 ms once in four runs, so the codemap's allowed fix went in:** `HumanoidRig._build_animation()` takes the named `AnimationLibrary` from a static cache per (scale, stance) (`_named_library()`); the per-move library stays per rig. Nothing alters the named clips after the build (the rig and the AI only read lengths and tracks; checked by search), so one library serves every `AnimationPlayer`, and the 57 rig tests and the rest pass unchanged. "Perf, six with ragdolls", probe R15, four runs each:

   | | p95 frame (ms) | spawn hitch (ms) |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | before the cache | 13.0, 13.2, 13.1, 13.3 | 34.9, 29.1, 28.3, 28.6 |
   | after it | 13.1, 13.1, 13.1, 13.1 | 24.5, 25.7, 22.9, 25.4 |

   The boss's phase 2 call-in ("Boss phase 2 adds", three runs): p95 11.4 to 11.5 ms and spawn 23.1 to 26.1 ms with the cache, 11.0 to 11.4 and 25.6 to 27.9 without. *Alternative:* leave it (the median passed). *Why not:* 34.9 is over the limit and the margin was 4 ms; the cache is mechanical, costs nothing and the gate file had already named it as the thing to watch. The first knockdown's ragdoll build is not a hitch worth a fix: the worst frame within 0.5 s of a body going down is the same 30 to 35 ms as any frame of the fight. (Corrected by the final review fixes: the original gloss, "physics ticks with the probe's lens sampling in them, not a build", leaned on the act's `physics` and `process` columns, which are one-second maxima and say nothing of what a given frame held, so the attribution is withdrawn. What stands is the measured comparison: the worst frame within 0.5 s of a fall is no worse than the worst frame of the whole run, and the median spawn hitch and p95 in the table above are the probe's R15, unaffected.)

## R2 and a hold's follow-up (a finding, and a change to the probe)

21. **R2 failed once, at seed 1 of "Mixed six, fight": a gap of 11 frames. The cause is the probe counting a hold's follow-up as a wind-up start, so the probe no longer does.** The sequence: a Bruiser's `bruiser_grab` wind-up starts (f853); the director gives the next go 21 frames later, a Kicker's jump kick (f874); the grab connects (f885) and the Bruiser's `bruiser_headbutt` starts, with the player already in its hands, and its `windup_started` is 11 frames after the Kicker's. The follow-up is the second half of the attack whose wind-up started at f853 (the grab's: gated by `may_start_windup()` and warned like any), its startup is the mash window, its warning is dark by design (`warning_reach_m()`), and the player held takes only his holder's hit. R1 already counts the hold as one attacker and R3 already exempts the hold's last hit for that reason. So `Probe._on_windup_started()` skips R2's bookkeeping while the player is GRABBED by that attacker (`_holds_player()`); the wind-up is still counted in `windups` and R3. `test_readability_probe.gd` has the case (a real `start_grabbed()`), which fails on the old code with 11. *Alternative:* gate it in the game, i.e. make `EnemyDirector.may_start_windup()` refuse a go within 21 frames before a grab in WINDUP connects (the follow-up's start is predictable: the grab's strike frame). *Why not:* it would reach into P1-director's and P2-grab's code for a case the player cannot read as two starts (he is held, face to face, mashing, and the kick that started 11 frames earlier cannot land on him); it is the lead's call at the gate if it reads the metric by its letter: the figure by the letter is 11 frames in 1 of the 8 "observed" and "fight" runs and none in the other 9 runs of the five acts. With the fix the least gap over the 17 runs is 21 frames (the director's 0.35 s).

## Findings flagged, not changed

- **The non-KO knockdown view (the "belly slab")** is frequent in a mixed crowd: in "Mixed six, observed" (a player who does nothing, god mode) he is knocked down three times in 22 s and in a reaction 43% of the time (longest chain 2.0 s); each knockdown is about two seconds of a pale floor slab and the player's own arm or torso filling the lens (sheet `captures/m3_p4/observed/sheets/overview_0.png`, frames 12 to 14, and `overview_1.png`, frames 14 to 15). Not severe (it is the view of the player's own body on the floor, and it ends), flagged for Game-boy's call as the plan asks.
- **The boss is too easy for the autopilot** (above, call 12; the starvation half is fixed at the final review, the length of the fight is Game-boy's call: M3-judgement-calls.md).
- **Lens cover in a fought crowd:** R8 p50 33%, p95 65% in "Mixed six, fight" (the player is punching the enemy in front of him, and a Bruiser or a Thug at 1.2 m fills half the view); in "observed" (the player does nothing) p50 5%, p95 45%.
- **R9** (blind grants, soft, at least 0.6 in a crowd): 50% of 4 in "Mixed six, observed", 100% of 1 in the perf act, none elsewhere: too few mixed grants to judge; the hard R9 is `test_enemy_director.gd`'s.

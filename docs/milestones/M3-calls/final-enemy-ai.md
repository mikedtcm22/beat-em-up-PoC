Companion of `fix-final-enemy-ai-ring-and-tokens.md` (the calls and the measurements); the lead owns README.md, docs/ARCHITECTURE.md and docs/milestones/M3-judgement-calls.md and applies the wording below.

## For Game-boy (spec question, default taken): should the boss have a poise or get-up armor window? (outcome spec_question)

The finding's second suggestion was a poise counter or a post-get-up armor window (heavy hits absorbed for N frames after the boss stands, or at most one heavy stagger per X seconds). Not implemented: it is a new armor rule beyond SPEC §7 (the boss's heavy-hit rule is already a departure awaiting a ruling, README), and P3-boss call 4 weighed "a stagger immunity window after a stagger" and "a poise counter" and left them out. With the AI fix alone the boss attacks every time it wakes (6 wind-ups and 2 landed attacks in a 43 s kill where it had 2-4 and 1) but the fight is as short, because it is the autopilot's DPS against 300 HP that sets the length. *Default taken:* nothing more; to judge in play, and the knobs now reach it (cooldown 2.0: about 45 s and 4 hits; `boss_health` 500: 60-70 s). *Proposal if Game-boy wants a longer fight without a bigger bar:* after it stands, the boss absorbs heavy hits (not finishers) for `boss_wake_armor_frames` (say 45, the length of the pound's wind-up less the commit pause), implemented as one more clause in `Boss._apply_armor()` next to the wind-up one and a `boss_wake_armor_frames` tunable; or a wake-up attack with no commit pause (the squared-up beat skipped for a woken enemy), which lands the pound 12 frames sooner. Either changes how the boss fight feels to a player who hits it, so it is Game-boy's call.

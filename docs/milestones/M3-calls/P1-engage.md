# M3 judgement calls: P1-engage (a token holder never waits at the player's nose)

Calls the P1-engage builder made where the brief left a gap or two readings. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked: the default that changes least was taken. Measurements are headless runs of the crowd bot's acts through the engage acts (`tests/bot/acts/engage_acts.gd`: the slice's own acts with the engage meter on, so R1-R15 equal the slice act's), `-- --act="Engage: observed" --seed=<n>`, deterministic per seed. "Seeds 0-3" is four such runs. "Before" is the code at `47c1915` (the slice gate's merge), "after" the final commit. The meter's words: the **nose** is within 1.3 m of the player's origin, a hold is the span an enemy has a token, "standing still at the nose" is a holder in ENGAGE there at under 0.3 m/s.

## The numbers, before and after

R10 was 100% with no false positive, no latency and no end lag in every run, before and after; R3 0 unwarned hits; R11 0 (max knockdown hit damage); R13 0 stuck in every run, and the waves act cleared 3 waves in 90 s both times. R1 and R2 are in the tables. R12 (passive): the first KO of the player came at 14.3-16.1 s before and 16.3-17.6 s after (the limit is 12), and the worst 2 s of damage was 30 before (exactly the limit) and 24 after.

**What the tables count.** "Nose s per hold" is the seconds a holder spends within 1.3 m of the player in ENGAGE (the last stretch of its walk in included; a Punk covers a quarter of the lens at 1.3 m), per hold: mean, 95th percentile and longest. "Nose s in all" is the same summed over the act, with in brackets the part where it stood still (under 0.3 m/s) there, waiting for the go. "Holder at nose" is the share of the act with at least one holder there. R8 is the probe's: the share of the lens covered by enemy meshes, the median and the 95th percentile of the samples, and the share of the samples over half. Holds are token spans; "wound up" says how many ended in a wind-up (the others were taken back: the player closed, a hit, a block).

**Reading them.**
- **The nose wait is gone.** Before, a holder stood at the nose for 0.36-0.57 s on average per hold (the longest 0.8-2.6 s), 5.7-9.7 s of a 22-25 s act with 1.8-5.2 s of that standing still, and a holder was there 23-38% of the time. After, 0.16-0.21 s per hold (the longest 0.27-0.37 s: the last stretch of the walk in), none standing still, 10-14% of the time. The slice test asserts it (`tests/test_slice_crowd.gd`); run against the old actor and director it fails all three assertions (0.93 s standing still, p95 1.37 s, a holder at the nose 39% of the run).
- **The lens.** R8's median and 95th percentile barely move: they are the Punk winding up in front of the player at 0.95 m, which covers about half the lens for its 22 frames (P1-int's finding, inherent to a standing jab). What fell is the part that was waiting: the share of samples over half in passive went from 4.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0 % to 0.0 in all four seeds (seed 0 before: 11 of its 15 over-half samples were a holder standing at the nose for its go, none after), and the 95th percentile in passive from 40-47% to 26-40%.
- **Pressure** falls a little and the attacks come one at a time. Hits on the player per act: 22.8 (observed) and 23.5 (passive) before, 20.5 and 20.8 after, about a tenth; wind-ups 14.5 and 15.2 before, 12.5 and 12.2 after; R1 is 2 before and 1 after (call 8). The autopilot's acts show the opposite: a Punk waiting at the nose used to be the autopilot's target and was punched down before it attacked (the waves act: 39 holds, 20 wound up), now it waits out of reach and attacks (39 holds, 32-35 wound up), so those acts have as many or more hits (fight 9.8 to 11.2, waves 10.0 to 12.5) and many more wind-ups (waves 19 to 33.5). ## The wait

1. **A read-only director query, `EnemyDirector.windup_eta_s(enemy)`, not a reservation of the next wind-up slot at step-in.** Both were in the brief. The query is stateless: it answers from the director's own clocks (the gap, the hit grace) and from what the enemies are doing right now, so nothing has to be given back on any of the exit paths (hit, KO, despawn, F4 removal, room switch). A reservation is state per holder that every one of those paths would have to clear (L4's lens). `may_start_windup()` is untouched and is still the one gate at the wind-up, so R2 is as guaranteed as before. *Alternative:* `reserve_windup(enemy, arrive_s)`. *Why not:* the pruning is the director's backstop for tokens and would need the same for reservations; the stateless answer is also the one a test double can fake (`tests/test_enemy_actor.gd`'s `EtaDirector`).
2. **The holder walks in when `eta <= distance still to walk / engage_speed_mps + engage_go_lead_s`, and otherwise stands squared up where it is.** Every tick, so it stops again where it stands if the go slips away mid-walk (a hit lands on the player). Because the walk shortens as the eta runs down, a holder that starts when the condition first holds arrives as the go does. `engage_go_lead_s` is 0.1 s (new tunable, Posture subgroup); the sweep is in the numbers above. *Alternatives:* 0.0 (arrives a hair late: the avoidance's one to three ticks of lag make a holder slower than its 3 m/s, which costs a tenth of the pressure for nothing), 0.2 and 0.3 (they bring the waiting back: a holder 0.3 s early waits 0.3 s at the nose). *Why 0.1:* the largest value where nobody stands at the nose.
3. **The eta counts strikes already on their way, not only what the director has recorded.** Without it a holder walked in while another attack was winding up and got to the nose a frame or two before the first hit landed (the wind-up gap, 0.35 s, is almost exactly a jab's 22-frame wind-up), then stood at the nose through the jab's flinch, the cross's flinch and the grace after them. The first version (the eta from the gap, the hit grace, the player's hitstun and invulnerability only) still left holders standing still at the nose for 1.8-7.7 s of a 22-25 s act and 0.4-0.7 s per hold. So `EnemyActor.last_strike_eta_s()` says when the last strike of the attack an enemy is throwing begins (through the chain: the jab's whole length plus the cross's startup; 0 once it has begun or whiffed), and the director adds the hit grace to the largest of them. *Alternative:* a fixed "stay out while anybody is attacking". *Why not:* it would hold a holder back through the whiff of a block or a dodge too, where the real go is open the moment the strike has passed; this ends with the strike.
4. **One holder at a time walks in: the head of the line.** The head is the first holder, in grant order, whose attack has not started; everybody else gets an infinite eta and waits on the ring, and its turn comes with the head's wind-up (item 3). Two holders whose etas are both small would otherwise arrive together, the second at the nose a frame after the first's wind-up started. *Alternative tried first:* any holder whose own eta is near may walk in, but not while another holder is walking in (a flag on the enemy, the director reading it). *Why not:* the race was won by whoever's tick came first, which is the tree order, and the one whose tick came last starved: 12.2 s of waiting on the ring in one act at seed 2. Grant order is first come, first served and needs no flag. Blind-spot priority acts at the grant (a blind waiter granted with others is ahead of them) and I left the line in grant order: letting a blind holder jump a holder that had already walked half way would stop that one mid-walk, at the nose if it was close. The cap (3) is rarely the limit, so the priority does little in either design; that is the director's own finding from the slice gate.
5. **The director does not run the token timeout down for a holder whose go it is holding back.** `director_token_timeout_s` (4 s) is for a holder that could go and does not; a holder standing on the ring behind the head through a long volley (the head's strikes, the grace, two more holders) is not sitting on its token. `_stretch_waits()` adds the time with `windup_eta_s() > 0` back to the grant clock. *Alternatives:* raising the timeout (it would also keep a stuck holder longer), and nothing (the holders' tokens are taken back and re-granted at once, a churn of commit pauses). A held fight or a closed player answers INF and the tokens are taken back as before.
6. **`engage_timeout_s` counts only the frames a holder spends walking.** The wait on the ring is not a failed chase. *Alternative:* count the wait too. *Why not:* the holders that wait longest (the third in a line, 2.4-3.5 s at the most) would all give the token back and take a 5 s cooldown, a Punk punished for being polite.
7. **The test double says 0.** A director with its own script (`tests/fixtures/stub_director.gd`, which the Kicker, Thug and grab builders' tests use) decides nothing, so `windup_eta_s()` answers 0 for it and an enemy steps in at once, as it did. No file I do not own changes. A test that wants a go that is far off uses a subclass in its own file.

## Why the attacks now come one after the other

8. **R1 is 1, not 2, with a player who takes every hit; this is the cost of the fix, measured, and I did not buy it back.** Before, two Punks were in WINDUP or ATTACK at once in every act (R1 max 2), by the two-frame window between the wind-up gap (21 ticks) and a jab's strike (22): a second holder standing at the nose got its go one frame before the first hit landed. After, the holder behind the head is on the ring and walks in to arrive as the grace ends, so a landed hit is followed by the next wind-up one after another. A block or a dodge gives no grace, so with a defending player attacks could still overlap, but R1 is 1 in the autopilot's fight and waves acts too (2 before in the fight act): the holders there wait out of its reach and attack in turns as well. `test_slice_crowd`'s "the crowd does attack together" (more than one in WINDUP or ATTACK, god mode and a player who stands still) was an artefact of that window and is replaced by "at least eight wind-ups in the run"; the rest of its assertions are unchanged, and new ones hold the wait. *Alternative:* let a holder start its wind-up inside the grace so that its strike lands as the grace ends (the starts would overlap, the hits would not). *Why not:* it changes `may_start_windup()`'s rule, which the slice gate locked (the gap, the grace, the cap), and it is more pressure, not less; the brief was where the holder waits. The lead may want it as the lever to restore overlap (a hit lands every 1.3 s either way); I did not try it.

## The lone Punk on the player's axis

9. **A step-in aims `engage_aim_offset_m` (0.1 m, new tunable, Posture subgroup) to one side of the player's origin, a side per enemy from its name.** The cause is the avoidance, not the path: a body whose wish points exactly at the centre of the player's obstacle may keep only the gap over the avoidance horizon as its speed ((d - 0.75 m) / 0.75 s, 0.4 m/s at 1.05 m), so it slows the whole way in and never gets to the 0.9-1.0 m it needs inside `engage_timeout_s`. 0.02 m off the axis the tie is broken and it walks in at full speed. Measured on the lone Punk of the finding in the numbers above (eight offsets and obstacle radii). The side is the parity of the name's hash, so neighbours in the spawn order take opposite sides and nothing is drawn from the enemy's own random numbers (that would have moved every enemy's seeded choices). *Alternatives:* the obstacle radius (it is `player_avoid_radius_m`, the Waves subgroup, P1-room's; 0.2 m or less does reach range, but 30-35 ticks later than the offset, and it changes how every enemy keeps off the player), no obstacle while engaging (the enemy cannot switch the player's node; an avoidance mask on the agent would also drop the other enemies, which the crowd needs), steering without avoidance for the last stretch (the neighbours stop seeing it for those ticks). *Why the offset:* it is local to the enemy's own step-in, it needs no other package's file and the lone Punk's numbers are the same as the off-axis ones.

## The jacket

10. **The Street Punk's jacket is deep teal, `Color(0.02, 0.5, 0.56)`, the accents stay the yellow cap and shoes.** SPEC §10 keeps red for blood, the rear warning and the finisher's accents, and the jacket was red (0.82, 0.12, 0.2). Teal is saturated (so the grunt pops against the muted street, SPEC §10 "enemies saturated"), is far from the training dummy's orange and from every effect colour, and the brief told me to take teal or purple because the Kicker and the Thug builders take the others. *Alternative:* purple. *Why teal:* with the yellow accents it is the complementary pair that reads at 1 m and 3 m against the lane's brown and grey (`new_obs_a`, `new_obs_b`); purple is left for the Thug or the Kicker, whichever picks it. A test (`test_the_punks_jacket_is_not_red`) holds it: saturated, a hue away from red and from the dummy's orange.

## Not done

12. **A holder that waits on the ring waits where it stood when the token came, not at its slot:** the director moves slots, not holders. It was posturing on the ring, so 2.5-4 m by construction. The posture ring is also still not spread by where the enemies stand (P1-int call 17).
13. **A holder that is already at the nose when its token comes** (it backed off only to 1.2 m, or the player walked up to it) has nowhere to wait but where it is, and waits there for the go. It does not happen in the acts (0.0 s standing still in every run above).
14. **Blind-spot priority is by grant, not by line** (call 4): a blind holder granted behind an in-view holder that is already waiting attacks after it.
15. **The meter's lens attribution is by the most covering enemy's state;** with 0-6 over-half samples an act it says no more than "what is left is the wind-up at the nose".

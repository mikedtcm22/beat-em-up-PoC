# M3 judgement calls: P1-hud

Calls the P1-hud builder made where the plan left a gap, two readings, or could not be followed. Each is a decision, an alternative not taken and why. Game-boy was not asked: the default that changes least was taken.

1. **`RearWarning` emits `cleared(side)`, not `hidden(side)` (the contract named in Part C section 2.9 cannot be written).** `RearWarning extends Control`, and `CanvasItem` already has a signal called `hidden`; a script cannot redefine a native signal (parse error "Member hidden redefined"). *Alternative:* extend `Node` and draw through a child control. *Why not:* the contract also says `extends Control`, and the HUD part list, the corner tests and every other HUD part are Controls; a rename of one signal is the smaller departure. **Consumers that connect `hidden` (P1-room's crowd bot, P1-instruments' oracle, both duck-typed) would connect the native, argument-less signal and fail at the first emit: they must connect `cleared`.** Reported as a request and a departure.

2. **`rear_warning_min_alpha` is used: it is the alpha at the trough of each pulse (a share of the burst's steady alpha), not a floor on a lone attacker's brightness.** Part B's brightness formula (`1 + step x (n - 1)`) has no use for it and Part C's (`min_alpha + step x (n - 1)`) was replaced. *Alternative:* remove it. *Why not:* `tests/test_m3_contracts.gd` (S0's, shared) lists it, so removing it means editing a file I do not own; and a pulse that beats between a dip and full is a use that costs nothing. Its doc comment in `tuning_data.gd` now says so.

3. **Brightness is Part B's multiplier `1 + rear_warning_brightness_step x (n - 1)`, applied to the burst's colour (a modulate above 1, which clips toward yellow-white) and to its alpha (`rear_warning_opacity x brightness`, capped at 1).** At the default opacity 0.9 the alpha alone gives only 0.9, 1.0, 1.0 for one, two and three attackers: too little to see. The colour brightening is the visible part (the two-on-the-left crops show the "!" going from cream to bright yellow). *Alternative:* alpha only (Part C's floor-plus-step ramp). *Why not:* the ramp starts a lone burst at 0.585 alpha, dimmer than SPEC's opacity, and saturates at three.

4. **The burst defaults are retuned: `rear_warning_base_px` 160 (was 170), `rear_warning_inset_px` 96 (was 48), `rear_warning_heavy_scale` 1.3 (was 1.4).** The burst is always fully on screen and inside the reserved 20% x 20% corner (384 x 216 at 1080p): with the centre 48 px in, a 170 px burst hung 37 px off the screen edge, and a 238 px heavy one could not fit a 216 px corner at all (a hard clamp would have made the heavy scale a silent 1.27). 160 x 1.3 = 208 fits; the normal one sits 16 px off the edges and the heavy one flush. *Alternative:* keep the plan's numbers and let the art overhang the screen edge. *Why not:* it cuts the star's points and the "!" off; the corner test wants it inside. The size, inset and scale stay live in F1.

5. **A tunable added: `rear_warning_pulse_grow` (0.25, 0..1): how much larger the burst pops in, fading over the pulses.** The pulse needs a scale as well as an alpha ("scale and alpha", Part B 7) and feel numbers are not constants. In my own group.

6. **`rear_warning_follow_turn` re-runs the whole rule every frame (bearing, range) and tracks a wind-up that started in view.** Off (default): the side is fixed at the start, and an attacker in view at the start never warns even if the player turns away (the rule fires "when the wind-up starts"). On: the warning goes while the player faces the attacker and comes back if they turn away. *Alternative:* re-evaluate only left, right and both for an entry that fired. *Why not:* a follow-turn that cannot light for a wind-up that began in view is the half of the feature a player would notice.

7. **While the player's health is 0 (`rear_warning_hide_in_ko`) the corners go dark but the wind-ups stay tracked, so one still on when the player revives lights again.** No warning is raised for a wind-up that starts in the KO view. *Alternative:* drop every entry when the health reaches 0. *Why not:* a revive inside an attack's window (a boss's long charge) is the one case where the player needs it, and the polled end drops stale entries anyway.

8. **The poll ends an entry when the attacker is not in WINDUP or ATTACK, or runs another move id (Part B), not "or GRABBING" (Part C).** A held enemy attack ends on `grabbed(attacker, _)` already; GRABBING is the state after it. *Alternative:* keep GRABBING live. *Why not:* it would hold a warning through a mash window that the held view and the MASH prompt already cover. A victim being grabbed (`grabbed(_, attacker)`) is ended by the poll (its state is GRABBED), not by an event, as the rule says.

9. **`corner_state()` has extra keys: `brightness`, `scale` (the pulse's) and `rect` (the drawn rectangle); `alpha` is what is drawn this frame, the pulse included.** The contract asks for `{active, count, heavy, alpha, size_px}`; the extras are for the tests and for P1-instruments. `size_px` is the steady size after the heavy scale and the corner clamp. `burst_rect()`, `live_count()` and `is_tracking()` are public too.

10. **The HUD scales the warning's time by `TimeController.sim_scale()` (and the pause's `game_delta`), the widget's `advance(delta)` takes plain seconds.** A bot or test that steps the widget directly decides its own time. *Alternative:* scale inside the widget. *Why not:* the same `advance()` contract as every other HUD part, and the tests can step it exactly.

11. **`shown` fires whenever a corner's count rises, including a side swap under follow-turn; it does not fire when the count falls.** `cleared` fires only when a corner goes dark. So a log of W+ and W- lines tells every light and every dark but not "one of two ended". *Alternative:* also emit on a fall. *Why not:* `shown` would then not mean "pulse again".

12. **The boss bar: shown by the HUD for the first live member of group `"boss"` it is not showing; it stays through the boss's death (the bar drains) and goes on `vanished`, on the boss being freed, or on `hide_boss()`.** `show_boss()` still works for any actor, and a bar shown by hand stays while no boss is in the group. *Alternative:* hide the moment health reaches 0. *Why not:* A3.4 says hide on `vanished`, and a bar that empties over the lying body reads as the kill.

13. **`AudioDirector` protects a wind-up cue from the moment it starts until `windup_ended` (either way); a strike coming out lets the cue ring but frees it to be stolen.** The oldest sound that is not a live cue goes first; only a pool full of live cues takes one, counted in the public `windup_voices_stolen` (R14, for P1-instruments to read). *Alternative:* protect until the voice stops by itself. *Why not:* a pool full of finished-wind-up tails would then starve the hits.

14. **`voice_count` stays 32.** The worst single frame (six wind-ups, three heavy hits on the player, a three-body finisher) asks the pool for 36 sounds; with the protection no wind-up cue is lost, and the sounds that give way are the oldest tails. *Alternative:* raise it to 40. *Why not:* the brief says raise it only if the test shows a wind-up cue starved, and it does not; real sounds are spread over many frames.

15. **`voice_pitch_mult` applies to every `voice_pitch()` caller: the wind-up effort and the pain grunt.** A multiplier of 0 or less is ignored. *Alternative:* the wind-up only. *Why not:* a deep-voiced Bruiser that grunts high when hit is wrong.

16. **The F3 AI layer draws only while a director and `"ai_enemy"` members exist, and its own colours, radii and lengths are `@export`s of `debug_draw.gd`** (like the block arc's radius): drawing parameters, not feel numbers. The line counts of `test_ui.gd` are unchanged. The view lines use `rear_warning_angle_deg` and `rear_warning_behind_deg` live. Colours were tuned from the side-view capture: lavender ring, blue view boundary, pink behind sector, magenta token, orange slot, violet path.

17. **`warning_bot` checks each act against the corner the act names (written by hand), not against `ViewGeometry`, and prints each log line once.** `ViewGeometry` is what the widget calls, so the oracle would agree with it by construction; the crowd oracle (P1-instruments) is that comparison. The bot prints rather than adding to `log_lines`, which the base bot dumps again at the end (a doubled `WARN-CHECK`). The "heavy" act uses the stub enemy fixture, since a `TrainingDummy` has no `heavy` flag; "blocked ends it" places the attacker at 58 degrees (outside the 55 degree view, inside the 60 degree guard arc).

18. **The sweep test places bearings at 2.5 + 5k degrees, not 5k.** 55 and 160 are multiples of 5, and a dummy placed there lands on the edge to within floating point. The edges are the truth table's (`test_view_geometry.gd`: 54.9, 55.1, 159.9, 160); the sweep proves the real room and the real HUD agree with the rule everywhere else, 72 bearings.

19. **Wind-ups from an actor that is not a `CombatActor`, or on team 0, never warn;** the HUD part reads `team` and `heavy` on the actor itself. A freed or out-of-tree attacker is dropped by the poll.

20. **(Final review, final-warn-01) A heavy burst is the normal one times `rear_warning_heavy_scale`, at every size setting: the normal burst is held to the corner's short side over the scale, and the heavy one is that capped size times the scale.** Item 4 clamped the normal and the heavy burst to the corner each on their own, so above a size of 1.04 the heavy one stopped growing (216 px at 1080p), the normal one caught it up at 1.35, and from there to the slider's 3.0 both were 216 px: SPEC section 8's "larger for the Bruiser and boss" held only at the defaults. Now `RearWarning._steady_size()` is `min(base x size, edge / heavy_scale)`, times `heavy_scale` for a heavy attacker (`edge` = 20% of the canvas's short side). The pop is the same rule: `burst_rect()` clips steady x pulse to the corner, so a heavy pop is never smaller than a normal one's. The steady defaults stay (160 px normal, 208 px heavy at 1080p) and so does the pop's clip at the default (a heavy pop 216 px against the normal pop's 200): changing it means changing item 4's defaults. At a heavy scale of 1.0 the two sizes are equal, which is right. *Alternative:* narrow the slider's `@export_range` to what fits (about 1.04 at the defaults). *Why not:* the ceiling depends on `rear_warning_base_px` (40 to 400) and `rear_warning_heavy_scale` (1 to 3) as well, so any fixed range is wrong for some setting; and it would move the 1.04 to 1.35 stretch where the normal burst used to grow.
21. **The size slider's upper range saturates, and the plan makes that inherent.** M3-plan.md (line 166) binds every burst to the reserved 20% x 20% corner, so the largest burst is 216 px at 1080p whatever the slider says, and keeping a heavy burst heavy_scale times larger caps the normal one at 216 / 1.3 = 166 px: the slider does nothing above a size of about 1.04 at the defaults (it used to stop at 1.35). The range, 0.25 to 3.0, is left as it is and its doc comment in `tuning_data.gd` says so. Not a defect to fix without Game-boy: he can raise `rear_warning_base_px` or shrink the heavy scale in F1 if a bigger normal burst matters more to him than the ratio.

# Milestone 3: judgement calls

A running log of the calls the lead (and the builders) made without asking Game-boy during M3, each with at least one alternative not taken and why. The Slack digest at the end of M3 is drawn from this file. Add an entry whenever a call shapes what Game-boy will play or how the work runs; keep each one short.

This is the curated version. Each package's full list is in [M3-calls/](M3-calls/). Where a later call replaced an earlier one, only the final state is here, with the old value named. The last section lists every place the build reads SPEC.md differently from its text, for Game-boy to rule on.

## Process and delivery
- **Resume after a usage limit wakes the same working session first.** A one-time scheduled task fires 10 minutes after the window resets and messages the session titled "Beat-em-up: working session"; only if that session is gone does it start a takeover session from HANDOFF.md. *Alternative:* always start a fresh takeover session, as M2 did at account switches. *Why not:* the same session keeps its context and Game-boy's ultracode opt-in, and a fresh one would re-read everything.
- **The watcher checks every 30 / 15 / 5 minutes (below 70%, to 85%, above)**, against 20 / 10 / 5 before the 5x premium seat. *Alternative:* keep the old cadence. *Why not:* the same work now moves the needle a fifth as fast; the fast-burn rule still pulls a check in when a big workflow runs.
- **The playable build is a release export with scripts kept as text.** *Alternative:* binary-token scripts (the default), a slightly smaller pack. *Why not:* the F1 panel's tooltips read the `##` doc comments from the script source, so binary tokens would leave only the default and the range.
- **Universal (Intel and Apple Silicon) macOS binary, ad-hoc signed.** *Alternative:* arm64 only, about half the size. *Why not:* it costs nothing that matters and runs on any Mac Game-boy picks up. Without an Apple Developer ID the app can't be notarized either way, so the first launch needs right-click → Open.
- **One Street Punk went end to end, judged in first person, before any other archetype was built.** *Alternative:* M2's pattern, every archetype in parallel from the start. *Why not:* every archetype inherits the base, the director and the warning, so a problem found after five archetypes costs five times the rework. The slice gate found three crowd problems this way (back-offs at up to 6 m/s the wrong way, too little posturing, a wall of Punks in the lens) and locked the feel defaults (ring 2.5-4.0 m, circle speed 1.1 m/s, cap 3, wind-up gap 0.35 s) before the rest was built.
- **No feel question goes to Game-boy: each builder takes the default that changes least and logs it, and only first-person looks count.** *Alternative:* ask him at each grey-block choice. *Why not:* he can't judge fine feel before the art is in, and the side view is debug-only.

## Enemy behaviour and pacing
- **A token holder squares up for 12 frames (0.2 s) before it steps in and winds up.** *Alternative:* wind up as soon as the token arrives, or pause for 20 frames. *Why not:* the pause is the visible "I'm next"; 0.2 s reads without slowing the fight, and nothing in the plan favoured 20.
- **A wind-up tracks the player for its first half, then locks its facing.** *Alternative:* track through the whole wind-up, or lock earlier (the readability-first plan's 35% share). *Why not:* the lock is what a sidestep or dodge beats. At 180°/s and a 0.5 share, a 22-frame Punk wind-up turns about 33° and then holds, so a late sidestep makes it miss.
- **After attacking, an enemy holds still for 0.35 s, then backs off to its ring slot with its face to the player.** *Alternative:* turn and run, or a shorter hold (0.2 s). *Why not:* a retreat with its back turned reads as giving up (the rubric asks for "back off", not "run away"), the hold is the player's punish window, and 0.2 s gave no lens benefit. The slice review found back-offs running the wrong way at up to 6 m/s from a stale avoidance velocity; that is fixed and the peak walk is now 3.0 m/s.
- **A token holder waits squared up on the ring and steps in only when its go is near, one holder at a time, head of the line first.** *Alternative:* reserve the next wind-up slot when it steps in, or "stay out while anybody is attacking". *Why not:* a reservation is state that every exit path (hit, KO, F4 removal, room switch) would have to give back, and the blanket rule also holds a holder back through a whiff, when the go is open. Before, a holder stood at the player's nose for 0.36-0.57 s per hold on average (longest 0.8-2.6 s) and covered 40-60% of the lens; now it is 0.16-0.21 s with none standing still.
- **The Street Punk's attack cooldown is 5.0 s (was 1.4).** *Alternative:* 4.0 s or 6.0 s (10.0 s was also measured). *Why not:* 5.0 is the lowest value where lens coverage holds in every seed; 4.0 gave 16 hits in a 90 s wave and one passive-act miss, 6.0 cut the waves cleared to 2 in two seeds, and 10.0 gave 2.6-2.9 of six posturing but only 16-17 hits in 22 s. Below about 4 s the cooldown never binds, because it counts from the attack's end through the back-off. Pressure fell a lot (a held-back autopilot takes 9.5 hits in 90 s of waves, was 32.8; a passive player still dies in 14-16 s). More pressure should come from more Punks alive, not a shorter cooldown.
- **A step-in stops 0.05 m short of the strike's reach (was 0.1 m), and the Punk stays a stand-and-jab.** *Alternative:* a 0.0 margin, or a 0.30 m lunge in the jab and shove so the Punk steps in for the strike. *Why not:* 0.0 makes a third of the openers whiff a player who stands still (69% land). The lunge cuts the share of lens samples over half from 5.0% to 3.7% but lands only 91% against 97%, and it changes how the Punk attacks, which is the gate's call. At 0.05, samples over half fell from 6-9% to 2-5% and openers landing from 99% to 95-97%.
- **The other archetypes' attack cooldowns are set from measured sweeps: Kicker 3.0 s, Pipe Thug 8.0 s, Bruiser 4.0 s, boss 4.0 s.** *Alternative:* the starting seeds (1.2, 1.8, 2.2 and 1.8 s). *Why not:* the cooldown counts through the back-off, so values under about 3-4 s never bind and the director sets the pace. Kicker: 21 wind-ups in 40 s at 1.2 against 15-17 at 3.0, about half again a Punk's attacks at the same damage a second. Thug: its 30-damage combo is 2.5 times a Punk's damage rate at 5.0 and 2.0 times at 8.0. Bruiser: mid-plateau, about 15 damage every 6.4 s. Boss: about 9 attacks a minute against a passive player, binding from about 3.2 s.
- **Every archetype's approach walks a point on the posture ring instead of the chord through the player, and a body that has not moved is stalled.** An enemy inside the ring backs out along a diagonal, and one whose slot is more than 60° round the ring walks to it in 40° legs, each point checked against the navmesh (the Kicker's way round, moved into the base; final review, crowd-02). A repeat stall's detour goes the other way round the player and never takes a side with no navmesh under it; a body that has not moved a slot reach in `enemy_stuck_s` counts as stalled whatever its target does (an armored Bruiser had stood wedged at the player's nose for 3-5 s). *Alternative:* a pure radial back-out then the arc, or re-seating an enemy that is inside the ring in the director. *Why not:* the diagonal is the radial back-out when the slot is straight out and one leg instead of an L when it is round the ring, and the director cannot see where an enemy walks. The cost: the six-Punk fight's posturing went from 1.8/1.7/1.6 to 1.6/1.6/1.4 of 5.8, with its lens samples over half covered unchanged (3 to 7 of 388).

## The director
- **Wind-up starts are paced, not grants: no two wind-ups start less than 0.35 s (21 frames) apart.** *Alternative:* also space the grants by the same gap (the base plan's version). *Why not:* the readability rule is about wind-up starts, and two enemies granted a second apart can still arrive together. The cost is that a volley of three grants becomes three wind-ups 0.35 s apart, with the waiting ones squared up at their reach.
- **No wind-up starts while the player is in hitstun or stagger, or within 0.5 s of a hit that was not blocked.** *Alternative:* a longer gap alone (about 1 s to clear a stagger), or the state hold alone. *Why not:* a 1 s gap halves the pressure for every attacker, and the hold alone still chains (1.9-2.1 s) because a wind-up started a frame before the first hit still lands. Without the grace the longest chain of hits on the player was 4.83 s (a passive player was KO'd at 10.9-11.8 s); with it 1.15 s and 12.1-14.0 s, for 27-28 hits in 22 s instead of 28-32. A grace of 0.35 s left 2-3 s chains, and 0.75 s gave the same chains with fewer hits. Blocked hits start no grace.
- **A token is held from the grant until the enemy starts backing off, and a dodge or get-up only withholds new tokens.** *Alternative:* a token for the wind-up only (the base plan), release at the end of the back-off, or take every unstarted token back whenever the player is invulnerable. *Why not:* the cap is about enemies attacking at once, and releasing at the start of the back-off hands the next waiter its turn while this one walks away. Stripping tokens on every dodge would re-queue the whole crowd and lose their places in line. A downed, flying, held or KO'd player does take unstarted tokens back.
- **While F1 or F4 is open the fight holds: no tokens are granted, unstarted ones are taken back, enemies stand and face the player, and the waves' timers and the boss's call-in countdown stop.** *Alternative:* leave the crowd running (the overlay only froze the player's input), or let the wave timer run and spawn the moment the menu closes. *Why not:* SPEC §11 tuning is live, so a crowd would beat him up while he tunes, and a wave dropping on him as F4 closes is what the hold prevents. An attack already winding up finishes.
- **Posture slots are spread evenly over 360° and every live enemy has one; blind-spot priority is a 0-1 chance that an out-of-view waiter jumps the queue.** *Alternative:* a front-biased arc with a score weight, or spreading only the enemies without a token (the base plan). *Why not:* the spec wants enemies around the player, not in front, and a chance maps directly onto the slider. Spreading only tokenless enemies re-spread the circle with every token, so the others shuffled 6-12° at every attack; a seated enemy now keeps its seat while it walks or is knocked about (seats that followed positions made three 7 m slot jumps as six Punks crossed in from one alley).
- **A spawn weighs 0.1 against an enemy already on the ring when the director seats the crowd, and seated enemies choose their slots first.** *Alternative:* skip the boss in the reslot its call-in triggers. *Why not:* it fixes one enemy at one moment, and every spawn flips someone's seat the same way (the call-in's first Punk had given the lone boss the seat across the player, 180° round, so it walked through him after its beckon).
- **The crowd's real spread is reported, not fixed: the slots are even but the enemies leave wider empty arcs.** *Alternative:* re-seat an enemy after each attack, or make slots follow where the enemy stands. *Why not:* it needs a decision, and the two fixes tried (a radial back-off, and walking round the player along the ring) changed nothing over eight seeds. The widest gap between slots is 28° against an even 60°, but standing enemies leave a mean widest empty arc of 103-108° (worst 173-190°), because a Punk backing off with another Punk behind it often orbits 150° or more the long way round.

## The rear warning
- **"Where is it from the look" is one helper, `ViewGeometry`, that reads the player's look and never a camera, and the director's blind spot uses the warning's 55°.** *Alternative:* the active camera's basis, or a separate blind-spot angle. *Why not:* simulation can't read a camera, spins and the KO view would flip the corners, and two angles could disagree.
- **The warning ends on contact (a blocked hit too), a whiff, an interrupt, a grab or a KO, and never when the strike comes out.** *Alternative:* end it as the wind-up ends. *Why not:* it would clear before the hit it warns of; the plan names an early or late end as the warning's main risk. Across 190 and 134 real-Punk wind-ups the corner state agreed with the rule 100% of the time, with no false positives and no end lag.
- **Several attackers on one side brighten the burst by colour (1 + 0.2 for each extra attacker, clipping toward yellow-white) as well as by alpha.** *Alternative:* alpha only, a floor of 0.65 plus 0.2 a step. *Why not:* at the default opacity 0.9 alpha alone gives 0.9, 1.0 and 1.0 for one, two and three attackers, too little to see, and the ramp would start a lone burst at 0.585, dimmer than SPEC's opacity.
- **The burst defaults were retuned: base 160 px (was 170), inset 96 px (was 48), heavy scale 1.3 (was 1.4).** *Alternative:* keep the plan's numbers and let the art overhang the screen edge. *Why not:* overhanging cuts off the star's points and the "!", and a 238 px heavy burst could not fit the 216 px reserved corner at all; 160 × 1.3 = 208 fits. All three stay live in F1.
- **A heavy burst is always `rear_warning_heavy_scale` (1.3) times a normal one's size, at every size setting, and the size slider does nothing above about 1.04.** At a size of 1.5 both were clipped to the reserved 20% x 20% corner (216 px at 1080p) and came out the same, which lost SPEC §8's "larger for the Bruiser and boss" (final review, warn-01). Now the normal burst is held to the corner over the heavy scale (166 px at the defaults) and the heavy one is that times 1.3, steady and at the pop's peak; the defaults are unchanged (160 and 208 px). *Alternative:* cap the normal pop's pre-heavy size too, or narrow the slider's range. *Why not:* the first takes the normal pop away at the defaults (160 x 1.25 = 200 is over 166), a default change; the slider's ceiling depends on two other tunables, so a fixed range would be wrong the moment one moves. The plan's binding corner makes the saturation inherent.

## The crowd room and waves
- **The new street-lane crowd room is the main scene, and the M2 training room stays frozen behind F6.** *Alternative:* extract a shared room base from `test_room.gd`, or bake the training room a navmesh. *Why not:* eleven test files and three bots pin its geometry. Enemies spawned there steer straight (no navmesh) and create their own director.
- **The boss is its own wave, played after every four regular waves, and it calls in its own Punks at half health.** *Alternative:* add the boss to every Nth wave. *Why not:* a wave of up to six plus a boss is over the cap of six, so the boss would spawn last, after the others had fallen. Alone it is a fight of its own.
- **Enemies spawn one at a time, 0.6 s apart, and the boss's two Punks come the same way.** *Alternative:* a whole wave in one frame (the plan has the boss's two together), or pooled and recycled bodies. *Why not:* each rig build costs about 10 ms. Both boss adds in one frame gave a 39-40 ms worst frame near the spawn against a 33 ms limit; one at a time it is 24-28 ms. The Punk waves' spawn hitch is 26-31.5 ms, so there is little margin.
- **A wave sends exactly round(size × 0.35) of its enemies from outside the 55° view, in random order.** *Alternative:* an independent coin flip per spawn. *Why not:* "about 35%" would be a statistic and its test a flake. A wave of six sends two from behind every time, and "behind" means outside the view, so beside the player counts.
- **The player's avoidance circle is 0.3 m (the plan said 0.5 m).** *Alternative:* keep 0.5 and make the AI close to the avoidance floor. *Why not:* at 0.5 the avoidance held a Punk 0.96-0.99 m out, it timed out, and a 60 s recording had 0 wind-ups. At 0.3 (about where the bodies touch) the same Punks wind up at 0.94 m. The circle's job needs only about the body's radius.

## Enemy grabs on the player
- **A holder pins the player 0.9 m out (1.0 m from a Bruiser, 1.05 m from the boss) and grips his upper arms, not his collar.** *Alternative:* the plan's 0.75 m with fists on the collar. *Why not:* the lens sits at throat height, so a fist on the collar is a few centimetres from it, and at 0.75 m the first capture showed the grabber's chest as a wall 0.4 m away with its face out of the top of the view. Now the nearest holder mesh is 0.26 m from the lens and the face is in view.
- **A grab connects only if the player can be grabbed at the strike frame, tested once there, and its range is 1.05 m on a 1.8 m rig (the plan's seed was 1.3).** *Alternative:* test every active frame, and the plan's 1.3 m. *Why not:* a dodge's 8 invulnerable frames would then have to cover all 6 active frames, and the grab's fists reach 1.05 m (Bruiser) and 1.18 m (boss) from the body, so 1.3 m scaled to 1.48 m would grab a player the hands do not reach. A test holds `reach_m` to the measured pose within 0.15 m.
- **While a holder has the player, its follow-up (headbutt or slam) raises no rear warning.** *Alternative:* let it warn, or turn the player's look to the holder before the follow-up starts. *Why not:* the grab's own wind-up already warned him, his look is turned to the holder within 8 frames, and the hold is the follow-up's telegraph. One method on the enemy keeps the HUD and the oracle agreeing (readability check R10 stays 100%).
- **The MASH prompt is a yellow-filled comic panel below the centre of the view.** *Alternative:* red lettering. *Why not:* red is reserved for blood, the rear warning and the finisher's accents.

## The archetypes
- **Each archetype has its own saturated jacket and none is red: Punk teal (was red) with yellow accents, Kicker lime, Pipe Thug olive green, Bruiser purple with steel grey, boss charcoal with orange.** *Alternative:* keep the Punk red, or pick by hue alone. *Why not:* SPEC §10 keeps red for blood, the rear warning and the finisher's accents; teal with yellow reads at 1 m and 3 m against the street's brown and grey and is far from the orange dummy and the blue player. Watch: lime and olive are both green, so the Kicker's near-black accents, the Thug's cream ones and its yellow pipe do the telling apart.

**Street Punk**
- **The Punk's shove winds up with two raised open palms and the torso rocked back, not the player's shove wind-up.** *Alternative:* reuse the player's wind-up, hands at the chest. *Why not:* the Punk's two attacks must be told apart from across the room; raised open hands read as a push where a single cocked fist reads as a punch.

**Kicker**
- **A Kicker is no part of the ring: it takes a seat of its own as near directly behind the look as it can be, at least 20° (`director_flank_gap_deg`) from every other seat, and the others' seats never move for it. It gets there round the ring instead of through the player (every archetype's way now).** *Alternative:* the straight path to the slot; the director turning the whole ring so a slot sits behind the look (the slice's version, with the Kicker's seat the rearmost of an even spread); swapping seats with the occupant only; a hysteresis on the Kicker's seat. *Why not:* the straight path crossed the lens at about 1 m, and the ring that swung round the Kicker as the player turned cost about 0.3 of the posturing and the rear-first seating about 1.0, so a fought mixed crowd had 0.4-0.8 of 5.8 enemies posturing and now has 1.6-2.0; swapping alone left 0.5-0.8; a hysteresis (re-seat it only when the look has turned more than some degrees) is the next thing to try and was not needed. The cost: a Kicker's seat can be up to about 40° off directly behind when another seat is near it, still outside the 55° view.

**Pipe Thug**
- **The Thug's dropped pipe slides toward whoever hit it at 0.5 m/s, and is thick and yellow so it reads when the player looks down.** *Alternative:* push it along the hit at 2.0 m/s (the plan), and a HUD pick-up prompt. *Why not:* along the hit the pipe came to rest 2.75-3.16 m from the player, out of LB's 1.5 m reach; toward the hitter at 0.5 m/s it rested 0.64-1.03 m in front of him (0.87-1.29 m once it was damped to stop spinning, below). A pipe within reach is below the first-person lens at the forward pitch (the lens sees 29° down, a pipe 1 m ahead is 58° down), so he has to look down; a HUD cue is left for the HUD's owner to add.
- **A loose weapon rests by damping from its first touch of the world (0.1 a second becomes 6 linear and 8 angular, `weapon_rest_linear_damp` and `weapon_rest_angular_damp`), not by a rotation lock or a freeze.** The dropped pipe had gone on spinning at 3.3 rad/s and crept 0.32 m more in the ten seconds after it landed, and a thrown one spun at 8.7 rad/s (final review, tells-02). *Alternative:* lock the rotation except yaw on the floor; freeze it once it is slow. *Why not:* a lock freezes a pipe that landed tilted at its tilt, standing on one end, and a freeze places a body by script; the damping lets it tip flat under gravity and stop, and the engine puts it to sleep. It lies still within about a second and 5 s after the drop it is 0.87, 1.10 and 1.29 m from the player (hit from 1.0, 1.2 and 1.4 m), inside LB's 1.5 m; in the crowd room 0.63 m. A pipe that hits a wall in flight is damped too (it falls slower than free fall), which nobody sees.

**Bruiser**
- **The haymaker's 2 s armor break counts from the moment the Bruiser is back on its feet, and every hit does its ordinary reaction while it lasts.** *Alternative:* count from the hit, and let only light hits lose their absorption. *Why not:* the haymaker's knockdown (flight, down, get-up) takes about 1.75 s, so a window from the hit would be gone before the Bruiser stood, and with the heavy and finisher rules left on the uppercut would still stagger it and never launch it. A second haymaker restarts the window, and a killing hit never starts it.
- **A perfect block staggers a Bruiser.** *Alternative:* the boss's rule, an ordinary recoil with no stagger. *Why not:* a plain block only drains 60 of the guard's 100, with no recoil and no punish window, so the parry is the player's answer to the smash, and a staggered Bruiser can be grabbed.
- **An absorbed hit shows as a grey spark, a low clank and a grey ARMOR line in the combat log, with no flinch pose while the Bruiser winds up.** *Alternative:* a new spark and sound, or a "brace" flinch pose on every absorbed hit. *Why not:* the spark is the block spark tinted (0.55) and the clank is the block clack pitched to 0.6, one F1 number each, using the existing art; swapping a pose mid-wind-up would break the telegraph and read as the flinch armor is meant to remove. The brace plays only while the Bruiser stands with no move running.
- **Armor does not apply to a hit from the player who holds the Bruiser.** *Alternative:* leave it (a held Bruiser's knee is absorbed like any light hit). *Why not:* the knee was absorbed (a grey spark, no jolt), and the throw's THROWN became a STAGGER that left the Bruiser pinned to a player who had let go (final review, code-01). Now the knee jolts it and the throw throws it.

**Boss**
- **A parry gives the boss a 36-frame (0.6 s) recoil, and a plain block does nothing to it.** *Alternative:* no recoil (a parry only saves the damage), or a blocked recoil in the charge's data. *Why not:* the boss's moves never recoil when blocked (a data rule), so a parry would have rewarded nothing. A recoil on the charge's data would fire on every block, not just a parry. 36 frames fits a jab, a jab and a hook, and a parried charge stops dead mid-dash.
- **The boss throws a charge only from 2.5 m or more (the seed was 4.0 m), and the pound and the grab at any range, by chance (0.4, 0.3, 0.3); with two or more other enemies near the player it only pounds.** *Alternative:* the plan's three bands (charge far, pound close, grab between) with the 4.0 m minimum. *Why not:* the posture ring ends at 4.0 m and the boss walks to the ring before it asks for a token, so with 4.0 it threw no charge in four 60 s runs. At 2.5 it threw 19 charges, 21 pounds and 15 grabs over four seeds.
- **The boss's health stays 300 and its cadence 1.5 s (was 4.0; poise makes it attack less often), although the autopilot beats it in about 38 s (41-43 s since it wakes in place, below).** *Alternative:* raise its health or attack rate now. *Why not:* SPEC fixes 300, and how hard it should be is for the fairness review and Game-boy's play. The player took one charge (20) each time and parried another once. *Corrected at the final review:* the cooldown was not a knob then (the boss never reached POSTURE, which alone asks for a token); with the wake-in-place fix it is, and so is `boss_health` (fix-final-enemy-ai-ring-and-tokens.md, call 12).
- **The boss gets up where it stands after a reaction and attacks, for `posture_resume_s` (3 s), and the director serves it first.** *Alternative:* the base's rule for every archetype (each walks back to its slot), or a boss-only override of the after-reaction step. *Why not:* the Punk's go-back-to-the-slot is the baseline of the slice gate's numbers, so the base keeps it and the hook is one `return true`; a boss-only override would copy a branch of the control tick. Before this the autopilot's chain kept the boss in reaction, it walked back to its slot after every knockdown, never reached POSTURE and never attacked (final review, fair-01). Against the full autopilot it now starts 5 or 6 wind-ups and lands 2 attacks (2 to 4 and 1 before). The cost: it takes a grant ahead of waiting enemies in the blind-spot rule whenever it wakes (the blind-grant share, soft R9).
- **The phase 2 beckon plays out in full.** A taunt in progress finishes before the stray rule acts. Before, the call-in's first Punk changed the director's seats and the beckon was cut one frame after it began (final review, tells-05). *Alternative:* the boss ignores the stray rule while it taunts. *Why not:* the order was the whole bug, and a taunt a posturing Punk rolls for itself is protected the same way for free; the seat half of the fix is in the director's section.
- **The ground pound's strike is a squat (the hips 0.33 m down on the same deep legs, the spine near vertical, both fists driven down in front of the knees), not a fold over the knees.** The fold put the cap in the lens at 0.9 m (0.000 m), a range the pound is thrown from in ordinary play (his magnetism brings him to about 1.05 m of it); now the nearest boss mesh is 0.41 m at 0.9 m, 0.47 m at 1.1 m and 1.55 m at 2.2 m, and the follow-through's arms go out wide and low so a pound that misses does not bring the fists up through the lens (final review, tells-01). *Alternative:* exclude the pound under about 1.4 m in `_choose_attack()`, or have the boss step back before winding up. *Why not:* both change what the boss does where the pose only changes how it looks, and the pose clears every distance a player stands at. Timing, reach and the held wind-up key are unchanged.
- **The grab's slam strike is a heave that rears back: the torso arches back, the head goes back 0.46 m and both arms fly up and out on both sides of the view as he is let go.** Before, it folded forward like the Bruiser's headbutt (the boss's cap filling the held view, 0.12 m from the lens) and only the throw after told the 25-damage slam from the headbutt, whose head goes 0.31 m ahead (final review, tells-04). *Alternative:* a torso turn with the arms swinging him to one side, or a sideways throw. *Why not:* his pose is the player's own animation, so nothing can make it follow the boss's hands without touching the player's files, and a sideways throw moves where he lands, which the grab tests and the room's walls pin. The release, the 25 damage and the knockback are unchanged.

## The first-person view
- **The lens never sits lower than the floor under the player plus 0.13 m (`cam_floor_clearance_m`): a hard `max` against the floor.** A player lying face-down (struck from the side or behind) has his eye point 0.15 m up, which put the lens 4.4 cm inside the street for the whole 1.1 s of the knockdown, and the street is drawn from above only, so the floor vanished (final review, crowd-01). The clamp takes the lens from 4.5 cm under the street to 13 cm over it there (the street fills the lower half of the view) and lifts a face-up fall by 1.3-1.4 cm; it adds no pop of its own (the lens never moves more in a tick than it would without it). *Alternative:* a smooth knee with a blend width (a second tunable). *Why not:* it lifts the lens before the edge, so the face-up fall would move by more than 2 cm, and the max is already smooth. The belly-slab question (how much of the view the own body covers face-up) is not touched and stays Game-boy's.

## Readability measurement
- **"A 6-enemy fight is readable" is measured with 15 metrics: five hard and ten soft.** *Alternative:* judge from sheets alone. *Why not:* the hard ones (at most the cap attacking at once, wind-up starts at least 21 frames apart, no unwarned hit, the warning agreeing with the rule at every wind-up, no non-boss knockdown over 15 damage) are asserted headless and checked in every act and seed, and they pass in all of them. The soft ones are judged and argued in the gate file. The thresholds live in the probe and the tests, never in the tuning data.
- **The first-person telegraph check keeps "the head stays in view" but waives "the striking limb is in view" for the Kicker's crouch, the Thug's cocked pipe and the Bruiser's overhead fists at 1.0-1.2 m, and the boss's wind-ups are judged only from 2.5 m and 4.0 m (its pound's strike, which comes to the lens, is also judged from 0.9, 1.1 and 1.3 m since the final review).** *Alternative:* re-pose the wind-ups so the limb stays in view at 1 m. *Why not:* a raised knee reads as a front kick, not a leap; a lower pipe cock stops reading as "about to hit me"; and a 2.3 m boss's head is over the top of a lens that sees 29° up at 0.65 m. What leaves the top of the lens is what a raise looks like at arm's length, and the player decides from the ring.
- **Two soft misses are accepted: lens coverage (R8) and R12's 30 damage in 2 seconds.** *Alternative:* a wider posture ring for the heavies, or a shorter Thug combo. *Why not:* the ring is locked from the gate and R8 is soft. The Punks miss only marginally (51-57% at the 95th percentile, passing in 29 of 32 runs), and six Bruisers read 53% against 50%. The worst 2 s of damage is exactly 30 with an opening volley of fresh Punks and 36-48 with a Thug, whose three 10-damage combo is the spec's; the metric was written for grunts.
- **The mixed waves and the boss wave have acts of their own: "Mixed waves" (the spawner starts at wave 3, boss waves off, the held-back autopilot, 90 s) and "Boss wave, held back" (it starts at the boss wave; the boss is put at the phase 2 line by hand only if the autopilot has not got it there in 45 s).** "Waves to the boss" plays only the two opening Punk waves and the boss's arrival and call-in, and the boss is beaten before it attacks, so the waves' evidence had no mixed wave in it (final review, crowd-04). *Alternative:* the default `boss_wave_every` 4 with the spawner started at wave 3 (the boss at 70-90 s). *Why not:* the boss wave has its own act, and with the boss waves off the first act measures R13's window on mixed waves alone. Each act is recorded in its own process: acts of one run are not bit-identical to the same act run alone.

## Possible spec departures (for Game-boy's ruling)
Each is a place where the build reads SPEC.md differently from its text. The README's "Where M3 departs from the spec" section will carry the ones he rules on.
- **The haymaker knocks the Bruiser down and breaks its armor for 2 s, while its other finishers (the uppercut, the spinning backfist) only stagger it.** *Alternative:* the SPEC §7 text taken literally: the Bruiser staggers on heavy hits and the haymaker. *Why not:* SPEC §4 says the haymaker "breaks Bruiser armor", which needs a visible payoff, and a stagger would show none. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **Armor absorbs light hits instead of ignoring them: the damage lands but there is no flinch and the wind-up goes on.** *Alternative:* a light hit does nothing at all. *Why not:* the plan reads "ignores" as "no reaction", and a hit that did nothing would give "armor hits look registered" nothing to show; the grey spark and the log line mark each absorbed hit. This applies to the boss too. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The player can grab a Bruiser only while it is staggered or its armor is broken, and never the boss.** *Alternative:* M1's rule that LB grabs any standing enemy. *Why not:* LB would otherwise hold and throw a heavy at will, walking round its armor. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The 15% knockdown rule is read per attack chain, so the Pipe Thug's third swing staggers instead of knocking down.** *Alternative:* per hit only. *Why not:* the Thug's three 10-damage swings would end in a knockdown that costs 30% of the player's health, against the spec's intent. The Bruiser's headbutt and smash are 15 each with nothing before them, the boss is exempt, and a data test checks every chain. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **One telegraph per attack: a chain link (the Punk's cross, the Thug's swings 2 and 3) has no wind-up of its own, and starts only if the previous hit landed or was blocked.** *Alternative:* a wind-up and a warning for every hit. *Why not:* it gives a readable rhythm and one warning per attack, where a re-telegraph per hit doubles the warning noise in a crowd. A whiffed opener ends the attack, and a blocked Punk jab ends it too (its 20-frame recoil), which keeps the player's punish window. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The warning's side is fixed when the wind-up starts, and it is hidden while the player is KO'd.** *Alternative:* re-evaluate the side every frame as the player turns, and show it in the KO view. *Why not:* the spec fires it "at wind-up start", a fixed side is deterministic to test and does not flicker during spins, and turning to face it is the intended response. In the KO view the camera is outside the body, so the corners mean nothing; a wind-up still on when the player revives lights again. `rear_warning_follow_turn` turns the per-frame version on. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The 55° warning angle leaves a band at 46-55° off the look where an attacker is off-screen and gets no warning.** *Alternative:* 45°, to match the lens's ±45°, or measure the bearing from the lens. *Why not:* 55° is the spec's number (SPEC §8), and the slice gate's skeptics refuted the band as a defect and kept it; `rear_warning_angle_deg` is live in F1, so he can set 45. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **"Attack range + 1 m" is each move's own `reach_m`, which includes its lunge or dash.** *Alternative:* one range per archetype, or a fixed range. *Why not:* a boss charge (reach 5.3, so it warns out to 6.3 m) or a Kicker's jump kick (2.75, so 3.75 m) from behind would otherwise not warn. One number then feeds both the AI's spacing and the warning, is editable per move in F1, and a rig test holds it to the measured strike within 0.15 m. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **Grunt wind-ups run 20-22 frames by default (0.33-0.37 s), where SPEC §8 says 0.3 s for grunts.** *Alternative:* 18 frames, the spec's minimum. *Why not:* 22 frames with the hold from 80% of the wind-up reads at 1 m and 2.5 m in the first-person sheets; 18 stays as the floor that `windup_mult` clamps to. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **Enemy grabs on the player can be broken by mashing P or K: 8 presses before the follow-up strikes, a 60-frame window, and a break-free staggers the holder and gives the player 20 frames of invulnerability.** *Alternative:* no break-out, or a dodge-only way out of the held state. *Why not:* it is Game-boy's own rule (his M3 answers), and at `windup_mult` 0.25 the window is 30 frames, 8 presses in half a second. *Approved by Game-boy (M3 answers, 2026-10-25).*
- **While an enemy holds the player, his look turns to the holder over 8 frames and the view tips up toward the holder's eyes by up to 30°, with the look locked.** *Alternative:* leave the look to the player's input (SPEC §5: rotation only from look input), or a fixed 5° tilt. *Why not:* the plan locks the look so he sees the grabber. From straight behind it is a 135° turn in 0.13 s that reads as being yanked round, and a longer turn lets the grabber land the first moments of the hold out of view. A 2.05 m Bruiser's eyes are 0.37 m above the lens, so 5° shows only its chin. Both are tunables (`grabbed_turn_frames`, `grabbed_view_pitch_deg`). *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **Target magnetism in a crowd uses a flat cone (the heading only), skips bodies in get-up or blink-in invulnerability, and adds the target's radius to the stop distance.** *Alternative:* keep the 3-D angle and lift the aim to the target's chest height. *Why not:* the lunge is flat, and at arm's length a Bruiser's chest sits 8° off a level look in 3-D. The cost is that with the look pitched far up or down he still lunges at an enemy on the horizon of his heading. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The Pipe Thug never picks its pipe up again; after dropping it (on a knockdown, launch, throw or killing blow) it fights with the Punk's punches.** *Alternative:* it walks back and re-arms, or drops the pipe on a grab or any heavy hit. *Why not:* it keeps the AI simple and the pipe is the player's prize; a Thug he holds and lets go unhurt keeps its pipe. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The boss's name stays a placeholder, "Boss".** *Alternative:* invent a name and a design now. *Why not:* SPEC §13 leaves the name and design open and his M3 answers allowed a placeholder; the bar reads BOSS. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **Spawn-any in the F4 cheat menu can put more than six enemies alive; only waves respect the cap.** *Alternative:* F4 refuses past six. *Why not:* it is a debug tool for testing crowds. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **While the boss winds up a charge or a pound, a heavy hit is absorbed like a light one; only a heavy hit on its grab wind-up cuts it.** *Alternative:* the plain rule, where a heavy hit staggers it out of any wind-up. *Why not:* the autopilot, whose third punch is a heavy hook, beat the boss in 38 s without taking a hit, and all four of the boss's wind-ups in the run were cut. With this rule it is hit for 20 by a charge, parries one, still wins in 38 s, and the grab wind-ups are still cut. SPEC §7 only says light hits do not interrupt a boss wind-up. It is one place in `boss.gd` to take out. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The boss has poise: after it gets up from a stagger or knockdown, a heavy hit or a finisher (not the haymaker, not a kill) is absorbed for 4 s or until its next attack ends, and after 2 reactions in 8 s; a heavy hit still cuts its grab, and it steps back before a too-close charge or pound.** *Alternative:* wake in place only (the final review's fix), or more health. *Why not:* waking in place alone left the boss knocked down every 3.3 s by the basic chain, 53-56% of its life in reactions and 2 hits landed. With poise: 21% in reactions, 4.6 hits a fight, killed in about 39 s, and the autopilot still wins 12 of 12. Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off; `boss_health` 500 gives about 6 hits and a 56 s fight (the spec says 300). Details: M3-calls/F-boss-poise.md.
- **A KO'd enemy lies limp, blinks off and its body is freed, with no pooling.** *Alternative:* bodies stay until the arena clears (SPEC §6), or recycled bodies. *Why not:* M2 already approved the blink-off, and freeing is simpler; one-at-a-time spawns spread the 10 ms rig builds, and a pool can come if the performance review asks. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*
- **The crowd mostly waits and attacks in turns: at most 1-2 attack at once against a cap of 3, and about a quarter of a six-enemy crowd circles the ring at any moment (1.4-1.6 of 5.8) while most of the rest stand squared up on it waiting for their go.** *Alternative:* a shorter Punk cooldown, or letting a wind-up start inside the hit grace so more attacks overlap. *Why not:* SPEC §7 says "most of their time posturing", and 5.0 s is the best posture share that does not gut the pressure (10 s gave 2.6-2.9 of six at 16-17 hits in 22 s). With a player who takes every hit the hit grace holds the next wind-up, so R1 is 1 (it was 2 before the holders waited on the ring) and the cap of 3 never binds; a hit lands about every 1.3 s either way. The gate locked the gap, grace and cadence. *Decided by default, for Game-boy's ruling at sign-off.*

# M4 judgement calls

The lead's calls, kept as the work goes, for the final DM's digest. Each one gives the decision, the alternative not taken, and why. The builders' own calls are in `docs/milestones/M4-calls/<package>.md`, and the slice gate's locks are in `docs/milestones/M4-slice-gate.md`.

1. **Phase 1 ran on concept A as a stand-in while the pick was out.** *Alternative:* wait for the pick. *Why:* the slice proves the chain, not the design. The real Punk replaces `punk.glb` in place (same path, same `punk_art.tres`), so nothing built on the stand-in is thrown away.
2. **Tripo's own weights and the A-pose bind route are locked.** *Alternative:* Blender's heat-map weights, or the arms-down bake. *Why:* the four variants can't be told apart at 1 m in first person, and their strain numbers differ by under 15% and split both ways. Game-boy's order was Tripo first. The other three stay behind `look_punk_art_variant`.
3. **The art keeps its bulk, and the hidden proxies keep M3's shapes.** *Alternative:* widen the proxies to the art, or slim the art to the proxies. *Why:* readability measured with the art drawn agrees with the proxies within M3's spread, so the crowd numbers stand. Widening would move every first-person number M3 tuned; slimming would lose the concept's chunk. Art-drawn guards were added only for the close-range distance rules.
4. **4x MSAA stays the default, with FXAA as a switch** (`look_aa_mode` 1). *Alternative:* FXAA by default, which is 2.3 ms faster per frame on the M1. *Why:* MSAA is the spec's reading and keeps the 1-2 px interior lines crisper. FXAA looks clean at 1 m too, so it's offered with its numbers.
5. **Placeholder outlines are on** (A7). *Alternative:* off. *Why:* no measurable cost, and it is the only setting where the placeholders and the Punk share an ink weight.
6. **The outline-normal bug was fixed in the exporter, not the shader.** *Alternative:* have the shader read the swapped axes. *Why:* the normals stay a Y-up file attribute like the mesh's own, so every later model (the real Punk, the player) is right by construction, and a contract test now guards it.
7. **The idle's life is held under A20's 1 cm hit-area limit** (a 5 mm bounce, not the plan's ±1 cm). *Alternative:* the ±1 cm bounce (`look_motion_bounce_m` reaches it). *Why:* A20 binds the hit areas so M3's tuned reach and readability don't drift. The visible life is in the gloves, shoulders and head, which have no hit area. Livelier is a dial, not a rebuild.
8. **Enemy walks are stride-matched, so their feet plant.** *Alternative:* keep M3's 0.6 s walk cycle, where the feet slid up to 0.9 m a step. *Why:* sliding feet read as fake at 1 m. The cost is a brisker step rate at the approach pace (`look_motion_stride_m` sets it).
9. **Motion polish and the pick-independent end work started before the pick.** *Alternative:* idle until the pick. *Why:* the plan's rule for a late pick, and none of it depends on the design.
10. **The slice gate used one Sonnet check per finding, and the lead chose the fixes after reading them.** *Alternative:* M3's three checks per finding with automatic fixes. *Why:* A15 asks for one, and choosing by hand kept the gate from "fixing" the stand-in's design, which F-punk replaces anyway.
11. **Perf was measured under load from other apps, with the builds interleaved run by run.** *Alternative:* wait for a quiet machine. *Why:* the interleaving makes the M3-to-M4 deltas fair, which is what the gate needs. The quiet-machine run comes at P3-int.
12. **Skin got its own damage marks** (a third palette class). *Alternative:* leave skin taking the clothes' scuffs, which turned faces grey and glassy. *Why:* M3's bruised-then-bloodied skin is what the damage looks were tuned on, and every later character inherits the class.
13. **The tonemapper is linear, not M3's filmic** (`look_tonemap`; 1 restores filmic). *Alternative:* keep filmic. *Why:* filmic turned the skin lilac and lifted the ink 10-16 levels off the HUD's. Linear keeps every swatch within a degree of its hue and the 3D ink within 2 levels of the HUD's. The comic effects now show in their authored colours, which the final review re-checks.
14. **Characters get a camera-fixed fill light, besides the sun** (`look_fill_strength`; 0 turns it off). *Alternative:* the sun alone, at any angle. *Why:* about half the enemies face away from the sun whatever its angle, and their fronts fell into one shadow band. The fill is the stronger of the two terms, so a lit side never gets brighter. The street keeps the sun alone.
15. **The F4 showcase Punk can't be hit.** *Alternative:* a live Punk that reacts to hits. *Why:* a hit would break the loop that lets you walk round it. The crowd Punks are the ones to hit.
16. **The four deferred feel questions are toggles that default to today**, measured with the art Punk in first person, for Game-boy's ruling in F1:
    - (1) `hit_flash_fp_max_share` (0 = off). A close flash washes out about 21% of the view: the hold's knee three times a hold, the flurry at 8.6 Hz. At the suggested 0.12 each hit becomes a glow on the struck spot (at most about 7%). *Cost:* an ordinary jab's flash shrinks by about 40%. No single value spares the jab and calms the flurry, because the cap works by distance.
    - (2) `hit_flash_finisher_radius_mult` (1.0 = off). Today the haymaker's flash barely shows (0.3% of the view). At the suggested 2.0 it whites the arm and shoulder, about a jab's size. At 3.0 it is too much.
    - (3) `fp_knockdown_hide_torso` (off). The knockdown's belly slab drops from 74% to 15% of the view on the primitives. On art the whole visible body hides unless the hero's model has a separate belly mesh.
    - (4) the L key toggles the combat log, and `combat_log_hide_while_holding` (off) hides it during a hold.

    *Alternative:* ship the suggested values on. *Why:* they are feel calls on M2's tuned numbers, and the plan has him judge them with the art in.
17. **The player's art seam merged before the pick, without changing the game.** *Alternative:* keep F-player-int unmerged until the hero model exists. *Why:* the wiring, the first-person layers and the lens rules are proven on the probe mannequin now, so the hero model drops in with no code edit, and the game keeps M3's arms until it does.
18. **A fix round after the first art, chosen from the lead's own look at the first-person captures beside the concepts.** *Alternative:* go straight to the six-lens review. *Why:* four faults were plain at 1 m and are cheap to fix before the review spends its effort on them. The Punk's yellow gauntlet cuffs came from our own brief (it asked for "an accent strap"), not from concept A, whose gloves and cuffs are dark. The cap and the shades share one yellow, so the head reads as one mass. The face has speckle and the vest has blotches. The hero's jab shows a bare elbow stump, and his fingers are lumps.
19. **The hero's jab keeps M3's framing, with the cut end rounded like M3's elbow capsule.** *Alternative:* hide the whole upper arm in first person. *Why:* M3 kept the elbow end on purpose, so a punch comes in from the edge of the view as an arm rather than a forearm floating in mid-view, and the probe mannequin frames the same way. What made the hero's arm read as a stump was the flat cut cap with its fan of ink lines.
20. **The hero's A9 against FPView's proxies is a ratchet at his own figures, printing the plan's bars**, as the probe and the Punk already are. *Alternative:* fit the hero to the proxies until the plan's bars pass, or loosen the bars. *Why:* the gate's lock 3 keeps the art's shape and keeps the proxies as M3 tuned them. Against the proxies' own meshes his strikes are within 2.4 cm. FPView's proxies are looser than those meshes, and even the primitive-shaped probe misses the bars against them.
21. **The hero's limbs are clamped to the proxies' girth** (the builder's call, kept). *Alternative:* keep the generated bulk, as the gate locked for the Punk. *Why:* the lead arm passes 20 cm from the lens, and a bulkier arm fills more of the view and moves M3's tuned first-person numbers. Deleting `parts.fists.girth` in `assets/source/characters/player/pipeline.json` and re-running gives the bulk back.
22. **The Punk's glove cuff follows the concept, not M3's primitive glove** (package F-cuff). *Alternative:* keep the wide flat flange the fix round left, which meets A9's 4-point lens-share bar. *Why:* the bar compares the art with the proxies' fist box, which is bulkier than the concept's glove. The flange made up that bulk, and it read as a plunger base at 1 m. If the concept's wrist band misses 4 points in some strike, that pose's share becomes a ratchet that prints the plan's bar, as A9's cells already are.
23. **The final review ran while F-cuff reshaped the cuff**, with V1 (the Punk standing at 1 m) held until the cuff merged. *Alternative:* wait for F-cuff, then review all six lenses. *Why:* five of the six lenses don't depend on the cuff, and running them now saves about an hour and a half.
24. **The Punk's stagger_back is re-posed with bent elbows**, a departure from A20's list of frozen clips. *Alternative:* keep M3's pose, as the review's skeptic argued (no rubric row names it, and A20 freezes it). *Why:* after the hold's third knee, on a grab release and in the long take, the skinned Punk throws both arms out nearly straight and level for about 0.3 s. On the art that reads as the rig's bind pose, not a body knocked back. M3 already fixed the same fault in stagger_right. It's a reaction, not an active frame, so no reach or readability number is involved.
25. **Two review items go to Game-boy instead of the fixers.** *Alternative:* fix them now. *Why:* both are taste calls on approved designs. (1) The damage looks are whole-body stamps, and the blood turns orange on the yellow cap. They match SPEC §6, and per-swatch strength is a new feature. (2) The hero's cut end is a bare knob in the haymaker and uppercut. It is M3's approved framing (call 19), and showing the whole upper arm reverses that.

# M4 calls: F-punch-turn

Package F-punch-turn (M4, after the build; the lead's note under Game-boy's playtest of 2026-11-02): the player's straight punches turn the fist over. Each call is the decision, an alternative not taken, and why. Part 2 is what the numbers say, part 3 what the first-person stills show. Base: `m4/art-pipeline` at 53279bc (the suite was 1600 of 1600 at 72cc4eb). No Tripo, Gemini or Blender; no tunable; the machine was shared (load 3 to 8), so time figures are noisy and the pose figures are exact (the clips are built, not timed).

## 1. Calls

**Call 28 (Game-boy's own request, copied to `M4-judgement-calls.md` by the lead).** His words, 2026-11-02: "Another fix to hand positioning: this time for the player-character. When the player throws a straight punch, the fist should turn 90 degrees so that the curled fingers angle down toward the ground (rather than inward toward each other)." A departure from A20's frozen active-frame poses that he asked for, not a SPEC change. It re-poses `jab_1` and `jab_2` for the player; no other move or named clip changes.

**C1. Measured first (step 1).** At today's strike key (`FK` of `PlaceholderBody.pose_globals`, rig frame: x right, y up, -z forward; the player's relaxed stance, the boxer's is the same at these keys):

At contact the fist stands on its edge, thumb up, the palm toward the midline (+x for the left hand, -x for the right), exactly what Game-boy sees: the bare fingers curl inward at the other hand. The hand bones carry no rotation in either pose (the hand is the stance's); every bit of that orientation is the arm chain's. The arm geometry at the strike (shoulder, elbow, wrist, fist): the left upper arm is level (0.1 degrees), the forearm rises **33.6 degrees**, the shoulder-to-fist chord rises 18.6; the right is -3.0, **35.9** and 18.4. **Which way pronation turns each hand:** the left hand's roll about the forearm axis is negative (hand Euler y -90 turns it palm down), the right's positive (+90). They are mirror images (`PlaceholderPoses.mirror()` negates y and z), so the mirrored clip of either move is palm down as well (tested).

**C2. Where the "curled fingers" are, measured from the art, not assumed.** The hero's fist is T13's generated piece: its bare fingers (the skin faces, shared palette slot 7) sit **+22.8 mm toward the midline** of the fist's centre for the left hand and +22.7 for the right (+16 mm toward the knuckles, 0 toward the front), the glove plate (slot 35) and the wrap (slot 14) the other way. The same measure F-fist-turn uses for the Punk (`test_art_contract`), taken on the hero's `ArtBody` skinned at the table's rest. So the curl direction in the pose maths is the palm side, +x left and -x right, and `test_punch_turn.gd` derives it from the mesh each run. *Alternative:* assume it from A5's text. *Why not:* an assumption is what put the Punk's fists the wrong way round.

**C3. The turn is the player's only: the Street Punk's cross and the sparring dummy's jab are the M3 clips.** `EnemyPosesPunk._cross` calls `PlaceholderPoses._jab_1(g)` and `_dummy_jab` (which `EnemyPosesPunk._jab` calls) calls `_jab_2(g)`, so editing the two functions' bodies would have turned both. `_jab_1` and `_jab_2` take `turn_fist: bool = false` and `_recipe()` passes `true` for the player's `jab_1` and `jab_2`. Hashes: only the eight `jab_1`/`jab_2` entries moved; `punk_cross`, `punk_jab`, `dummy_jab` and every `punk_*` hash are the M3 ones (tested twice: the hash table, and `test_the_punks_cross_and_the_dummys_jab_are_not_turned`). *Alternative:* turn them all (the lead's brief says "`_dummy_jab` reuses it" and "the training dummy's jab still reads"). *Why not:* Game-boy asked for the player-character; A20 freezes every enemy clip and the Punk's hashes, strain ratchets, A9 jab and cross ratchets (exact at 5.0 points) and F-cuff's numbers all sit on those poses; F-fist-turn is changing the Punk's hands at the same time and a shared edit would have made the two packages meet in the same functions and fixtures. It is one argument to change (`turn_fist` true in `EnemyPosesPunk` and the dummy): the lead may flip it if he wants the Punk to corkscrew too, with the Punk's hashes and strain table regenerated. Reported under departures.

**C4. How far down the palm can go: 33.7 and 36.0 degrees, not 20 (the lead's "about 20").** With a straight wrist the palm normal is perpendicular to the hand axis, so it cannot be nearer straight down than the forearm's rise: 33.6 degrees in the jab, 35.9 in the cross (a low elbow and a head-high target). Rolled 90 degrees both land **33.7 and 36.0 degrees from straight down, 1.8 and 3.2 from the lowest the forearm allows**, the curled fingers' direction y = -0.83 / -0.81 (down, and a little forward). To reach 20 the fist would need a wrist flexion of 14 degrees, which moves the fist point 1.6 cm (the hits allow 1), or an elbow swivel of about 80 degrees (a flared elbow, the upper arm out to the side, a different punch), or an IK re-solve of the whole arm. *Alternatives:* (a) a wrist flexion, compensated by moving the elbow and upper arm: the numbers do not reach 20 without a drooped wrist and re-tuning M3's arm; (b) the swivel: no. *Why not:* "palm down" in the sense Game-boy means (the fingers under the fist, the back of the hand up) is met; the lead's 20 assumed a level forearm. The test holds two bars that say what is true: the palm is within 20 degrees of the lowest the forearm allows (`ROLL_ERROR_MAX_DEG`) and within 40 of straight down (`PALM_DOWN_MAX_DEG`; M3's was 87-89). Reported under departures.

**C5. The roll is about the forearm's own axis, so the hit is where it was.** The fist point sits 5 mm off that axis (`POINT_OFFSET` z -0.005), so a 90 degree roll moves it 7.1 mm: at the wind-up and strike keys of both moves and both stances the worst is **7.1 mm** (A20's bar is 10). *Alternative:* none worth taking: the offset is the table's.

**C6. The roll is split lower arm 27 degrees, hand 63 (the strike's), as a real forearm's roll is spread.** A skinned wrist blends lower arm to hand over about 9 cm (the hero's weights: lower arm 1.00 to 0.97 at 9 cm from the wrist, 0.83 at 6, 0.57 at 3, hand 1.00 at the wrist and beyond), and a twist between two binds pinches by `cos(half the twist)`. Measured on the hero's skinned forearm and hand (the mean distance of the skin from the forearm's axis, over its rest value, in 3 cm bands; 1.0 keeps the width), the narrowest band at the strike frame for the left jab, share of the 90 degrees on the lower arm:

| Share | 0 (all in the hand) | 0.25 | 0.3 | 0.35 | 0.4 | 0.45 | 0.5 | 0.75 | M3's pose (no roll) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Narrowest band | 0.777 | 0.870 | 0.886 | 0.901 | 0.915 | 0.909 | 0.896 | 0.813 | 0.959 |

The best split balances the wrist (hand against lower arm) with the elbow (lower arm against upper arm), at about 0.4; I took 0.3 (27 degrees), **0.885** at contact, because of C7. The whole roll in the hand pinches the wrist to 0.78 (22% narrower at the strap); the right cross gave the same figures within 0.003. The edges stretch nowhere (an axial twist keeps edge lengths) and the whole-mesh A19 strain does not move at all (C10). *Alternative:* all in the hand (a clean Euler y, no lower-arm change). *Why not:* the pinch, which the lead named. *Alternative:* the upper arm takes a share too. *Why not:* its roll swings the forearm out of its place (the fist point would move by centimetres), and the shoulder is already the weak joint (`F-punk`, `F-player-art` C15).

**C7. The lower arm's roll is nearly the same at the wind-up and the strike (15 and 27 degrees), the hand's is what turns over between them.** Three tries. (1) The roll as the principal Euler triple (x in -90..90) with 9 degrees at the wind-up and 36 at the strike: the wind-up's 112 degree bend reads back as x 68 with y and z near 180, `PlaceholderAnims._subdivide()` sees a jump over `MAX_STEP_DEG` (90) and inserts Euler-lerped in-betweens (7 keys against M3's 5), and the wrist ran 100 mm off M3's path at the frame between the keys. (2) `_rolled()` reads the triple back as the equivalent one nearest the pose's own (x stays 112 to 131), which keeps the wind-up's representation. A lower arm rolled only at the strike (0 then 18, 27 or 36 degrees) then slerps from the wind-up's bend to the strike's roll-and-bend and wanders: 23, 32 and 42 mm off M3's path at the frame between the keys. Two keys that differ by a bend alone slerp as a bend, so the roll should be the same at both; but the lower arm's Euler x grows with the roll (112 to 131 at 36 degrees), the step from wind-up to strike passes 90 degrees and `_subdivide()` fires again (36 and 36: 7 keys, 64 mm off at frame 1). (3) Scanned (lower-arm roll at the wind-up and at the strike, left jab; keys against M3's 5; the fist point's distance from M3's at frames 1 and 3, mm):

| Wind-up / strike | Keys | Frame 1 | Frame 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 / 27 | 5 | 1 | 32 |
| 9 / 27 | 5 | 8 | 23 |
| 18 / 27 | 5 | 17 | 14 |
| 18 / 36 | 6 (a sub-key) | 17 | 32 |
| 36 / 36 | 7 (sub-keys) | 64 | 40 |

I took **15 / 27** (and the hand 7.5 / 63): the keys are M3's, the fist point is at most **16.8 mm** from M3's at any frame of either clip (jab frame 3; frame 1 is the guard to the chamber, which the lens does not see) and 7.1 at the keys. The pinch bar at contact is therefore 0.885 rather than the 0.915 of the 36 degree split; that is 3 points of width at a strap, and a smooth path is worth more. *Alternative:* an extra key (`MovePoses.windup_hold`) through the mid-frame: it holds every other bone at the wind-up until then and changes the whole jab's motion. *Alternative:* a change to `_subdivide()`'s step: not my file, and every clip would change.

**C8. The wind-up is turned 22.5 degrees, not 0.** The lead's bar is 30. With 0 the whole 90 degree turn happens in the last two frames of the startup (33 ms); a quarter of it at the wind-up spreads the roll over four frames (0, 22.5, 90 from the guard, the chamber, the strike) with the fast part still the last two. The chamber is at the chin and is mostly out of the lens's view. The hand counter-rolls (7.5 degrees at the wind-up) to make up the difference between the lower arm's 15 and the 22.5. *Alternative:* 0 at the wind-up (cleaner, a harder snap). *Why not:* the fist flips visibly over two frames; the lead allows 30. The roll comes back out over the recovery (10 frames, the strike to the stance's hand at zero, tested: the clip ends in the stance's arm).

**C9. No tunable, and no runtime switch.** The poses are art data like every pose in `placeholder_poses.gd` (its header says so) and static: they cannot read `Tuning`, and the four turn values are named constants beside `_jab_1`. The spec value (M3's pose) is `turn_fist = false`, one argument in `_recipe()`; the hash table's `notes` record the M3 hashes it replaced. *Alternative:* a Debug A/B tunable read when the library is built (as `look_motion_polish`). *Why not:* it would make the pose recipes depend on an autoload and the hash tool run as a scene only, for a change Game-boy asked for by name; adding it later is small.

**C11. Ragdoll.** The lower arm's elbow is a hinge in `PlaceholderBody.ragdoll_specs()` (0 to 150 about X, no twist) and the hand has no body. A partial or full ragdoll starting while the lower arm holds its 27 degree roll (the player hit during the strike's active frames, or knocked out in the punch) asks the hinge to untwist it: the forearm's body is a cylinder, so only the hand's carried orientation would turn, over a few frames, in a pose the player is leaving anyway. `test_ragdoll`'s joint-limit scenarios start from the dummies' guards and are unchanged and green. I did not stage a hit during the punch (an open risk, below). *Alternative:* the whole roll in the hand (no hinge issue). *Why not:* C6.

**C12. The Euler triple.** `_rolled()` composes the pose's own Euler basis with a roll about the bone's y and reads it back in the order `PlaceholderBody.euler_basis()` reads (YXZ), picking the triple nearest the original, because a roll that follows a bend is not a lone y angle. The numbers in the poses are therefore computed (Euler angles for the jab's lower arm at contact: (26.5, -41.1, -21.3) at 36 degrees, (29.5, -31.4, -16.7) at 27), not typed; the constants beside them are the art data.

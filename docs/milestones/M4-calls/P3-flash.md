# M4 calls: P3-flash

Package P3-flash: the part of P3-int (M4-plan Part C section 8, as amended by A23) that is feel questions 1 and 2 (the contact flash in first person), F-motion's warm-up request for the Bruiser's and the boss's named libraries, and a look at the comic VFX under F-look's linear tonemapper. Each alternative is a toggle that defaults to today, so with no value touched the game is as it was. Each call is the decision, an alternative not taken, and why. Part 2 lists every assertion that changed (none), part 3 the numbers (all first person, the art Punk, 1280x720 recordings through `tools/capture_bot.sh`, deterministic: a rerun printed the same figures), part 4 what the sheets show, part 5 the requests, departures and risks. The machine was shared (load 3 to 5), so the only wall-clock figures are the warm-up's, and they are far outside the noise.

## 1. Calls

**C1. The cap is a stretch across the line of sight, not a smaller radius.**
- *Decision.* `hit_flash_fp_max_share` (Look > FP feel, 0 = off) is handed to `hit_flash.gdshader` as `flash_max_share`. With it on, the shader measures each fragment's distance from the flash's point in view space, the offset across the line of sight to the point stretched by 1 / k and the offset along it as it is, and flashes what is inside the radius by the usual falloff. k is how much the flash's reach across that line at the fragment's own offset along it (`sqrt(radius^2 - along^2)`) has to shrink on screen to cover at most the share of the view on a surface at the fragment's depth (`limit * depth / (reach * proj11)`, `limit` the half-heights of the disc that covers the share), and 1 when it already fits, so **a flash that fits is not touched, fragment for fragment** (tested). The camera that draws the pass supplies the projection (`abs(PROJECTION_MATRIX[1][1])`) and the viewport; the simulation reads nothing and `CombatActor._flash_at()` and its point are untouched. The GDScript mirrors are `HumanoidRig.flash_cap_scale()` and `flash_cap_distance()` (like `ToonMaterials.outline_width_m()`), `flash_view_share()` gives the disc's share for reports.
- *Alternatives.* (a) The brief's reading, scaling the world radius by the camera distance (`min(R, limit * depth / proj11)`): built and measured first. On the hold's knee it made the flash vanish: the contact point lies behind the surface the lens sees (a knee under a cap's brim), so a sphere that reaches the visible cap only because it is 0.25 m wide has nothing left to reach once it is 0.1 m wide. At a cap of 10% the knee changed 0.16% of the view (20.8% today). (b) A window in screen space around the point's projection, multiplying the alpha: it either squares the flash's own profile (changing flashes that already fit) or ends in a hard disc edge. (c) A hard cap on the alpha or the strength: that is the photosensitivity softening of hit-01's option (a), a different feel (a dimmer flash, the same patch).
- *Why this.* It limits what the lens sees (the disc around the point's screen position, area at most the share), leaves a flash that fits exactly as it was, keeps the flash's shape, and still flashes what is in front of a hidden point. Where the point is off the view axis the disc is at most `1 / cos(angle)` wider than the share says, across the line of sight to it (tested: the bound is that plus the grid's step); on the axis it is exact.
- *A finding that cost an hour.* In a fragment function `PROJECTION_MATRIX[1][1]` is **negative** when the pass draws into a viewport (the flipped target), so the first shader read it as 0 and capped nothing (`toon_outline.gdshader` already says `abs()`). The scratch scene that showed it printed the matrix as a color.

**C2. What the number means: the share the flash's disc may cover, and what it changes on a body is a third to a half of that.**
- *Decision.* The tunable's unit is the share of the view the flash's disc (radius `hit_flash_radius_m`, around the point's screen position) may cover. A disc is partly off the body (the cap, the hair, the gap behind), and the flash only paints the body, so at close range the body changes about 30 to 50% of it (part 3). The tunable's comment says so.
- *Alternative.* A cap on the visible patch itself (the share of pixels changed).
- *Why not.* The patch is not known in the shader that draws it: it needs the frame the pass is part of. The disc is a function of the point and the camera alone.

**C3. A finisher's flash is widened from `CombatEvents.hit_landed`, by a static listener in `HumanoidRig`.**
- *Decision.* `hit_flash_finisher_radius_mult` (Look > FP feel, 1 = off): `HumanoidRig` connects once (`_watch_finishers()`, on the first flash) to `hit_landed`; a clean finisher (`hit.tier == FINISHER`, not blocked) multiplies the struck rig's aimed overlay radius (`_widen_flash()`), once per flash. `receive_hit()` aims the flash and `hit_landed` follows in the same tick (`test_hit_flash` leans on that order), so the radius is scaled before the flash is drawn, hidden or not (a finisher's flash waits out the impact frame).
- *Alternatives.* (a) Tell the rig from `CombatActor._flash_at()` (a tier argument): not mine, and "the sim's `CombatActor.flash_at` and its point stay untouched". (b) Infer a finisher in the rig from `shown == false` (the impact frame's delay): only with the impact frame on. (c) Widen in the shader from a uniform the actor sets: the same actor change.
- *Why this.* It is presentation reading an event, in the rig's own flash code, and works with the impact frame on or off (tested through a real P P P P chain). The request for a plain `tier` argument is in part 5 for the day someone owns `combat_actor.gd`.

**C4. Blocked finishers and guard breaks keep the ordinary size.** A blocked hit flashes at `BLOCK_FLASH_AMOUNT` of the strength, a guard break is a blocked hit: widening them would make a block louder than a clean hit. Same rule as `impact_frame_ticks()`. (Alternative: every finisher-tier hit. Not taken: the brief says clean finishers, "applied to finishers only", and a block is not one.)

**C5. The widening grows the cap's share with it (mult squared), so the toggles compose.**
- *Decision.* `_widen_flash()` also multiplies the overlay's `flash_max_share` by `mult * mult` (at most 1): with both on, a finisher's flash is `mult` times an ordinary one across wherever the cap bites.
- *Alternative.* The cap simply wins (it caps every flash by the same share).
- *Why not.* Measured: haymaker, mult 2 alone changes 7.3% of the view (today 0.27%), mult 2 with a cap of 6% and the cap winning changed 0.44% (the cap shrank the widened disc, 33% of the view at 1 m, to 6%), which undoes the widening and makes the two toggles cancel. With the share growing too the same pair is 4.4% (cap 6%) and 7.4% (cap 12%, whose finisher share is 48% so a finisher at 1 m is not capped). Off, the cap's share is 0 and stays 0.

**C6. The tunables, and where.** Both are `@export_range` in Look > FP feel after `combat_log_hide_while_holding`: `hit_flash_fp_max_share` 0 to 0.5 step 0.005, default 0; `hit_flash_finisher_radius_mult` 1 to 4 step 0.05, default 1. Their names begin `hit_flash_`, not `vfx_`, so A9's VFX-group rule does not apply; the brief puts them in FP feel (the sibling of `fp_knockdown_hide_torso`). They are in presets (Look is saved, only Debug is skipped) and `tuning/default.json` lists them at the defaults (516 values). The rig reads `hit_flash_fp_max_share` when a flash is aimed (a flash lasts two frames), so a slider move reaches the next flash.

**C7. The warm-up: `RoomKit.warm_up_libraries()`, called by `warm_up_art()` windowed only.**
- *Decision.* `warm_up_art()` returns early headless as before, then calls `warm_up_libraries()`: for each of `RoomKit.WARM_LIBRARY_ENEMIES` (`&"bruiser"`, `&"boss"`) it reads `EnemyCatalog.body_size(id).y / PlaceholderBody.REF_HEIGHT` (the rig scale `CombatActor._ensure_body()` gives it, 1.1389 and 1.2778) and calls `HumanoidRig._named_library(scale, PlaceholderPoses.STANCE_BOXER)`, so the cache holds the key before the first spawn. The Punk's library is built by the throwaway rig as before. The function is separate so a test can call it headless.
- *Alternative.* A public `HumanoidRig.warm_library()` wrapper.
- *Why not.* `humanoid_rig.gd` is mine only for the flash; the request names `_named_library` and a GDScript call to it from another class is the whole of the change.
- *Measured* (a windowed run of the real crowd room, `Time.get_ticks_usec()` around `bot.spawn()`, three repeats): the first Bruiser spawn after the load 3.7 ms, the first boss 3.1 ms; with the cache emptied first (what a spawn paid before): 29.7 to 30.9 ms for the Bruiser and 29.5 to 31.2 for the boss, 2.7 to 3.6 ms for the spawn after. So the warm-up takes about 27 ms off each first spawn (F-motion estimated 17), once per run, at the room's load (two builds, about 60 ms in a frame that already builds the art's pipelines).

**C8. How the flash is measured.** The acts grab the frame the flash is drawn in, set the overlay's strength to 0 and call `RenderingServer.force_draw(false)` (no tick runs, and the recording takes no extra frame: the marks and the video line up), grab again, and restore the strength, so the two frames differ by the flash alone. `white` is T5's figure (white pixels in the frame, `LookProbe.white_share`, WHITE_LEVEL 0.92, the L3 measure); `pure` the pixels the flash made white; `any` the pixels it changed (`LookProbe.CHANGED_DELTA`), its whole visible patch. M2's "16% of the view" for the knee is the `any` figure (20.8% here), not the white one (2.9%): a flash that is white only in its core is a wash over much more.
- *Alternative.* The white share against a baseline frame two ticks later (the first version). It reported 2.9% for the knee at every cap, because the white it counted was not what the cap changed.

## 4. What the sheets show (first person, `captures/p3flash/ff1`, `ff2`, `ff3`, `vfx_linear`, `vfx_filmic`; never committed)

- **The hold's knee** (`ff2`, today, cap 0.06, cap 0.12 side by side). Today the knee washes the right half of the Punk's cap and the hair white, a gradient that is as large as the burst and the lettering together; at 0.06 it is a small bright glow at the contact, beside the burst, readable and quiet; at 0.12 the glow is a little bigger and rims the hair. Both read as the same flash made smaller and still on the spot it struck. At 0.04 it is a pinprick.
- **The flurry** (`ff1`, today, 0.08, 0.12). Today a flurry blows the Punk's chest and both gloves out to white, seven times; at 0.08 and 0.12 each hit is a glow on the chest under the burst. The hits no longer strobe a patch; they pulse a spot (the 8.6 Hz is the same). At 0.12 the glow reads as a flash and nothing more.
- **The jab.** The cost of the cap: at 0.12 the jab's flash is 60% of today's, at 0.06 under a third. Nothing in a jab complained.
- **The haymaker and the uppercut** (`ff3`, today, x2, x3). Today neither shows a flash: the burst and the lettering sit on the Punk and the Punk looks as if it were not hit; at x2 the raised arm and shoulder go white, a wash about the size of a jab's; at x3 the arm and the sleeve are white out to the edge of the view, too much (19%). x2 is the value that reads as a finisher without being a screen.
- **The VFX under the linear tonemapper** (`vfx_linear`, `vfx_filmic`: the same acts with `--tune=look_tonemap=1`; the first-person P P P P to the finisher with the impact frame, the haymaker, the block, parry and guard-break sparks, the tier sizes). The sprites are as authored: the lettering's yellow reads (255, 215, 89) under linear, (255, 234, 110) under filmic; a burst's orange (254, 207, 53) against (254, 230, 58); blood (215, 51, 60) against (227, 63, 35). So linear is deeper and a little more saturated than M2's filmic look, which lifted and paled every sprite by 15 to 25 levels on blue and green; it is the authored value, not brighter than it (the yellow's red channel is 255 in both). The cyan parry spark reads cyan, and the impact frame and the speed lines (canvas layers) are not tonemapped at all, so are identical. Against the muted street and the grade it is a better pop. **I changed nothing:** no VFX tunable is needed, none clearly was.

## 5. Requests, departures, risks

- **Requests.** (1) A tier argument on `CombatActor._flash_at()` / `HumanoidRig.flash_at()` (`finisher: bool`), if the owner of `combat_actor.gd` wants the widening without the listener; the listener then goes. (2) P3-int proper: `Tuning.hit_flash_fp_max_share` and `hit_flash_finisher_radius_mult` are the F1 sliders for the final DM's A/B stills; the three sheets above are the pairs (hold, flurry, haymaker). (3) The lead: both tunables are new preset values; a preset saved before this branch loads them at their defaults.
- **Departures.** None: both alternatives default to today, and the spec's flash is the default.
- **Open risks.** (1) The cap is exact on the view axis and at most `1 / cos(angle)` wider off it (a point 45 degrees off the axis, the edge of the view, up to 1.4x across); the contacts the first-person view sees are within 15 degrees. (2) The cap and its stretch run for every camera that draws the pass; the side view and F3 are far from the body, where a flash fits, so they are as they were (not measured). (3) The suggested values are one reading of a feel question Game-boy can adjust on the F1 sliders: 0.12 for the cap (a flurry's flash no bigger than today's jab, a knee under 5%, the jab 40% smaller) and 2.0 for the finisher; at 0.06 the jab loses two thirds of its flash. (4) The warm-up's 27 ms is gone from the first Bruiser's and boss's spawn and added to the room's load, which already builds the art's pipelines in the frame the camera is placed; measured per spawn, not for the load (perf is the lead's).

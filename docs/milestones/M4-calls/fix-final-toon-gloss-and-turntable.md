# M4 calls: fix-final-toon-gloss-and-turntable

The final review's wave-2 package "per-swatch gloss and C10's turntable front" (findings final-v1-04 and final-v1-07 in `docs/reviews/M4/final-findings.json`, `packages_wave2`; the lead's note and `F-punk.md` W2 asked for it: the cap's hard highlight washed over the strap arch's lower half). Base: `m4/art-pipeline` at e476c0a. Each call is the decision, an alternative not taken, and why. Part 2 is the tests, part 3 the numbers, part 4 what the first-person stills show, part 5 what is handed to the lead. The machine was shared; recordings are 1280x720 renders in a 1600x900 movie, one Godot process at a time. No Tripo or Gemini call, no Blender run except the Punk's `--check`, no spec change.

## 1. Calls

**C1. final-v1-04: the hard highlight is per swatch. Only the shades' lens is glossy; the vest, jeans, cap, laces, tank, gloves, skin, wraps and boots take no highlight at all.**
- *What was found.* Confirmed. `ToonMaterials.art()` was the non-glossy kind and `spec_strength` is 0.5 for every non-room surface, so the whole Punk shared one hard highlight (cut 0.99) that fell wherever a smooth patch faced the half vector: V1's recordings have 3,844 (C2 f109), 4,006 (C4 f85) and 3,841 (C4 f163) pale-mint pixels on the vest, a pale slash on the forehead, blobs on both vest panels and the cap's emblem, streaks on the forearms and gloves. The concept has no highlight there: its only highlights are the white streaks on the gold lens; the bible says glossy regions (shades, leather, studs) get x3.
- *Decision.* A swatch is glossy or matte, from a `"gloss": true` field in the palette JSON (`punk.json` slot 19, `punk_lens`; no other swatch in `shared.json`, `player.json` or `punk.json` has it, so those two files are unchanged). `toon.gdshader` reads it from the atlas (C2), sets two varyings in `fragment()` (`swatch_spec`, the share of the highlight the surface takes, and `swatch_glint`) and `toon_light.gdshaderinc` multiplies the sun's highlight by `swatch_spec`. A flat-painted material (placeholder, prop) has `swatch_spec` 1 and keeps its own `spec_gloss`, unchanged. `ToonMaterials.art()` is now the glossy kind (`spec_gloss` = `look_spec_gloss` 3, the bible's x3), which only a glossy swatch takes.
- *Alternatives not taken.*
  - The finding's `uniform float swatch_spec[64]` filled by `ToonMaterials` from the JSON: the JSON is a pipeline source file (`assets/source`), not a runtime asset, so the game would read a file it does not ship, or the atlas would need a second data file and an ASSETS row. Reading the flag from the texture the shader already samples needs neither.
  - The cap (slot 17) glossy, as the finding's suggested fix has it ("1 for the cap"): the concept's cap is flat yellow with no highlight, the cap's smoothed normals are exactly what made the pale blob, and the slot is also the laces. Matte.
  - The hero's charcoal glove (slot 35) glossy, for the bible's "leather": at 0.2 to 0.4 m the fist's smoothed normals would make the highlight the size of the glove. The hero's arms, gloves and wraps are matte (his C11 stills read clean); a one-line flag in `player.json` turns it on if Game-boy wants a leather glint.
  - A bit in the damage-class alpha with a re-decode of the classes (six classes): it would change `test_art_contract`'s alpha classes and thresholds, a file outside the package, for no gain.

**C2. The gloss flag is one 8-bit step in the atlas alpha (254, 203, 127 for 255, 204, 128), read by the shader as "the class's alpha minus exactly one step".**
- *Decision.* `gen_palette.py` writes `alpha - GLOSS_ALPHA_STEP` for a glossy swatch (a skin swatch cannot be glossy: skin takes no hard highlight, and the script refuses it). The class thresholds (0.9, 0.75) and the 0.01 tolerance of `test_art_contract`'s atlas test read every glossy alpha as the same class, so that file is untouched and the damage look is unchanged. `toon.gdshader`'s `swatch_gloss(alpha)` rounds to 8 bits, takes the class's alpha from the thresholds and is 1 only when it is exactly one lower (a transparent texel or an unused slot is matte); `ToonMaterials.swatch_glossy()` mirrors it. The atlas is 289 bytes, regenerated (`--check` current), lossless import as before, so the 8-bit alpha is exact.
- *Alternative.* A separate gloss texture or an extra row of the atlas (the atlas size is pinned at 256x16 by the contract, and a second texture is a new file and sampler). The alpha already carries the swatch's classes; one more bit of it is the smallest change.

**C3. The lens glints from a camera-fixed light, `look_glint_cut` 0.88 and `look_glint_strength` 1 (Look > Toon). A second hard highlight on a glossy swatch only.**
- *What was found when the matte swatches went matte.* The lens never glints on the sun's highlight in a front view. I measured it: the lens is a curved visor (127 vertices with normals spread from x -0.99 to +0.98, mean tilted 13 degrees down), the sun is 45 degrees up and 50 to one side, and a lens facing the player has `dot(normal, half vector)` about 0.87, so `look_spec_cut` 0.99 shows nothing, and the experiment of lowering that one cut (0.95, 0.90) showed nothing either in C1 (matte vest, no glint, a flat yellow band: J2's "the shades read as a flat yellow band" and J5's "one small hard highlight" would both have lost). The skeptic had flagged it as "not verified".
- *Decision.* On a glossy swatch `light()` adds a glint: `smoothstep` at `glint_cut` of `dot(NORMAL, normalize(fill_dir + VIEW))`, the half vector of the characters' camera-fixed fill light (`fill_dir`, already a Look value: 35 degrees left, 40 up), times `spec_strength * spec_gloss * glint_strength`, for the directional light only. The shader and the sun's highlight are otherwise the same. Because the light is fixed to the camera, a lens that faces the player catches it on the part of the visor that tilts toward the fill's side, a hard white shape that moves with the head turn. 0.88 is within 28 degrees of that half vector. `look_glint_strength` 0 turns it off (the lens then glints only on the sun's highlight).
- *Measured by eye, C1, C2, C4 (the Punk's front, 1 m):* cut 0.93 gave a speck at C1 f109 and none at f229 and C4 f90 or f85; 0.90 a speck and a medium shape, none in three of five C4 frames; 0.88 a glint in every frame the lens is in view (small at C2 f109, a triangle at C1 f109, a third of the lens at C4 f163 and the whole lens of the lying Punk seen from above). It is the white of an additive highlight (spec 0.5 x 3), clipped, a hard shape, as the concept's streaks are.
- *Alternatives not taken.*
  - A painted streak decal in rest space on the glossy swatch (two diagonal bands like the concept): always there, but a pattern with its own angle, width and spacing numbers (more tunables) that does not move with the light and would show from behind, and it would not be "the highlight". The light-driven glint reads as the one hard highlight the bible asks for.
  - A wider `look_spec_cut` for the whole material: nothing on the lens in the front view until it was so wide (below 0.8) that the whole visor flashed, and the placeholders' highlights would move.
  - A band (a contour of `dot(normal, half)` between two cuts) instead of a cap, for a streak shape: its width depends on the visor's normals, which are coarse (127 vertices), and the lying Punk would lose the glint when the whole lens passes the upper cut.
- *Not a spec change.* The SPEC's look is "cel bands, one hard highlight, rim"; a glossy region glinting is the bible's own sentence.

**C4. The tunables are two numbers in the Look > Toon subgroup, saved in presets, and `tuning/default.json` is regenerated (519 values).** No number is a script constant: the cut and the strength are `@export_range` values (`look_glint_cut`, `look_glint_strength`) fed to the art's material by `apply_look()` and moved live by the F1 panel; the shader's defaults match. `ToonMaterials.apply_look()` sets them on the ART kind only (the floor's grid shader does not declare them: the include declares them under the define). The preset is regenerated here, not left to the lead (wave 1 left it, and four `test_tuning_panel` tests read it), so the lead's merge of this file should be "regenerate".

**C5. final-v1-07: the turntable films the Punk's front.** `tests/bot/acts/look_acts.gd::_turntable` (that act only):
- the hero's body is hidden for the act (`CombatActor.rig.set_shown(false)`, shown again before the end): the Punk stands `POSED_M` 1 m ahead of the hero and faces him, so the orbit's q0 and q360 eye (2.2 m, lying 1.8 m, in front of the Punk) is 1.2 m behind the hero, whose navy back then filled the lens. A teleport of the hero 3 m aside was the alternative: it leaves a second body in the orbit's background and its cast shadow on the Punk's floor; hiding removes both.
- the camera is placed before the settle and before each mark (`_orbit_camera()`), and a mark is printed after the tick that draws the pose it names (`LookRect.video_frame()` is the frame the state read now was drawn in, so a mark read before the camera is set describes the previous pose): the first stance's q0 was the free fly default view, every other quarter lagged the orbit by a tick.
- the camera-mode toast ("CAMERA: FREE FLY", 1.5 s) the act's switch raised is cleared, since it covered the Punk's cap in rest q0.
- *Checked:* the C10 recording's q0 and q360 stills at rest, in the guard and lying are all the Punk's front, with the hero out of the frame. The skeptic's narrowing stands (the 45-degree frames, C1 and C2 and the long take had the front elsewhere): this makes the exact quarters usable.

## 2. Tests (`tests/test_toon.gd`; no other test file changed)

- `test_a_glossy_swatch_is_one_8_bit_step_below_its_damage_class`: the decode's truth table (255 and 204 and 128 matte, 254 and 203 and 127 glossy, 0 and 64 matte), the class thresholds still read the glossy alphas as the same class, and the shader's decode text is the mirror's.
- `test_the_atlas_marks_the_lens_glossy_and_every_other_swatch_matte`: for every swatch of the three palette JSONs the atlas's alpha says what its `gloss` says, the damage class survives, a skin swatch is never glossy, and the only glossy swatch is `punk_lens`. Fails on the old atlas (the lens at alpha 128).
- `test_the_punks_glossy_texels_are_the_lens_and_nothing_else`: in all four Punk GLBs the vertices whose UV is a glossy slot all lie in the lens box (x +-0.105, y 1.582 to 1.662, in front of the face), more than 20 of them; the hero's GLB has none. Fails on the old atlas (no glossy faces).
- `test_the_art_highlights_only_where_the_atlas_says_glossy`: the shader and the include's text: the define precedes the include, `swatch_spec` and `swatch_glint` are set from the atlas, the sun's highlight is multiplied by `taken`, the glint comes from `fill_dir` on `swatch_glint` for the directional light, the grid does not define the macro and the include's other branch is 1. (A headless run draws nothing, so the shader's pixels are the captures'.)
- `test_the_art_material_is_the_glossy_kind_and_follows_the_glint_tunables`: the art's `spec_gloss` is `look_spec_gloss` and the character's is 1, the glint uniforms follow `Tuning` live and are the art's alone, both are exported TuningData values, and the default cut is in a small-glint range.
- No existing assertion changed. `test_art_contract`'s atlas test passes on the glossy alphas through its 0.01 tolerance.

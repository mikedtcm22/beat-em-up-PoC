## 1. Views and the generated body (steps 1-2)

**C1. The whole hero is concept C, as crops.** The four references of every view job are crops of `hero_c_martial.jpg` made with ffmpeg (the figure, the open hand's back, the fist, the shoe), so the model sees the hand and shoe callouts as separate pictures and not as a sheet to copy (`views/refs/`). *Alternative:* the whole sheet as one reference. *Why not:* a sheet with a layout invites a sheet back. The prompt is the smoke Punk's (flat unshaded albedo, no ink outlines, no cel shading, no cast shadow, flat #d9d9d9, the mannequin's proportions win, open hands, no logos, no red) plus the outfit and "fine material details only as thin tone-on-tone lines". Accepted as it came: heights within 0.1% of each other, feet within 2 px (`player_views_check.py`, `views_report.json`).

**C2. The right view is the left one mirrored.** The design is left-right symmetric (wraps, gloves, belt on both sides), so `hero_right.jpg` is `hero_left.jpg` through `ffmpeg -vf hflip`: three Gemini images instead of four. *Alternative:* a fourth image. *Why not:* it would only add a chance of a left-right mismatch the model sees. The T11 result shows no mismatch.

**C3. **C4. ## 2. The chain, and why the player has its own part step

**C5. `player_parts.py` and `player_run.py` instead of editing the shared steps.** The shared `blender_parts.py` belongs to F-punk (A14: a change to a shared step is a request), and it fails on this body: it cut the Left hand 17.8 cm up the forearm (a long black cuff tube) because one boundary vertex of the generated mesh sat far from the others, and it keeps the replaced hand's triangles untouched, which would be 2 x 19k for a generated fist. `player_run.py` imports `run.py`, replaces `blender_parts.py` by `player_parts.py` in its step list, adds `detail` after `parts_split`, and calls the shared `main()`: every other step, the report, `--check` and the Godot import are the shared ones. *Alternative:* a flag in the shared step. *Why not:* a request that F-punk, running at the same time, would have to take in its own file. A second hero is another `pipeline.json` and the same command.

**C6. The fist is T13's piece, placed by a config.** The drawn piece turned out to be a LEFT fist (its thumb is on the viewer's left in the front view): the model's palm side is +X, its thumb +Z, its knuckles up. For the left hand that is the table's frame turned 180 degrees about X (knuckles down the arm, palm to the midline, thumb forward); the right hand is its mirror (faces reversed). `parts.fists.map` is a piecewise-linear map of the model's height onto the arm axis (the strap's bottom -0.075 m from the wrist, the wrist plane at the model's y -0.05, the knuckles at +0.121 m) and `xz_scale_m` 0.235 scales across: the Hand-weighted vertices measure 1.01 x 1.01 x 1.10 of the table's fist box (A5: the centroid 11 mm from the box, 15 mm allowed). The strap and the wrist column under the hand are flared (x1.27 to x1.32 down to the wrist, found from the body's radius where the strap begins plus 6 mm, easing to 1 over the hand's base: the generated wrist was 40% narrower than the forearm it sits on) so that the forearm's cut ring, 6 cm above the wrist, ends INSIDE the strap: nothing is open, nothing pokes out, and the step from forearm to strap is a 5 mm ledge that reads as a strap. The strap and the wraps under it are LowerArm, the hand block is Hand (a smoothstep over -0.045 to 0 m). *Alternative:* the Blender-made fist of P1-chain (a block, four bumps, a cuff tube), or cutting the piece open and welding. *Why not:* the first is the "canister" of gate gap 3; the second needs both rings to match.

**C7. Colour: three swatches on the fist, borders protected, 3,000 triangles each.** Each dense face is labelled Lab-nearest to charcoal, skin or cream from the piece's texture (two majority passes), the piece is decimated from 19,273 to 3,000 triangles with the vertices on a colour border protected (they moved 0.0 m), pinholes closed, and the faces are relabelled from the dense piece (`pipe_slot`, so the shared palette step keeps them). The generated texture is a patchwork of flat colours **with no strand lines in it** (the strands and the stitching are real geometry in the 19k mesh), which is why the detail mask takes them from the geometry (C11). *Alternative:* texture ridges for the wraps' strands. *Why not:* measured, the texture's ridges are 0.3% of its pixels and none is a strand.

**C8. The girth clamp (a departure from the slice gate's "keep the art's bulk", for the player only).** `parts.fists.girth` pulls a limb's surface toward its bone's axis past a radius (forearm 0.059, upper arm 0.076, thigh 0.108, shin 0.092, spine 0.15, hips 0.16; the excess keeps 10-20% of itself; weighted by the bone's weight and faded at the segment's ends), 3,794 vertices, 4.7 cm at the most. The proxies' radii are 5.6 (forearm), 7.5 (arms), 10 (thigh), 8.5 (shin); the generated arms were 6.3 / 8.3 median and 7.4 / 10.5 at the 90th percentile. *Why:* the player's own limbs are what first person measures against the M3-tuned proxies (A9), and the lead arm passes 20 cm from the lens in a straight punch. Before the clamp the bare upper arm filled a third of the jab frame. *Alternative:* keep the bulk (the Punk's lock). *Why not for him:* the lock's reason (J1, the silhouette) is about what the player sees of an enemy, and the player never sees his own silhouette except in the KO view. The spec value is reachable: delete `parts.fists.girth` and rerun. **It is a departure for Game-boy's ruling** (README M4 departures).

**C9. The split: a clean horizontal cut, cleaned cuts, no third mesh.** `player_parts.py split` differs from the shared one in three ways. (a) It bisects every face that can be hidden (the shoulder zone and the upper arms too) by the Chest plane, so `Body__fp_hidden` starts at the Chest joint (a first run had one shoulder-zone vertex 1.3 cm below it, and `test_art_contract` wants 2 mm). (b) It welds the hairline cracks of the generated mesh (boundary vertices within 2 mm), collapses slits under 12 mm and, after the caps, cuts out and patches what is still open or shared by three faces (a manifold patch by `holes_fill`), in both parts: the contract wants no open edge on `Body` and the seam to close (24 faces cut out, 7 patched on `Body`; 15 and 4 on the hidden part). Without it, 69 open edges (40 of them the forearm rings) and 5 survive the caps. (c) It triangulates everything at the end (the patches are n-gons). *Alternative:* a voxel remesh to a clean surface. *Why not:* it would eat the wraps and the creases.

**C10. No `Body__fp_torso` (F-player-int's request, not done).** A belly-and-belt mesh needs two edits outside this package: `pb.body_objects()` in the shared exporter writes only `Body` and `Body__fp_hidden`, and `test_art_contract` names only those two. *Alternative:* build it behind a config flag. *Why not:* it would be dead code that nothing could export or test. With `fp_knockdown_hide_torso` on, the switch hides `ArtBody` as a whole (the legs and forearms too), as `F-player-int.md` C8 says. Requests below.

## 3. The detail mask (step 3)

**C11. `player_detail.py`: borders from the palette, creases from the dense geometry, on a UV2 layout that spends texels where he looks.** The shared palette step lays UV2 out by area, which gave 3.3 mm per texel everywhere and half of them on the hidden chest and head. The detail step re-unwraps UV2 of both meshes together (smart project, then every island scaled by its bone: the hidden mesh x0.3, forearms and hands x1.8, feet and shins x1.5, thighs x1.2; all islands packed with rotation) and the exporter reads that file: texel size **1.7 mm at the 10th percentile (forearms, hands), 2.3 mm median, 10.5 mm on the hidden part**; coverage 40% of the 1024 square. A texel is ink when (a) it lies within 1 texel of a triangle edge whose neighbour wears another swatch (analytic from the barycentric coordinates, so an island's own edge is never a line), or (b) the dense surface nearest to it sits in a groove: the vertex "cavity" (the mean of the neighbours' height above the vertex along its normal, in edge lengths) rises from 0.12 to 0.28 (the wraps' strands, the glove's stitching, the finger creases, the cloth's folds), or (c) the dense texture has a thin dark ridge (none on the fist, a few on the body). Bare skin takes no crease ink on the body (a 2 mm groove is 9 px at the 0.2 m the lead arm passes the lens; the fingers' creases on the fist piece do): `crease.skip_slots` 6-9. Of the covered texels 2.7% are ink: 7,965 border texels, 2,782 crease and 4,019 crease-or-ridge. The cap faces stay at UV2 (0, 0), which is empty. The mask is `player_detail.png` (R = ink, G and B repeat it), imported lossless with mipmaps (`player_run.py` patches the `.import`; the toon shader reads `texture(detail_tex, UV2).r`). *Alternative:* the plan's "palette borders in UV2 space and the fist texture's dark strokes" only. *Why not:* the first alone has no wrap strands, and the texture has no strokes (C7).

### F1. The front kick's "near-black shape" was the legs inside out, and it was in the look-down too (the brief's fix 3)

**What the brief saw.** In `pa1`'s front kick (frame 326) the kicking leg and shoe are one flat shape of the ink colour, (16, 15, 23), with a sawtooth edge where the lit slate trouser begins; the look-down 65 shows the near shoe the same colour. The brief's reading was the charcoal swatch's shadow band, and its remedy a lighter charcoal for the shoe.

**What it is.** Diagnostic recordings of that frame on the old GLB, one change each: the hull's width 0 (`look_outline_fp_scale=0`): the black stays; the edge pass off: stays; both bands forced to the lit value (`look_band_1=look_band_2=0`): stays, with the same sawtooth; the detail mask removed: stays; **the hull's colour painted red: the black becomes red, so it is the hull pass, which draws a mesh's back faces; the toon material with culling off: the whole leg and shoe appear, a charcoal upper with a cream sole and sock, the slate trouser, a few black triangles.** So the base pass culls the near wall of the shin and shoe (it faces away from the lens) and the hull pass draws it as ink. The nearest distance of the leg to the lens at the strike is 0.31-0.37 m, so it is not the near plane. A per-triangle test of the GLB (the winding normal against the direction from the dominant bone's axis; `test_no_limb_is_inside_out...`): 88% of the shin's triangles, 49-96% of every leg bone's and the feet's, and 22% of the Hips band faced the bone. Through the chain's work files: 1% after `fit`, `rig`, `parts_replace` and `palette`, **88% after `parts_split`**. The cause is in C9: `player_parts.py split` ran Blender's `recalc_face_normals` over the whole body (in the hole fill and in the caps' clean-up). One shell of a layered, partly non-manifold generated mesh gets its inside picked, and nothing local says it is wrong; the legs, feet and part of the hips lost. (The F-player-art round called the black "the band's sawtooth edge" and the look-down shoe "black slip-ons that read": both were this.)

**The fix.** No global recalculation anywhere in the split. `mark_trusted()` marks every face of the generated body (`pipe_trusted` 1) before anything is cut; the faces the split makes (hole patches, caps) start at 0, and `orient_untrusted()` gives each the winding of the trusted faces it grew from by a flood fill over manifold edges (0 caps needed flipping, 0 patches in the player's run: the geometry was fine, only the global step was wrong). After the fix the same test reads 0-7% on every leg bone (the creases and the crotch), 0% on the Punk and the probe. **Alternative not taken: the lighter charcoal.** The charcoal is #55535a, value 0.353, already above the 0.25-0.3 the brief names; it was never the shoe's colour that rendered, and darkening it to 0.25-0.3 would have broken the slice gate's glove rule (value at least about 0.3) on the gloves, belt and wraps that share the swatch (a seventh swatch for the shoes would break A3's six). After the fix the shoe's lit band is (78, 78, 86) against the slate's (100, 100, 108), the shoe also has its mid and shadow bands (54, 52, 69) and a hard highlight, a cream sock and sole separate it from the trouser, and the ink line is continuous round both. The recording is in "What the sheets show" below.

**Guard.** `test_art_contract.test_no_limb_is_inside_out_its_triangles_face_away_from_their_bone` (every GLB, every mesh: a leg, foot or thigh bone with 100 or more triangles faces its bone in under 15%; it fails the F-player-art GLB with 49-96%; the forearm is not measured, the Punk's gauntlet flares to 17-26%).

### F3. The fists: 4,000 triangles, the label borders cleaned, the fingers kept (fix 2)

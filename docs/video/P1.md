Four Sonnet builders, run by `tools/workflows/build.js` with `docs/video/args-p1.json`, in two waves:
- **Wave 1, the look:** LD-WORLD and LD-INK.
- **Wave 2:** LD-STAGE and LD-ASSET.

## What Phase 0 found (read this first)
**The bars.** The Gemini paint-overs of our six stills are the look-dev targets: `/Users/arachne/repos/beat-em-up-PoC/.worktrees/video/captures/video/p0/gemini/target_<still>.jpg`, for `fp_punk`, `fp_kicker_lamp`, `ox_closeup`, `ox_low`, `street_wide` and `roof_silhouette`.
- `target_fp_kicker_lamp` is the best map of the night street:
  - the sodium lamp makes a **hard-edged warm pool** on the ground, a graphic shape rather than a falloff;
  - everything else is navy;
  - steam;
  - the foreground arm in two hard values with an ink contour.
- The targets are judged beside the reference frames in `.../captures/video/ref/frames/`. Our stills (the game's own look) are in `/Users/arachne/repos/beat-em-up-PoC/.worktrees/mv-p0-stills/captures/video/p0-stills/<still>.png`.

**The spike** (`video/spike/`, now merged; renders a-e in `/Users/arachne/repos/beat-em-up-PoC/.worktrees/mv-p0-spike/captures/video/p0-spike/r7/`) scored about 1.4-1.6 against a bar of 2.4. What it showed:
- The flat-paint environment (`video/shaders/flat_paint_env.gdshader`) was the biggest single step.
- The light-weighted screen ink was invisible at night: dark ink on dark fill. The references' lines read against **lit rims and lit fills**, so the characters need rim and key light shapes to draw against.
- The game's toon `light()` floods a night scene: every point light adds `shadow_level × light colour` across its whole volume. A cine shader must add nothing in a light's shadow band.
- Godot's glow and volumetric fog are smooth Gaussian gradients, and they read as a 3D render.
- The cool share was too low, and the median L\* about twice ref 2's.

**The blind check.** One Opus agent sorted 12 unlabelled stills into "3D render" or "drawn":
- It called all six reference frames drawn, and our plain game frames 3D.
- It called the spike 3D (0.92): a smooth bloom blob on the lamp; cel bands with jagged, shadow-map terminators; a hard, noisy shadow edge on the ground; flat texture-mapped faces on a crate.
- It called **two of the three Gemini targets 3D too.** The giveaway was our geometry, which the paint-over kept:
  - brick tiled mechanically in exact perspective;
  - window rows that repeat identically and converge to a ruler;
  - a chain-link mesh in exact perspective;
  - even, ruler-clean outlines on every straight edge;
  - two identical parallel cast shadows from a multi-light rig;
  - three identical copies of one enemy model;
  - fog that fades exactly with depth.
- Only Ox's close-up target passed, at 0.55. Even there, the window grid behind him and a rim glow of constant width pointed to 3D.

**So the rule for Phase 1:** paint over the shading, and also **break the mechanical regularity** everywhere the eye can find it. That means irregular, simplified, hand-wobbled shapes and lines, anchored in world or object space so they never swim. One shadow caster per shot, soft-edged then posterised with a painted edge. Banded halos in place of bloom. Flat haze layers in place of an exact depth fog. Far geometry replaced by painted mattes.

## Shared by all four packages
- **The look-piece interface.** `video/look/look_piece.gd` (`VideoLookPiece extends Resource`, with `func apply(level: Node) -> void`) is the contract.
  - Each package delivers its look as a `VideoLookPiece` subclass, with its values in `.tres` files under `video/look/<location>/`. That's PLAN.md's `CineLook`, split by owner.
  - Per-frame work goes in a helper Node that `apply()` adds under the level.
  - A shot applies its pieces in the order art, then world, then ink.
  - Locations: `street_night`, `roofs_dusk` and `boss_fire` (PLAN.md §2, "Our three locations").
- **Testing your piece.** Use the Phase 0 still stage, merged on `video/cinematic-trailer`:
  - `video/stage/still_bot.gd`, `still_grab.gd`, the six shots in `video/stills/*.tres` and `tools/video/shoot_still.sh`.
  - Add **your own** act file (`video/acts/<your key>_acts.gd`) that applies your pieces and shoots the shots. Never edit another package's act file.
  - The spike's light rig (`video/spike/spike_look.gd`) is a stand-in for the world look until LD-WORLD lands.
- **Measure** with `tools/video/measure_refs.py` against STYLE.md's location columns. The lead is adding `tools/video/style_meter.py`, which scores against those ranges; use it if it's on the branch when you start.
- **The 2560×1440 render scale.** The game's LookPost edge widths and Godot's glow are in pixels, so they halve relative to the frame at 2560×1440 (the P0 builders measured this). Set every width and radius for the render size, and judge at the render size.
- **Determinism and motion.**
  - Everything you add (noise, grain, wobble, jitter) is anchored in object or world space, or seeded per frame from the frame number. It's never `TIME` or a free-running random.
  - Line boil is the one deliberate flicker: re-seed it at 12 fps from the frame number.
  - **Check motion:** render 12 consecutive frames of one shot while the camera moves (`_turn_by` in your act), and look at a few in sequence for swimming and flicker.
- **Never** edit `game/`, `tuning/` or `tests/`, another package's files, or Tuning presets (`user://tuning`). Runtime changes go only through `Tuning.set_value`, material swaps, and nodes you add.
- **Assets** you generate go under `assets/video/`. Check `git check-attr filter -- <file>`: if `.gitattributes` routes it to LFS, commit through LFS; if not, keep files small (PNG, 2048 px or under) and list their sizes in your calls file.
- **Video tests** go in `video/tests/test_<key>_*.gd`, using the PLAN.md §6 command.

## LD-WORLD: the world's light, paint and atmosphere (wave 1)
**Owns:** `video/world/`, `video/look/*/world*.tres`, `video/shaders/flat_paint_env.gdshader` (take it over from the spike), `video/shaders/sky_*.gdshader`, `video/shaders/haze_*.gdshader`, `tools/video/paint_mattes.py`, `assets/video/mattes/`, `video/acts/ld_world_acts.gd`, and `video/tests/test_ld_world_*.gd`.
**Reads:** GAME_NOTES.md §1, §2, §7 and §8, `video/spike/`, and the targets.

Deliver `VideoWorldLook` (a `VideoLookPiece`) with one `.tres` per location. It does the following:

1. **The light rig per location, shot-designed** (PLAN.md §4, part 1).
   - One motivated key, one accent of the opposite temperature, and **one shadow caster**.
   - Video-only practical lights with dressing placed from data:
     - sodium lamp posts with their heads, on the street (the P0 stage has a lamp-post prop);
     - fire barrels;
     - the boss roof's fire light from below.
   - **Lamp pools as hard graphic shapes**, like `target_fp_kicker_lamp`, not falloffs (a light projector/cookie, or the env paint's banding).
   - Turn the level's own section light and sun off or override them (GAME_NOTES.md, gotcha 10).
   - Rim lights belong to LD-INK. Leave a hook for them: an exported key colour and direction they can read.
2. **The flat-paint environment, version 2.**
   - Coarse-mip posterised trim, 2-3 value bands for each material, hard terminators with a painted edge, and world-anchored grain (from the spike).
   - **Light shapes:** point and spot lights add nothing outside their band, and their pools have hard, slightly broken edges.
   - **Break the regularity in object or world space.**
     - A low-frequency domain warp of the trim UVs, so window rows and brick courses wobble.
     - Drop or merge some repeats, for example blank out some windows with a world-hashed mask.
     - Brick shown only as a few painted strokes near light edges, and none in flat shadow.
     - The chain-link fence becomes a simplified painted pattern or a flat silhouette.
   - The ground too.
3. **Painted skies and mattes** (PLAN.md §4, part 4).
   - A sky shader: domain-warped noise quantised into brush bands, and a moon or sun disc with a **banded halo** (uneven painted rings, like ref 2 at 50.5 s).
   - `tools/video/paint_mattes.py` (the venv: numpy, scipy, OpenCV) paints matte textures: far skyline silhouettes and smoke banks in brush bands, with an anisotropic Kuwahara filter allowed on these layers only.
   - Matte cards behind the playfield replace the far city where a shot looks down the street (`street_wide`).
4. **Atmosphere.**
   - **Flat haze layers** (2-3 depth bands, each a flat tint with a painted edge) in place of an exact depth fog. Volumetric fog only for the shafts at lamps, if they read as painted.
   - **Banded halo cards** on light sources, with Godot glow turned down.
   - GPU particles with code-made sprites: embers (boss roof, fire barrels), steam (street manholes), dust.

**Done when:** each of the six shots, rendered with your world look over the game's toon characters, meets:
- STYLE.md's numbers for its location;
- read beside its reference frame and its target, as a painted environment with none of the blind check's environment tells.

Report each shot's numbers, and your honest P2-P5 and P8 scores for each.

## LD-INK: the characters' paint and line, the 2D cues and the post (wave 1)
**Owns:** `video/ink/`, `video/look/*/ink*.tres`, `video/shaders/cine_toon.gdshader`, `video/shaders/cine_outline.gdshader`, `video/shaders/ink_screen.gdshader` (take it over from the spike), `video/shaders/post_*.glsl` and the compositor scripts, `tools/video/palette_lut.py`, `tools/video/look_sweep.py`, `video/acts/ld_ink_acts.gd`, and `video/tests/test_ld_ink_*.gd`.
**Reads:** GAME_NOTES.md §2, §5, §6 and §7, `game/fx/toon.gdshader`, `toon_light.gdshaderinc`, `toon_outline.gdshader`, `video/spike/`, and the targets.

Deliver `VideoInkLook` (a `VideoLookPiece`) with one `.tres` per location. It does the following:

1. **`cine_toon.gdshader` for every character**, the player's first-person body included. Swap it onto the rig-art materials (GAME_NOTES.md §2, "How a video-only script can override looks").
   - It includes `toon_light.gdshaderinc` where it can, rather than forking it.
   - 2-3 posterised bands, and a hard terminator with a painted edge (no shadow-map jaggies: soften, then posterise).
   - The scene's shadow hue.
   - **A strong rim band tinted by the key**, with a width that varies by surface curvature, never constant.
   - Point lights add nothing in their shadow band.
   - Object-space brush grain.
   - Hatching (tonal art maps generated by code) in the shadow band, switched on for close-ups.
   - Glints on metal only.
   - **For LD-ASSET,** declare these uniforms now, defaulting to off:
     - `ink_tex` (R is ink in UV0 space, 1 = ink) and `use_ink_tex`;
     - `paint_tex` (painted albedo in UV0) and `use_paint_tex`;
     - `ink_color` and `ink_strength`.
2. **The line.**
   - `cine_outline.gdshader`, a video copy of the hull, whose width follows the key: heavy on the shadow side, thin or gone on the rim side. It's scaled by distance and by the render height, in the ink colour, with a slight width variation along the contour from object-space noise.
   - **Rim lights that follow each character**, so the lines and the rim have lit shapes to read against: one light, or a shader rim from a key direction set by the world look.
   - The screen-space ink (the spike's `ink_screen`, taken over):
     - for characters, creases and inner lines where they read against lit fills;
     - for the environment, only major silhouettes, thinning and dropping out with distance, with broken, world-anchored wobble and weight. Never an even ruler line.
   - Turn the game's LookPost edge quad off (GAME_NOTES.md §2).
   - A **sketch mode** for close-ups: 2-3 jittered hull passes with overshoot, re-seeded at 12 fps from the frame number.
3. **The 2D cues** (PLAN.md §4, part 7), each as a switch in the `.tres`, for Gate 2's A/Bs.
   - **Characters on twos:** a character's pose holds for two output frames at 24 fps while the camera moves every frame. Suggested way: a helper node stores each skeleton's bone poses on "draw" frames and re-applies them on "hold" frames, after the animation has run and before render (`process_priority` above the rigs'). Physics is never touched.
   - **Smears on fast limbs:** a smear card or stretched shape along a fist's path during an attack, from `CombatEvents`.
   - **The comic words and starbursts hidden** (GAME_NOTES.md, gotcha 4).
   - **Impact flash frames and speed lines, re-done inside the 3D render.** The game's are `CanvasLayer`s of the main viewport and never reach the 2560×1440 SubViewport.
4. **Post** as `CompositorEffect` compute passes (check the 4.7 API with `godot --headless --doctool`):
   - a static paper grain (fixed to the frame, like paper);
   - a vignette;
   - a little chromatic aberration.
   - No other screen-space noise on fills.
5. **The palette LUT for each location** (`palette_lut.py`, from the matching reference frames), and `tools/video/look_sweep.py`, which renders one still under a sweep of a few `.tres` values and ranks them with the style meter.

**Done when:** the Punk at first-person distance (2-3 m) and Ox in a close-up both render with the cine character look under the spike's night light, or LD-WORLD's if it's on the branch. Judged at full resolution beside ref 1 at 0.9 s and 7.2 s, ref 2 at 12.9 s and the targets, their lines read and their fills look painted. Report your honest P1, P2, P7 and P8 scores, the A/B renders of each 2D cue, and the 12-frame motion check.

## LD-STAGE: the cine stage and the frame writer (wave 2)
**Owns:** `video/stage/` (taking over the still stage and the spike's capture), `video/cine/`, `video/shots/`, `tools/video/shoot.sh`, `video/acts/ld_stage_acts.gd`, and `video/tests/test_ld_stage_*.gd`.
**Reads:** PLAN.md §5 (format) and §6 (LD-RENDER's frame writer and determinism), and GAME_NOTES.md §4, §5 and §7.

This is PLAN.md §6's LD-RENDER without the look:
- **`CineDirector`** applies a shot's pieces (art, world, ink) and hides the HUD.
- **`CineCamera`**: a keyframed path, a look-at target, and FOV and focus keys, with depth of field.
- **Shot scenes** in `video/shots/`: one per Gate 1 still (from `video/stills/*.tres`), plus **the 5 s moving shot**: first person, P P P P on a Punk under a street lamp.
- **The frame writer**, `tools/video/shoot.sh <shot>`, as PLAN.md §6 specifies:
  - a 2560×1440 SubViewport, and a character-mask SubViewport;
  - only the 24 fps picks of the 60 fps simulation (frame i when floor(i·24/60) changes), never blended;
  - only the [from, to] window, with the window's own viewport disabled outside it;
  - encoded every 200 frames to the mezzanine (`ffmpeg -c:v libx264 -crf 8 -preset slow -pix_fmt yuv444p`), with the PNGs deleted after each encode;
  - the capture lock.
  - It records seconds per frame and MB per frame.
- **Determinism:** seed the VFX and audio RNGs, start every act through `_reset`, and render the moving shot twice and compare frame by frame.

**Done when:** the seven Gate 2 shots render through `shoot.sh` with all the pieces on the branch, the two renders of the moving shot match, and the measured seconds and MB per frame are reported.

## LD-WORLD2: the world, round 2: drawn detail, no glare (after Gate 1)
**Game-boy at Gate 1 (2026-11-08):** "The characters look pretty good, but I think the environments are maybe a bit too washed out by lighting/glare in places. I'd like there to be more detail in the environments - so that people can discern how the player is moving through the game. Can you try to render the environments with some more detail? Use the first 10-15 sec or the 'pt5' video as a reference."

His eye outranks the blind check: where they disagree, detail wins. **Approved:** Game-boy chose round 2 as the environment look ("Yes, round 2", 2026-11-08 15:46 CST).
- **The new bar:** the round 2 targets, `/Users/arachne/repos/beat-em-up-PoC/.worktrees/video/captures/video/p0/gemini_env/target_<still>.jpg`, for `fp_punk`, `fp_kicker_lamp`, `street_wide`, `roof_silhouette` and `ox_low`.
- **Beside** ref 2 at 3.5 s and 4.5 s (`.../captures/video/ref/frames/ref2_3.5.png` and `ref2_4.5.png`): the camp and the tent, dark navy with everything drawn.
- **The comparison sheet** is `.../captures/video/p0/gate1/env/overview.png`: our still | round 1 | round 2.

**Owns:** LD-WORLD's files (merged), building on `VideoWorldLook`, plus `video/acts/ld_world2_acts.gd` and `video/tests/test_ld_world2_*.gd`.
**Reads:** P1.md's LD-WORLD section and `docs/video/calls/ld-world.md` first.

1. **Draw the detail back.**
   - Brick courses, window frames and sills, doors, pipes, fire escapes, signs, cracks, puddles, kerbs and paving are each drawn: ink lines and hard shadow shapes, with a few painted strokes.
   - Undo round 1's flattened brick and hash-blanked windows.
   - Let the trim texture's own detail guide the drawing. For example: ink from the edges of the trim's albedo or normal at a middle mip, with object-space wobble and a weight that varies, over 2-3 value bands.
2. **No glare and no wash.**
   - Cut the haze bands and halo cards back, so walls, ground and props stay readable at every distance.
   - Lamps are small, contained sources with hard-edged pools. No orange veil.
   - The street at night is navy with readable shapes, like ref 2 at 3.5 s.
   - The boss roof keeps its fire from below, but no smoke wall or glow hides the roof and the buildings.
3. **The path reads.** The ground (paving, kerbs, manholes, puddles) and the bases of the walls carry the most drawn detail and contrast, so the player's movement through the street reads in first person.
4. **The props get the same drawn treatment:** water tanks, AC units, cars, crates and dumpsters (`flat_paint_prop.gdshader`). Find their materials at run time without touching `game/` (LD-WORLD's open request).
5. **Keep** round 1's light rig and single shadow caster, the sky and its banded sun halo, the embers and steam, and the world-anchored stability.

**Done when:** the five shots read like their round 2 targets beside ref 2 at 3.5 and 4.5 s: detailed, drawn, crisp, with the route readable, and the 12-frame motion check still clean. STYLE.md's numbers are only a guide here. Edge density and ink share will rise above round 1's, which is intended: record the new values.

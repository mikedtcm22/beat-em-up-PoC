# Phase 0 packages: the stills and the spike

Two Sonnet builders, run by `tools/workflows/build.js` with `docs/video/args-p0.json`, alongside the lead. This file holds their briefs. The context is [PLAN.md](PLAN.md): §1-2 for the goal and the style, and §4 (Plan A) and §6 (Phase 0) for the approach. Each builder reads its own section here, PLAN.md §2 and the parts of [GAME_NOTES.md](GAME_NOTES.md) its section names, and searches the code for the rest.

## Shared by both packages
- **The reference frames** are full-resolution PNGs in `/Users/arachne/repos/beat-em-up-PoC/.worktrees/video/captures/video/ref/frames/` (git-ignored): `ref1_<t>.png` (2560×1440) and `ref2_<t>.png` (1280×720), with `<t>` the time in seconds. Read them as images (they count against your image budget). Never copy them into git, and never sample their pixels into anything we render.
- **The style numbers** are in [STYLE.md](STYLE.md). `tools/video/style_metrics.py` measures any PNG the same way:
  `~/.venvs/beat-em-up-video/bin/python -I tools/video/measure_refs.py --out <json> --frames <png> ...`
  The venv has numpy, scipy, opencv-python-headless, imageio and Pillow. Never install anything.
- **Reusing the bot harness without touching `tests/`:**
  - A script under `video/` may `extends "res://tests/bot/level_bot.gd"`, the way `level_bot.gd` extends `crowd_bot.gd`.
  - It overrides `_collect_acts()` to read its own act files, with a new constant name: the base constants can't be redeclared.
  - It may override `_run` to skip the readability probe and the autopilot.
  - See GAME_NOTES.md §4, and `tests/bot/level_acts/tour_acts.gd` for placing the player by hand and settling the section light.
- **Rendering at 2560×1440, independent of the window.**
  - A `SubViewport` of 2560×1440 shares the main `World3D` (`own_world_3d` false). Its own `Camera3D` copies the main viewport's active camera every frame, after the game cameras have moved (they use `process_priority` 50): `global_transform`, `fov`, `keep_aspect`, `near`, `far` and `cull_mask`.
  - Give it MSAA 4×.
  - The HUD and the 2D effect layers are `CanvasLayer`s of the main viewport, so they never reach the SubViewport. Hide the HUD in the window anyway (`level.get_node("Hud").visible = false`) and the bot caption (GAME_NOTES.md §3).
  - Save with `subviewport.get_texture().get_image().save_png(...)` after `await RenderingServer.frame_post_draw`.
  - **Check once** that the SubViewport's frame matches the window's frame (the same camera, the same edge lines, the same grade). The LookPost edge lines are a full-screen quad, and the colour grade is on the Environment (GAME_NOTES.md §2). Report any difference.
- **Running it.** A windowed Godot (rendering needs the GPU, so never `--headless` for a still):
  `godot --path <your worktree> --fixed-fps 60 --resolution 1280x720 <res:// scene> -- --level=res://game/level/mission1/mission1.tscn ...`
  Take the machine-wide capture lock that `tools/capture_bot.sh` uses (copy its lock code into your shell script): the game session's captures share it. Always give a `--quit-after` frame cap.
- **Determinism.** After the room's `_ready`, seed `VfxDirector.rng` and `AudioDirector.rng` with fixed numbers (GAME_NOTES.md §7, gotcha 7). Use the bot's `_reset` path and fixed seeds.
- **Never** call Tuning's preset functions (load, save, reset, apply_preset, backup_unsaved). They write `user://tuning`, which is Game-boy's own preset folder for this project. Change values only with `Tuning.set_value` or the bot's `tune()`, at runtime.
- **Video tests** go in `video/tests/test_*.gd` and run on their own, so the main suite doesn't grow:
  `godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s addons/gut/gut_cmdln.gd -gdir=res://video/tests -gexit` (check the flags against `addons/gut`).
  - The main suite's compile tests only scan `res://game` and `res://tests/bot`, so nothing under `video/` reaches them.
  - Give video classes a `Video` or `Cine` prefix, and grep that no `class_name` clashes with the game's.
- **Captures.** Make `captures/` in your worktree with a `.gdignore` (`mkdir -p captures/video && touch captures/.gdignore captures/video/.gdignore`), and put every render under `captures/video/<key>/`. Report their absolute paths in `captures`: the lead reads them from your worktree.

## P0-STILLS: the still stage and the six Gate 1 stills
**Owns:** `video/stage/`, `video/acts/stills_acts.gd`, `video/tests/test_still_stage.gd`, `tools/video/shoot_still.sh`.
**Reads:** GAME_NOTES.md §1, §3, §4, §5 and §6.

**Build a reusable still stage.** This is the seed of Phase 1's frame writer (PLAN.md §6, LD-RENDER), so keep it clean and data-driven.
- `video/stage/still_bot.gd` and `still_bot.tscn` extend the level bot. They read acts from `res://video/acts/` (`*_acts.gd`) and skip the probe and the pilot.
- `video/stage/still_grab.gd` is the 2560×1440 SubViewport mirror above, with `capture(path)`.
- Placements live in data, not script constants: a `video/stage/still_shot.gd` resource (`VideoStillShot`), with one `.tres` per still in `video/stills/`. It holds:
  - the arena or section, and the player's position, yaw and pitch;
  - the actors (catalog id, position, facing, an animation or move and the tick to hold it at);
  - the camera: first person, or free with a position, a look-at point and a horizontal FOV;
  - the warm-up ticks.
- `tools/video/shoot_still.sh <act filter> <out dir>` runs it under the capture lock and writes `<out>/<name>.png`.
- `video/tests/test_still_stage.gd` is a headless smoke test: every still's `.tres` loads, and the act file compiles.

## P0-SPIKE: the hardest question first
**Owns:** `video/spike/`, `video/shaders/ink_screen.gdshader`, `video/shaders/flat_paint_env.gdshader`, `tools/video/palette_lut.py`.
**Reads:** GAME_NOTES.md §1, §2, §3, §4, §5 and §8 (and §7's RNG note).

Plan A rests on one question: can code alone make the game's frame read as drawn and painted, not as a lit 3D game? This still answers it early, before any big build. Make **one still** at 2560×1440, then render it at night: the street in first person, a Street Punk in his guard at about **2 m**, facing us, a little off-centre, with the fists in the near foreground. Use **only these four things** on top of the game's own scene:

1. **Hand-placed light** for a night street, like ref 2 at 37.4 and 42.0 s (PLAN.md §2, "Our three locations").
   - Night ambient: navy, low.
   - Cool blue moonlight as the fill, from a `DirectionalLight3D` with shadows.
   - One motivated warm key: a sodium lamp at about `#ff9a3c`. The street has no lamp lights (GAME_NOTES.md, gotcha 1), so place an `OmniLight3D` or `SpotLight3D` with shadows where a lamp would be, above and to one side of the Punk.
   - A rim light on the Punk from behind, tinted by the key.
   - Volumetric fog tinted navy, with the lamp scattering in it (`light_volumetric_fog_energy`).
   - Glow with a wide halo on the light sources.
   - The level re-applies its section light every physics frame (GAME_NOTES.md, gotcha 10): turn that off or override it the way the notes say.
2. **The palette transfer.** `tools/video/palette_lut.py <reference png> <our png> <out lut png>`:
   - It measures the reference frame and our frame, and writes a 32³ LUT in `tools/gen_lut.gd`'s strip format (GAME_NOTES.md §2, item 5) that moves our colours toward theirs.
   - Use a smooth transfer that keeps our hues where theirs have none: Reinhard means and spreads of a\* and b\* per L\* band, with a gentle L\* curve, or a few rounds of Pitié's distribution transfer, regularised. Check that the result has no banding or posterisation.
   - Use ref 2 at 37.4 s as the reference (and try ref 1 at 15.6 s).
   - Apply it as an `ImageTexture3D` on `Environment.adjustment_color_correction`, after LookPost has set the grade, the way GAME_NOTES.md §2 says.
3. **A screen-space ink pass from depth and normals, weighted by light** (`video/shaders/ink_screen.gdshader`): a full-screen quad in Forward+, like the game's `game/fx/edge_pass.gdshader`, which is the model to start from.
   - It reads `hint_depth_texture` and `hint_normal_roughness_texture`, and finds lines from depth and normal discontinuities.
   - **Line weight follows the light:** heavier and darker where the surface faces away from the key, thin or lost where the rim or key hits it. The key's view-space direction comes from a uniform the spike script sets every frame.
   - Lines also thin and fade with distance; the environment keeps only its major silhouettes.
   - The ink is tinted warm brown-black, never pure black.
   - The characters' inverted-hull outline may be tinted to the same ink and made thinner, so the screen-space pass carries the weight. Log that call.
4. **The flat-paint environment** (`video/shaders/flat_paint_env.gdshader`): a video-only copy of `game/fx/trim.gdshader` with the same uniform names, swapped in at runtime (`ToonMaterials.trim().shader = ...`, GAME_NOTES.md §2, "How a video-only script can override looks"). Do the same for the ground if it's a separate material.
   - It reads the trim texture at a coarse mip, which keeps the big shapes (windows, doors, shutters) and drops the brick and grime detail.
   - It posterises the value into 2-3 bands for each material, keeping the hue.
   - Hard terminators with a slightly broken, painted edge.
   - A fine brush grain anchored in world or object space. Never screen space: under a moving camera, screen-space noise swims.

**What to deliver:**
- `video/spike/spike_bot.gd` and `.tscn`, extending the level bot with one act, "Spike: street punk".
- `video/spike/spike_look.gd`: the light, LUT, ink and paint set-up, with every value in a `.tres` or an `@export` in a `.tscn`, not script constants.
- Its own small 2560×1440 SubViewport capture. P0-STILLS builds the shared one at the same time; don't wait for it. Keep yours private under `video/spike/`, and the lead merges them later.
- **An A/B strip** at 2560×1440, each render saved:
  - (a) the game's plain look;
  - (b) + light;
  - (c) + light + flat paint;
  - (d) + light + flat paint + ink;
  - (e) all four, with the LUT: the spike.
  - Then make a contact sheet of the five.
- **Judge it honestly.**
  - Read (e) at full resolution beside ref 2 at 37.4 s and ref 1 at 7.2 s.
  - Score each of the eight pillars of PLAN.md §2 from 0 to 3, using the Gate 2 rubric in PLAN.md §6.
  - Measure (a) and (e) with `measure_refs.py`, against STYLE.md's "street at night" column.
  - Put the scores, the numbers, and the two things that would move the frame furthest toward "drawn, not rendered" in `feel_judgement`.
- **Iterate on what you see.** At least two rounds of look, change and look on (e), within your image budget. The spike's value is its honesty, not its polish.

**Done when:** the five renders and their sheet exist, the scores and numbers are reported, the code is committed, and `git diff --quiet origin/video/cinematic-trailer -- game tuning tests` exits 0.

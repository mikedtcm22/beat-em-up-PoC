# R5-SHOTS: judgement calls (round 5: the hero frames, staged for their plates)

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

## Calls
1. **The first-person lens (55-60 degrees) is written through Tuning at run time, not in the frame writer's camera.** The director's `apply_presentation()` already writes `fov_deg` through `Tuning.set_value` for a first-person shot and puts it back; the shot's `first_person_fov_deg` is now 58 for the nine first-person trailer shots (the stills keep their 75). `@export_range` on `TuningData.fov_deg` (75-110) is an editor hint: `Tuning.set_value` does not clamp, so 58 reaches the game's camera. Alternative: the frame writer's camera overriding the FOV (the round 4 note). Not taken: the writer's camera is a copy of the main camera, and R5-PAINT's plate bot reads the main camera: a plate captured at 75 would not match a frame drawn at 58. One lens everywhere is the plates' contract. Nothing is written outside the run, and nothing in `game/`.
2. **The viewmodel is lowered by moving the lens, in a presentation node (`CineViewRig`).** It runs after the game's camera (priority 60, the camera's is 50): the camera moves along its own up and back axes by the shot's `view_lift_m` and `view_back_m` (about 4-5 cm up, 8-15 cm back) and banks by `view_roll_deg`. EyePoint, the hitboxes and the player's bones are not touched. Alternative: moving the player's arm bones. Not taken: the hitboxes read the bones. Alternative: `Camera3D.v_offset` (a lens shift): it moves the arm and the opponent together, as R4-SHOTS found. A raised lens lowers the arm only relative to the lens, and the lens is where the plate's camera sits.
3. **The off-hand is hidden by scaling the forearm bone (0.001) on the drawn skeleton, and put back before every physics tick.** The rig's `_physics_process` runs first (physics priority -1000) and resets the scale, so the game's `_refresh_pose` (hitboxes, weapon grips, attachments) never reads a collapsed pose; the `_process` after the tick hides it again. The off-hand shows when a move's hitboxes name it (a `RightFistPoint` sphere), in a block, a dodge, a grab hold, or while a weapon is held (the grip is the right hand), so a guard or a throw never loses a hand. The weapon shots (t06, t08) hide the left arm instead, since the pipe and the chain are in the right hand. Alternative: hide the arm's mesh by render layer. Not taken: the video art is one skinned mesh (`ArtBody`), with no per-arm surface.
4. **Foreground masses are cards (`CineCard`), not geometry.** A flat polygon in a vertical plane, unlit, one deep colour, with a drift on the shot clock. The t01 fire escape (a rail, two stringers and two balusters) and t14's roof edge (a chimney, a stack and an antenna) are cards. Alternative: modelling a fire escape and a roof edge in Blender. Not taken: no paid or long pipeline for a dark silhouette, and the plate paints the mass anyway; a card has no texture to tile and no normal to shade. The cards' names start `CineCard`, which R5-PAINT's capture does not hide, so a plate keeps the foreground mass.
5. **t14 is multiplane by hiding every section's visual instances (`hide_kit`).** The boss roof's skyline blocks are the street section's (`Sections/Street/Geometry/SkylineL`), so hiding only the shot's own section left 3D buildings. What draws is the world look's own nodes: the fire and smoke mattes, the haze, the embers. The camera trucks 2 m while the card drifts, so the layers move at different speeds.
6. **t01 is a tilt-down from a fixed point 6.6 m up at the street's left side**, ending on the fight in the key lamp's pool: (-4.9, 6.6, 0.2), from a skyline-level aim down to (3, 1, -17.3), FOV 48 -> 44. The street's vanishing point sits off-centre at the left edge, the figures are small (about a fifth of the frame's height), and the barrel and the second lamp are at the right. The cast is four figures in four held poses (a Punk's block, a Thug's stagger, the Veteran's taunt, the Kicker's brace), none live. Alternative: the brief's close, character-led ending. Not taken: the brief says it is not.
7. **t07 moves to the top roof, with the camera on the sun's line.** The sun disc follows the directional light (yaw -73, elevation 15), so its direction is fixed: 17 degrees south of west and 15 up. The camera is at the roof's east edge, 10 cm above the floor, north of the gap between the two water tanks: the near tank (north) is a big foreground silhouette at the right, the far tank is at the left edge, the Kicker and the player stand in front of the disc, and the launch rises into it. Alternative: the catwalk, where it was. Not taken: the sun is behind the camera there, and no tank is between the camera and the sun. The numbers came from a projection check (the sun's direction, the tanks' boxes, the Kicker's box) before any render.
8. **t10-t13: the camera is below the facades.** t10 is a camera fixed at the roof's floor, pitched up 17-19 degrees (a zoom from 48 to 38, not a push-in: the plate holds the camera's position), aimed 6-8 degrees right of the fire so that Ox stands at the left third against the brightest fire; the building at the left is a sliver. t12 is over the player's shoulder from the floor, 0.7 m up, pitched up 18 degrees, so that Ox's face is against the fire and the player's head is the dark foreground mass at the right; no facade is in frame.
9. **The banks alternate down the list:** t03 -5, t04 +5, t05 -4, t06 +4, t07 -5, t08 +5, t09 -5, t11 +5, t12 -6, t13 +4 (degrees); the establishing shots (t01, t10, t14) and the guard-up (t02) are level. The first-person banks go through the view rig (the head's own roll from a hit, the game's, adds to it).

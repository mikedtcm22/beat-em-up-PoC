# LD-ART2: judgement calls (round 3, after Gate 2)

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **A new UV2 layout for all three characters, not padding of the game's.** The Gate 2 holes were triangles of the game's UV2 with no area (a quarter of the player's arm, 2% of Ox's gloves): nothing to bake, so the paint was empty there. The video GLB now carries a fresh unwrap in the same "detail" layer (smart project at 89 degrees, island density weighted by bone through `paint.json` "layout", packed at 4096 with 12 px between islands, then 16 px of padding from the nearest island and the mean colour beyond, alpha = coverage). Alternative: keep the game's layout and only pad and fall back to the swatch. Not taken: the slivers still have no texel of their own, and the arm's density was about 20,000 texels in all. Cost: the game's detail mask no longer matches, so the art rig turns it off (`game_detail_off`); the ink map draws its own creases.
2. **cine_toon falls back to the swatch where the paint's alpha is 0** (the brief's second half of finding 1), as well as the new layout. The fallback is the safety net: `test_ld_art2_data` finds no triangle that samples an empty or black texel.
3. **The player gets a video mesh, and it was never attaching.** `CharacterArt.triangle_budget` is 30,000 and a subdivided player is about 180,000, so `SkinnedArt.entry_of` refused it and the rig silently fell back to the game's mesh with the new paint on the wrong UV2 (that was the camo blotches and the zigzag I chased for an hour). The rig now raises the budget on its own duplicate (`VideoArtLook.triangle_budget`, 250,000; the game's art resource is untouched). Alternative: decimate the legs to stay under 30,000. Not taken: the legs and torso are not in first person, the 180k are video only, and decimation costs the arm's roundness.
4. **The arm: Laplacian relax (24 rounds, arm bones only, cut boundaries held), one level of Catmull-Clark, smoothed normals (30 rounds, 100 degree crease, the Punk's recipe stronger).** Alternative: smooth normals only. Not taken: the silhouette stays an 8-sided tube and the fist a block. Cost: the fists and elbows pull in about 2.5 cm each side (test tolerance 80 mm for the player's box, was 2 mm for the others); the hull normals (outline) are interpolated, not recomputed.
5. **The material borders of the player's vest, skin, wraps and glove plates are cut from smooth vertex fields, not from the faces.** The game's faces are labelled one by one from a decimated mesh, so the armhole and glove borders were staircases of 7-14 mm teeth, magnified 60x in first person. `blender_asset.vertex_fields` diffuses the cell shares over the vertex graph (60 rounds) and `bake_maps` interpolates them over each triangle, so the border is cut where the shares cross: a curve through the triangles. Alternative: relabel faces by majority (also done, `smooth_cells`, and it does not help below face size); a blur of the id map in texture space (tried, the teeth are centimetres wide and the sigma that removes them eats the thin straps).
6. **No curvature shapes on the player's skin and wraps** (`shade_amount`/`light_amount` 0 for cells 7 and 14, 0.35 for the rest). The form-curvature thresholds drew random blotches on the smoothed limb. The tone shapes come from the toon bands on the smooth normals (two or three: light, mid, shadow), as the brief says. Alternative: keep the painted shade and light shapes. Not taken: they are a second set of shapes that disagrees with the lighting.
7. **A warm fill on the arm** (`paint.json` "fill": cells 7 and 14 of the arm bones, B = 0.6 and 0.5): the shader adds a share of the albedo, unlit, in the location's face-fill colour, so the shadow band is a lifted warm tan, not the scene's blue multiplied into salmon (the Gate 2 arm was grey-mauve). The skin is (0.80, 0.60, 0.47), hue about 24 degrees, saturation 0.41; the old one was saturation 0.46 and read pink.
8. **The wraps' stripes became tapered fold lines** (`fold_lines`): 16 short curves per forearm in the (along, around) plane of the limb, slanted 24 degrees plus or minus 14, heavy at the start and tapering to a point, seeded and not evenly spaced. The old spiral lines wrapped round the limb with equal pitch. The skin has 4 faint ones instead of the seven muscle meridians. Alternative: paint the wrap as folded cloth in the albedo. Not taken: there is no cloth geometry to fold; lines on the form are what a drawn wrap is.
9. **The fists' knuckles and fingers are drawn** (`assets/source/video_art/player/features.svg`, over the arm_r render, mirrored for the left): four knuckle arcs, three finger separations tapering up the back of the hand, the first joints. They are small in the frames (the fist is far in most first-person shots), so they are the last thing I would call finished.
10. **Faces: a face zone in the ink map (B) and an unlit fill in cine_toon** (`face_fill`, 0.5, warm; amber for the boss roof), plus catchlights in the ink map's A drawn unlit (`catch_strength`). Alternative: raise the shadow floor for the whole character. Not taken: it would flatten the body; the zone is the head's forward-facing texels from the chin to just under the brim and the headband. The drawn lines stay dark on the lifted face because they are in the albedo. The Punk's lenses are painted dark glass with a streak and a glint (they read as shades from 1 m); Ox's eyes have a warm-grey white, a dark amber iris and a catchlight under each lid.
11. **No plastic specular (`plastic_spec` 0)** on the cap and the rest: the soft highlight is gated by `spec_gain`; the directional glint is gated by the ink map's G (the metal mask from `paint.json` "metal", empty for now), so nothing glints until a cell is named metal. Alternative: keep the glint on the glossy swatches. Not taken: the cap was the glossy swatch.
12. **Sticker blood is off for the video** (`damage_marks` 0, the shader's `marks_gain`). Alternative: draw the blood into the paint. Not taken in this round: the shots we have do not need it, and a painted blood layer needs a per-shot state. `damage_marks = 1.0` brings the game's decals back.
13. **The Punk's cap patch** (cell 2) has no curvature shapes. It showed a jagged dark patch with blue shards (the patch is a thin shell near the cap; the curvature shapes of its small flat triangles were noise).
14. **Ox's hood and glove cells are lifted off near-black**: cell 54 (gloves) base (0.36, 0.22, 0.30), shade (0.22, 0.14, 0.24); cell 50 (hood) base (0.30, 0.23, 0.38). Two or three values, never below about 0.14.
15. **The textures import lossless with mipmaps and no alpha-border fix** (`tools/video/art_import_flags.py`): the ink map's A is data, not transparency, and a drawn line is two texels wide. VRAM: about 90 MB each for the paint and the ink, six maps. A lossy or VRAM-compressed import would save about 400 MB, at the cost of the lines.
16. **Tried and removed:** a shadow switch (the first-person body does not cast, then does not receive shadows) and a wobble gain on the terminator. The zigzag I thought they cured was the mismatched UV2 (call 3); with the right mesh the on and off renders differ in 0.05% of pixels, so the switches are gone.
17. **Test changes (old -> new, with the visible-path twin).** `test_ld_asset_data.gd`: PAINT_SIZE 2048 -> 4096 (the new maps); `GLB_IDS` punk, boss -> punk, boss, player; "the same body, to 2 mm" stays 2 mm for the Punk and Ox and is 80 mm for the player (call 4); `test_the_player_needs_no_video_mesh` (assert_false on the file) -> `test_the_player_has_a_video_mesh_with_smooth_normals` (assert_true), with its twin in `test_ld_art2_data.gd` (the player's GLB obeys the art contract at the raised budget and keeps UV2 inside the atlas); the ink share check is unchanged. The game's `player_art.tres` names its scene only in metadata, so the tests build the game art with `scene_path`.

## Would a viewer who has not been told read it as drawn or as 3D?

- **fp_punk, fp_kicker_lamp (first person):** the arm is the best of these and still leans 3D. For drawn: a round limb in two or three flat tone shapes with a hard terminator, warm skin, a tapered-fold wrap, brush grain, no black. Against: it is one huge, smooth, featureless form (nothing drawn on the upper arm but a few faint lines), the fist is small and far, and the outline is the one weight everywhere. A viewer would hesitate: probably 3D, but a stylised one.
- **ox_closeup:** reads drawn at a glance (a hatched, warm, lined face, painted gloves, no black); the violet cast on the hood and the brick (LD-WORLD3's keys) pulls it toward a render.
- **ox_low:** too small for the face; it reads as a figure with a drawn costume. Drawn, mostly because of the background.
- **moving_punk, close frames:** the Punk's face, cap and shades read as drawn (flat olive, painted glass); the arm is the same as in fp_punk. Probably 3D on the arm, drawn on the Punk.

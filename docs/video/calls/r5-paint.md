# R5-PAINT: judgement calls (round 5: Gemini plates own the background)

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **The capture is a bot that quits late, not a writer.** `video/paint/plate_bot.gd` extends the cine bot, plays the shot to the chosen frame (`--shot-from`/`--shot-to` of one frame) and overrides `_quit()`: with the game's time stopped it hides what the plate must not hold, renders its own SubViewport (the same World3D, 2560x1440, MSAA 4x) from a camera of its own, and writes the files. Alternative: a subclass of the frame writer (`capture_frame` overridden). Not taken: the bot makes the writer in `_run()`, which R5-SHOTS owns; overriding `_quit()` needs no change to `cine_bot.gd`. The cost: the shot's own `<key>.png` is also written (harmless).
2. **The plate camera is the frame's camera with a wider lens.** Position of the live camera at the frame's tick; aim along the mean of the shot's camera keys over its window (a still or first-person shot: the live aim); the horizontal FOV is the widest any sampled frame's corner needs from that axis, plus `margin_deg` (8), at most `max_fov_deg` (120). The same centre of projection maps a frame 1:1 at any FOV, so nothing is resampled but by the plate's own resolution. Alternative: a 360-degree plate or a cube. Not taken: Gemini paints one picture, and the brief says "pan, tilt and zoom about the camera".
3. **Depth is two 8-bit channels, not an EXR.** The depth quad (`plate_depth.gdshader`) writes the 16-bit linear view depth (0..`depth_far_m` 200 m in 65534 steps, 65535 the sky) into red and green, through the inverse sRGB encode so the viewport's output is the byte (the capture camera has its own linear Environment and no post). A PNG loads in Godot without an importer's compression, nearest-sampled, and 16 bits is 3 mm at 200 m, far finer than the 3% + 5 cm test. Alternative: a half-float EXR (needs a float readback). Not taken: Godot has no float viewport readback of a spatial pass here.
4. **The blockout is the paint shader's own switch (`blockout`), not a second shader.** One uniform in `flat_paint_env_body.gdshaderinc`: a mass takes the average of its sheet strip (eight taps down the mips) or of the ground; no windows, brick, slabs, cracks, puddles, grain; the chain-link's alpha is 0. The lamps' pools, the key, the dark masses and the moon's tops are untouched (they are the "designed light kept as soft shapes"), and a prop keeps its own colour. The world look has `blockout` (per run: `--shot-set=world.tres.blockout=true`) and `blockout_paint` (uniform overrides; empty in all three looks). Alternative: a separate blockout material swapped onto the kit. Not taken: it would re-implement the lamp pools. What it leaves: the kit's own geometry (window recesses and sills, rails, cars) is still there, so a facade's windows still stand in a regular row in the blockout; Gemini reads them as a starting point and varied them (call 12).
5. **The thin line on depth edges is a screen quad drawn on the nearer side of a relative step** (`blockout_line.gdshader`, 12% of z, 1.6 px at 1080p), also against the sky. Alternative: an edge filter on the colour. Not taken: depth steps are exactly the "outline of every large mass" the prompt tells Gemini to keep.
6. **What the capture hides:** every CombatActor (the first-person body with it), PickupSpot and loose Weapon, GPU/CPU particles, and by name prefix the steam plumes, embers, flames, contact shadows, lamp shafts, lamp halos and the ink rig's cue nodes. The haze quad stays: the plate carries the aerial perspective. The mattes stay (the far skylines are already paintings). All of it is data in `plate.tres` (`capture_hide_prefixes`, `capture_hide_classes`).
7. **The plate quad: priority MIN and 2990 m down the view,** the brush quad being at 3000: transparent objects of one priority draw far to near, so the plate draws second, over the brushed world, before any haze, steam, flame, cue or the ink quad. Alternative: a higher priority number. Not taken: it would draw after the haze and the ink's quad. A plate pixel is thus never brushed (the plate overwrites it); R5-INK turns the environment lines off (request).
8. **The depth test looks at five texels** (the pixel's and its four neighbours) of the plate's depth: a silhouette edge differs by a texel between the plate and the live view, and a one-texel test turned an outline into a blurred strip. Tolerance as the brief: 3% of z plus 5 cm. Where it fails inside the frame the plate's blurred mip (lod 5) shows; outside the frame the quad discards, so the brush render shows.
9. **The sky is never a "character".** The normal buffer's roughness tag marks a sky pixel like a character (cleared to zero), so the first cut skipped the sky and the sun's painted rings showed through; the sky is tested first.
10. **Live hides on a plated shot:** the haze quad, lamp shafts, lamp halos, and (street, dusk) the matte cards, because the plate carries them; the boss's `plate.tres` leaves the mattes alone (its fire-and-smoke painting drifts, the brief's "drifting smoke matte stays"; the boss plate is for later: if it is painted with its own fire sky, set the prefix in boss_fire/plate.tres). Loose weapons are hidden live too (`hide_loose_weapons_live`): the pilot's roof still had a pipe on the floor, which was the one 3D object the painting did not hold and read as one.
11. **Registration:** ECC (affine) at a quarter size on blurred edge maps, the painting's Canny edges to the blockout's depth edges (a step of 5% for the template, 15% for the strong edges that are measured); the fit is kept only when it lowers the 75th percentile of the distance from the strong edges to the nearest painted edge, and a plate whose 75th percentile is above 8 px is rejected. Pilot numbers: street_wide before 9.6 px, after 6.7; roof_silhouette 7.6, after 6.7 (medians 2.7 and 1.9). Gemini's 16:9 is 1.793 here (not 1.778), stretched onto 2560x1440 before the fit.
12. **The prompt is the brief's, one take each, no retake.** The street plate came back with its windows mostly lit and alike in size (the blockout's windows are the kit's regular recesses): Gemini kept the row and drew 3-5 different lit windows per facade, which is the brief's allowance. Retakes were not needed (the pilot's allowance is four images; two were spent). The roof's painting turned the right tank blue-grey (the blockout's was dark red): the plate wins, it is the picture.
13. **A shot's `plate.tres` is data, the plate files are the switch.** `VideoPlateLook.apply` finds `assets/video/plates/<shot key>/plate_<n>.png` + `depth_<n>.png` + `plate_<n>.json` (n = 0, 1); with none it adds nothing, so a shot with no plate is unchanged. `VIDEO_PLATE_DIR` (an absolute folder with `<key>/` inside) overrides the root, for the motion check. The piece finds the running shot through the `CineDirector` node's `shot` (no change to `cine_director.gd` but the load order).
14. **Test changed (log old -> new):** `test_ld_stage_shots.gd` `test_each_location_names_its_pieces_in_order_art_world_ink`: "the ink piece applies last" -> "the ink piece applies last of the look's own pieces (the plate piece after it)", with the rank of `plate.tres` 3. The visible-path twin is `test_r5_paint_data.gd` `test_every_location_has_a_plate_piece_and_the_director_applies_it_after_the_ink`. No other assertion changed.
15. **The ledger:** `docs/video/gemini_ledger.md` has the pilot's row, written before the batch and its result after: 2 images, $0.28, running total $2.80 (the approved extra $2 has about $1.72 left, about 12 images at $0.14).

## How to make a hero plate (the hand-off to the lead, after the merge)
`tools/video/make_plate.sh <shot key> <output frame | still> --location street|dusk|boss [--swatch <png>] [--index 0|1] [--fov DEG] [--take N]` captures the frame (`captures/video/plates/<key>/`), writes the job (`assets/source/video_plates/<key>/`) and stops. Add the ledger row, then run `python3 -I tools/gemini_image.py captures/video/plates/<key>/gemini/jobs.json plate_<n>_gemini<take>` (or `make_plate.sh ... --run`), look at the painting at full size, then `make_plate.sh ... --install` registers it and installs `assets/video/plates/<key>/plate_<n>.png`, `depth_<n>.png`, `plate_<n>.json`. Once a plate of a location is approved, pass it as `--swatch` for the others of that location so the plates agree. A moving shot's plate is at the frame the lead picks (the hero frame's); the camera of the rest of the shot should pan, tilt and zoom about that position (a first-person shot's bob and sway show as disocclusion strips of blurred plate at the edges, as the motion check's frames do).

## Beside the references (full-resolution crops, 1280x720 of each at native size)
- Against ref 2 at 42.0 s (the camp at night): ours is the same family of paint (flat shapes, an ink line, clustered detail, warm light on a blue-violet night), and reads drawn. It is brighter and cleaner than the reference, whose darks are large near-black masses with a few lit accents and whose ground is rock planes; ours still has an even mid-value over the walls and lit windows in every facade. The lamp heads' glow is a hard bar, not a soft bloom.
- Against ref 2 at 50.5 s (the dune and the sun): the sun is one smooth disc and the sky two long clouds, as the reference; but the reference has a warm glow round the sun and the figure is a pure dark silhouette on a dark mass. Ours has a white disc with almost no glow, and the Kicker is a lit, outlined 3D body (white double outline), which is the first thing the eye reads as 3D.

# LD-INK: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **cine_toon.gdshader is a copy of toon.gdshader's fragment and its own light(), not an include of toon_light.gdshaderinc.** The include defines `light()`, a shader has one, and a processor function's built-ins (NORMAL, LIGHT) are not reachable from a helper, so an include cannot be reused and then changed. Alternative: `#define light ...` to rename the included function (it still would not compile: its body reads built-ins outside a processor function). Every uniform name and type of toon.gdshader and the include is kept, so the swap is in place and `ToonMaterials.apply_look` keeps working.
2. **The shaders are swapped in place on the game's shared materials (`material.shader = ...`), not replaced by new materials.** Alternative: new ShaderMaterials per mesh (the damage look and the flash write to the old ones, which would stop reaching the mesh). In place keeps the atlas, the detail mask, the damage tiles and the contact-flash chain. The cost: the swap is for the whole run (an act that wants the game's own look runs in its own process; the acts take one variant per process).
3. **Point lights add `max(shade - shadow, 0)`, the sun adds the whole shade.** The spike's finding (a point light floods its volume with `shadow_level x colour`). Alternative: set `look_shadow_level` low through Tuning (the spike's way), which darkens the sun's shadow too. A lamp's light term is also multiplied by `point_gain` (2.2) before the bands: a lamp's falloff keeps its term under the cut, so its pool would be all shadow band otherwise.
4. **A directional light lights a character at no less than `dir_floor` (0.42) of full, in its hue.** A dim moon (energy 0.1) lit the paint at 3%, near-black. Alternative: leave the game's cap and ask LD-WORLD for a stronger moon (not mine to change). The floor keeps the hue and lets the world's energy still raise the level.
5. **The painted terminator is object-space brush noise added to the light term plus a band edge of at least `term_soft` pixels, and the shadow filter is raised at runtime** (`RenderingServer` directional and positional soft-shadow quality to ultra, directional atlas 4096, `shadow_blur` at least 2.5 on every shadow caster found). Alternative: noise only. The noise alone made the cast-shadow edge wavy but the shadow map's texel steps stayed; the filter raised the smoothness (checked at 2x zoom). These are RenderingServer settings and Light3D properties of the running scene, not Tuning and not game files. One request for LD-WORLD: set the same on its own lights.
6. **The rim is a shader rim from a key direction (view space, written each frame), plus an optional omni rim light per near character.** The brief allowed either. The rim in the shader needs no light and can be tinted per location; the omni lights give the line a lit shape on the back edges. The key is found from the scene (the point light that lights the nearest character most, else the sun, else the look's fallback direction), so LD-WORLD's lamps are picked up without a name contract. Alternative: a named `Key` light from LD-WORLD (a contract that does not exist yet).
7. **The hull follows the key by vertex normal in the vertex shader** (heavy 1.7x on the shadow side, 0.25x on the lit side, thinning to 0.45x from 5 to 20 m, wandering by object-space noise). Alternative: a screen-space hull in the ink pass (cannot draw outside the silhouette). The cost: the width varies per vertex, so a low-poly mesh gets coarse variation.
8. **Sketch mode chains extra hull passes through `next_pass` on the shared hull material** (jittered by object-space noise re-seeded from the output frame at 12 fps, 0.7x width). Alternative: three separate overlay meshes. The chain is one material and costs no nodes. It does not overshoot corners (a hull cannot): the doubled, offset line is the stroke feel, not true overshoot. Reported as a limit.
9. **Hatching is a code-made tonal art map (256x256, four cumulative densities in the RGBA channels, every stroke with its own length, gap, wobble and taper from a hash) sampled triplanar in the rest-pose space.** Alternative: a screen-space hatch (it swims) or a UV hatch (the art's UVs are palette swatches, not a layout). It fades in as the nearest non-player character comes inside `hatch_near_m` (1.6 m) and is gone past 3.2 m; `hatch_force` pins it.
10. **The 2D cues are switches in the look, each default on**, so a Gate 2 A/B is an `INK_SET` of the game's set (`smears=false;hide_comic_fx=false;flash_frames=false;speed_lines=false;on_twos=false`). The comic words and starbursts are hidden by hiding the VfxDirector (all `ComicSprite`s are its children) every frame; alternative `vfx_scale = 0` through Tuning (it also kills blood and splats, which the references do have).
11. **Characters on twos holds bone poses, not the body's transform.** Alternative: also hold the character's world position (a visual offset node per rig). The simulation moves bodies every tick and "physics is never touched"; a held pose on a moving body can slide a foot by a few cm for one frame. The frame writer sets `CineInkRig.frame_source` (a Callable returning the output frame index); without one the frame is counted from the physics frames at 24 fps.
12. **Smears are a camera-facing ribbon through the last 7 positions of the fists and the right foot, shown while the limb is fast and for 20 ticks after an attack starts.** Alternative: a stretched copy of the limb mesh (the true anime smear). The ribbon fades and narrows inside 1.5 m of the lens so the player's own fist in first person never fills the frame. In first person the player's smear is therefore almost absent; the speed lines carry it.
13. **Flash frames and speed lines are one full-screen quad in the 3D scene (cine_cue_screen.gdshader)** driven by `CombatEvents.hit_landed` (finisher; heavy with `flash_on_heavy`), seeded from the output frame at 12 fps. Alternative: the game's CanvasLayer pair (never reaches the 2560x1440 SubViewport). It is not in the character mask (a flash covers everything).
14. **The finish pass is a CompositorEffect with two compute dispatches** (a temp image, then back). The colour texture has no `CAN_COPY_FROM`, so a `texture_copy` of it failed; reading it with offsets and writing a temp, then copying back with an image store, needs neither. It runs after the transparent pass on the resolved colour, before tonemapping. A camera's own `compositor` wins over the world's, so the rig also assigns it to every camera in the tree (the grab's SubViewport camera among them) and exposes `compositor` for the frame writer.
15. **The paper grain is two octaves of value noise at about 1.6 px, seeded by a constant, fixed to the frame, a few percent of value, less in the darks.** Alternative: white noise per pixel (reads as digital sensor noise, not paper). No other screen-space noise on fills.
16. **The palette LUTs are the spike's Reinhard-per-band transfer at strength 0.7, one per location, from one reference frame each** (ref 2 at 42.0 for the street, ref 2 at 50.5 for the roofs, ref 1 at 5.3 for the boss) against our render under the ink look without a LUT. Strengths 0, 0.4 and 0.7 were scored with the style meter: 0.7 was best on the boss (0.94 against 0.89) and equal elsewhere. Alternative: Pitie's transfer (not built). The LUT files are committed through LFS under `assets/video/ink/`.
17. **The stand-in world look is the spike's night light and flat paint** (`CineSpikeLook`, stages b and c), as the brief says, until LD-WORLD's is on the branch. The acts stage a shot as `VideoStillBot.shoot()` does (calling its helpers) with a hook between the staging and the grab, because the stage has none; I did not edit the stage. Ox's close-up uses the same night rig with the `boss_fire` ink look: it is judged for the character look, not the boss roof's light.
18. **Tuning of the line: `hull_px` 3.2 (1080p units, 4.3 px at 2560x1440).** The look sweep (hull 3.4 and 4.4, heavy 4.0, thin 1.6, depth threshold 0.04) moved the style meter's ink share only from 0.0012 to 0.0014: against the stand-in world the characters are small and mostly dark, so ink on dark fill does not register as ink. Alternative: far heavier lines, which would read as a filter. The remedy is lit fills from LD-WORLD, not more ink.

## Limits and what is not done
- The sketch mode's overshoot is a doubled, offset stroke, not true corner overshoot.
- Hand-drawn features and inner lines on faces are LD-ASSET's (`ink_tex`, `paint_tex` are declared in cine_toon and default off).
- Glints: the game's per-swatch gloss (the Punk's lens, studs) stays the metal-only rule; no new glint shape.
- The silhouette of the screen-space ink pass is per-pixel (1 to 2 px stair-steps at 2560x1440 at 2x zoom); the video's downscale hides them. A coverage-based line would fix it.
- The Kuwahara filter on the matte layers is not here: the mattes are LD-WORLD's cards and the plan lists the filter as optional on them.

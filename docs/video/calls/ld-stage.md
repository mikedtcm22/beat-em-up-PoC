# LD-STAGE: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why. (The cine stage and the frame writer: `video/cine/`, `video/shots/`, `video/stage/cine_bot.gd`, `tools/video/shoot.sh`.)

1. **A shot scene is a bare Node running `cine_bot.gd` with its shot as an exported resource; the shot is data (`VideoStillShot` for the six Gate 1 stills, `CineShot` for moving shots).** Alternative: a scene that instances the mission-1 level and a `CineDirector` node, as PLAN.md §6 words it. The level is built in code by the bots' harness (the scripted player, `_reset`, the navigation wait), so a scene that instanced it would copy that harness; the bot is the harness. `CineDirector` is a node of the bot's scene all the same, and applies the pieces, hides the HUD and seeds the rngs. A still plays as a one-frame shot, so the six scenes cost nothing.
2. **`CineShot extends VideoStillShot`.** Alternative: a new resource that wraps a still. Extending lets every staging helper of the still stage (`_place_actor`, the lamps, the player pin, the warm-up) take a `CineShot` unchanged, and keeps the other packages' acts (which call `bot.shoot(still)`) working: `still_bot.gd` only gained `stage()` (the old `shoot()` is `stage()` plus the grab; same behaviour), `_place_free_camera()` and `stage_lamps`, and `VideoStillActor` gained `live` (default false = the old held pose).
3. **The shot clock is the physics-frame count since the staging ended (`CineDirector.start_clock`), 60 ticks a second; beats and camera keys are in seconds on it.** Alternative: wall time, or the bot's own counter. The physics-frame count is what `--fixed-fps 60` makes exact, it is what `Engine.get_physics_frames()` gives the ink rig, and it never reads `TIME`.
4. **The window is [from, to): 5 s is 120 frames.** The first build wrote 121 (an inclusive end). A window's first tick is always a pick, and the picks keep the clock's index `floor(t * 24 / 60)`, so a window that starts late still agrees with the ink rig's on-twos holds and boil (the bot sets `CineInkRig.frame_source` from it).
5. **The main window keeps drawing every tick (a 480x270 window); only the two 2560x1440 SubViewports are disabled off the picks.** The brief says the window's own viewport is disabled outside the window. Alternative: disable the main viewport with `RenderingServer.viewport_set_update_mode`, which would save the small window's cost. I kept it on because GPU particles (embers, steam, the world look's and the game's sparks) advance only on frames some viewport draws them; with the 24-in-60 skipping they would run at 0.4x speed. `CineFrameWriter.show_window = false` (`--shot-window=0`) turns it off for a look that has no particles. Cost: a 480x270 render each tick, about 5 ms.
6. **The mask is a second SubViewport with its own camera that copies the first, `cull_mask` = one extra render layer (layer 4), a transparent background and an empty `Environment` override (clear colour).** The game has no characters-only layer (everything is layer 1), so while a frame is captured the writer ORs layer 4 into every mesh of `HumanoidRig.ART_GROUP` that the main camera would draw (so the player's head, on the layer the first-person camera never draws, is left out) and clears it otherwise. Alternative: a copy of every character mesh on a mask layer. The layer is free in the game (`test_the_mask_layer_is_not_a_layer_the_game_uses`). The mask camera has an empty `Compositor`, so the ink rig's finish pass (grain, vignette) never reaches it; it is written as RGBA PNGs and encoded as lossless gray (`alphaextract`).
7. **Frames are written as one raw RGB chunk file (`store_png = false`, the default) and encoded every 200 frames; PNG storage stays as a switch (`--shot-store=png`).** The brief says PNGs. Godot's `save_png` costs 0.75 s a frame at 2560x1440 (the render is 0.15 s), so a 960-frame video would spend 12 minutes just compressing. Raw RGB is the same pixels (the two stores give identical SHA-256 lines, checked) and the encode reads it with `-f rawvideo`. Cost: a 200-frame chunk is 2.1 GB on disk until its encode ends (89 GB free here); `chunk_frames` is an export. The mezzanine is `libx264 -crf 8 -preset slow yuv444p`, converted with the BT.709 matrix, tagged BT.709 limited range (an untagged one is shown with the wrong matrix by some players).
8. **The two SubViewports are warmed up through the staging (drawn every frame), then idled.** The first draw of a pipeline at a size can use Godot's ubershader while the specialised one compiles. It did not remove the render noise below, but a first frame that differs from the second would be worse, and it costs only the staging frames.
9. **Seeds: `CineDirector.seed_rngs` sets `VfxDirector.rng.seed` and `AudioDirector.rng.seed` to the shot's `rng_seed` after the level is built; the bot starts every shot through the still bot's `_reset` path.** Alternative: `call_deferred` from the director's `_ready`, as PLAN.md words it: the level's presentation nodes exist when the bot runs, so the direct call is simpler and happens before the staging.
10. **`--shot-skip=` and `--shot-set=` leave a piece out or set a property of a duplicate of it for one run** (`--shot-set=ink.tres.post_enabled=false;...`). Alternative: edit the .tres for each A/B. The lead's Gate 2 A/Bs (the 2D cues on and off) and the look sweeps need a run per variant; the .tres files stay untouched.
11. **Piece discovery is by file name per location: `art*.tres` (by name), `world.tres`, `ink.tres`, applied in that order.** Alternative: a list in each shot. LD-ASSET delivers `video/look/<location>/art*.tres` (its brief) and nothing here has to change when it lands; a location without an art file just applies two pieces.
12. **The camera path is a Catmull-Rom spline through the keys' positions and look-at points with the keys' own timing, eases per segment (smoothstep or linear), linear FOV, roll and focus; depth of field is Godot's `CameraAttributesPractical` driven by the focus keys.** Alternative: one Bezier per shot. The spline does not stop at each key and the shot reads the keys as a list. A key with no focus next to one with a focus takes its neighbour's so the blur does not pop in. The FOV is horizontal (`KEEP_WIDTH`), like the game's first-person lens and the stills.
13. **The still stage's `VideoStillGrab` copies the active camera's `attributes`** (the depth of field) onto its SubViewport camera, one line in `still_grab.gd`; `CineFrameWriter extends VideoStillGrab`, so the bot's `grab` is the writer.
14. **The moving shot (`moving_punk`): the street, the Punk standing 3.1 m from the player a little left of the key lamp, the player walking up (stick forward from 0.15 to 0.65 s), then P at 1.0, 1.4, 1.8 and 2.2 s: jab, jab, hook, uppercut finisher (the finisher launches him backward as a ragdoll and the game's slow-motion and the ink piece's flash frame fire).** Alternative: launching him into the chain-link: the fence is 14 m away, and the finisher's launch carries him back along the street toward it. He is a live enemy (`VideoStillActor.live`) with the AI frozen. The Gate 2 brief says "P P P P on a Punk under a street lamp".
15. **`crane_street` is an extra shot (PLAN.md §5 shot 1: the crane down, three Punks idle).** Not asked for, built to exercise the camera path and the depth of field through the writer. The camera starts at z = 2 m: a start at z = 5 m was inside the street's end wall and rendered black for 22 frames.
16. **The writer's numbers are in `<shot>_stats.json`** (`seconds_per_frame`, `png_mb_per_frame` = raw MB, `mezzanine_mb_per_frame`, `video_memory_mb`, `static_memory_mb`).

## How to run it
- A shot: `tools/video/shoot.sh moving_punk [out dir]` (default `captures/video/shots/<shot>/`). The shots are `video/shots/*.tscn`: the six Gate 1 stills (`fp_punk`, `fp_kicker_lamp`, `street_wide`, `ox_closeup`, `ox_low`, `roof_silhouette`: a PNG and its mask), `moving_punk` and `crane_street` (a `.mkv` and `_mask.mkv`). It takes the capture lock; one process; never headless.
- Variants: `SHOOT_ARGS="--shot-skip=ink.tres"`, `--shot-set=ink.tres.post_enabled=false`, `--shot-to=<s>` (a short look), `--shot-from=<s>`, `--shot-mask=0`, `--shot-tag=<text>`, `--shot-repeat=N` (a still captured N more times: the renderer's own determinism probe), `--shot-store=png`.
- Determinism: render a shot twice, then `tools/video/compare_shots.sh <dir A> <dir B> <shot>`: it counts the bit-identical frames from the SHA-256 files and gives the PSNR of each pair through ffmpeg.
- A quick look at the staging through the still bot: `tools/video/shoot_still.sh "Stage:" <out dir>`.
- The tests: `godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s addons/gut/gut_cmdln.gd -gdir=res://video/tests -gexit` (34 are LD-STAGE's: the pick math, the encode arguments, the camera path, the shot data, the director).

## What the determinism check found
- **The stage and the simulation are deterministic.** With no look piece (`--shot-skip=ink.tres,world.tres`), two 5 s renders of `moving_punk` have 120 of 120 mask frames bit-identical, 105 of 120 picture frames bit-identical, and the others at 61.5 dB or more. The differing frames start at the finisher's impact (the game's GPU-particle sparks and blood).
- **With the world piece only** 90 of 120 picture frames are bit-identical (lowest 51.3 dB), the masks 120 of 120: the world look's GPU particles (LD-WORLD noted the same, "at most 7 levels in a few particle pixels").
- **With the ink piece, the renders are not bit-identical**: 24 of 120 pictures, 107 of 120 masks, 50.5 dB lowest and 53.7 dB mean. Not visible (under 8 levels, 0.13 % of the pixels in a still), but not "pixel for pixel". Isolated: a still captured four times in one process with the ink piece gives four different images, and with the game's own toon shader (no ink piece) one image. Switching off every cue (screen ink, rim lights, post, twos, smears, flash, speed lines, hatching, LUT, shadow filter), freezing the rig's `_process`, and freeing its children each left it. What remains is the two shaders it swaps in (`cine_toon.gdshader`, `cine_outline.gdshader`) or Godot's handling of them. I did not find the cause; it is LD-INK's (request below).

## Where it falls short
- The render is not bit-reproducible with the ink piece (above). The video's downscale and encode hide it.
- The crane shot's first frames look down from 8.6 m: the street's end wall and its roofs are the round 1 world's. Round 2 (LD-WORLD2) is not on this branch yet.
- The moving shot's Punk is dead by 2.2 s and the remaining 2.5 s are his ragdoll flying and lying: a 3 s shot cut would use less. The window is data (`window_to_s`).
- The depth of field is Godot's, a smooth Gaussian-like blur, and `dof_amount` / `dof_transition_share` are the camera's own exports; the Phase 2 shots decide how much to use.
- No speed ramp, no handheld shake path, no audio: not this package's.

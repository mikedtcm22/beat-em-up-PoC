# LD-WORLD: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **The paint shader's body moved into `flat_paint_env_body.gdshaderinc`; three thin shaders include it** (`flat_paint_env` the opaque kit material, `flat_paint_env_scissor` the chain-link, `flat_paint_prop` the video-only props). Alternative: one shader file. The chain-link needs `ALPHA_SCISSOR_THRESHOLD`, and a shader that writes it loses the depth prepass for every opaque face (the game split `trim_scissor` off for the same reason), and the lamp posts and barrels need a flat colour in place of the trim sheet. The spike's test read the uniform names from `flat_paint_env.gdshader` alone; it now reads that file plus the include. Old assertion -> new: the same names must be declared, in the main file or its body (no assertion weakened; `test_ld_world_look.gd` adds twin assertions for all three variants).
2. **The look is data in dictionaries (`paint`, `sky`, `haze`, `environment`) plus typed resources for the lists (lights, emitters, mattes).** Alternative: typed exports for every uniform. A dictionary keeps a `.tres` a short, editable file and makes the paint shaders' uniforms the one list; `test_every_shader_value_names_a_uniform` checks every key against the shader, and `apply_uniforms` warns on a typo at run time.
3. **The lamps are spot lights with a 140 degree cone (70 half-angle), the key lamp casts the shot's one shadow, and the moon (the sun node) casts none.** Alternatives: an omni lamp (its light climbed the whole wall: a tall orange "spire" on the right wall), or the moon as the caster (a second set of long shadows, which the blind check named as a tell). A spot with a wide cone lights the ground and the lower wall, not the wall above the lamp. The key lamp's shadow is the Punk's cast shape in the pool, like the target.
4. **A lamp pool is a hard-edged shape made in `light()` of the paint shader** (two thresholds on `ndl * attenuation`, edge roughened by a low frequency noise, `pool_norm` so the lit ground is a bright sodium colour whatever the asphalt's value). Alternative: a light projector texture (a cookie). The shader route needs no texture and follows the geometry, so the same pool bends over a kerb and up a wall; a cookie would be a decal on a regular circle.
5. **`pool_norm`: the lamp's contribution is divided by the albedo's luma, so a dark ground still shows a bright pool.** Alternative: raise `pool_gain` (the toon cap of 1 per channel, and the ground at 0.15 albedo, left a muddy salmon). Cost: a pool no longer darkens where the paint is dark; it reads as paint (a tint laid over the ground), which is the target.
6. **Brick is flattened to its average colour (`brick_flat`) and returns only as a few dashes near a pool's edge.** Alternative: the game's sheet at a higher mip (the sheet is a stack of 128 px rows, so past mip 4 one strip bleeds into the next). PLAN.md part 7 asks for exactly this, and it removes the blind check's "brick tiled mechanically" tell.
7. **Windows: a world-hashed blank or lit state per window cell, a per-floor sideways slide (`row_jitter_m`) and a low-frequency domain warp of the sheet's coordinates.** Alternative: displace vertices (the walls are boxes with 8 corners, so a vertex warp only skews them). The three together break the grid without cutting a window: the slide is seamless because the strip wraps in U, and the blank swaps a window's pixels for the wall's own colour.
8. **The exact depth fog is off; flat haze bands from a screen-space depth quad replace it** (`haze_band.gdshader`: three distance bands and one low mist band, edges bent by a world-anchored noise). Alternative: keep Godot's fog at a low density. A smooth fog is the "fog that fades exactly with depth" tell. Volumetric fog for lamp shafts is left out: the spike showed it as a smooth gradient, and the brief allows it only if it reads painted.
9. **Godot's glow is off; the halo is a billboard card with banded rings** (`haze_halo.gdshader`), and the sky's sun or moon has banded rings in the sky shader. Alternative: glow with a high threshold. The glow's Gaussian is the smooth bloom blob the blind check called 3D.
10. **The sky is a `shader_type sky` material (no time input), the disc follows the Sun light's direction (`sky_disc_follows_sun`).** Alternative: a skybox texture. The shader needs no asset and the disc always agrees with the key light's shadows.
11. **Mattes are RGBA PNGs painted by `tools/video/paint_mattes.py` and hung on cards in section space.** Four: a night skyline, a dusk skyline, a smoke bank, a fire wall (each 2048 px wide, 11-75 KB, LFS). The painter has a four-sector Kuwahara on these layers only. Alternative: Kuwahara in a screen pass (it would swim).
12. **The look writes four Tuning values at run time, through `Tuning.set_value` and never to a preset:** `section_light_enabled = false` (GAME_NOTES gotcha 10), `look_shadow_level` low (the toon light floods a point light's whole volume), `look_grade_enabled = false` (the game's dusk grade; the palette LUT is LD-INK's), and for the roofs `look_fill_strength = 0` (so a character against the sun is a silhouette). Alternative: swap the characters' shader (LD-INK's). Reported in "requests".
13. **A second look replaces the first on the same level** (`apply` removes the old rig and clears the paint uniforms the old look set and the new one does not). Alternative: one look per process. The stills run all six shots in one process, and a shot list will switch location, so this keeps `apply` honest.
14. **The act hides the game's edge pass by default (`--world-edges` keeps it).** The ink is LD-INK's; the world's paint is judged without the game's constant-width ruler lines, which are one of the blind check's tells. The characters keep their hull outline.
15. **Embers, steam and dust are GPU particles with a fixed seed, `fixed_fps = 60` and a preprocess,** their sprites made in code (`VideoWorldSprites`, hashed, byte-identical on every run). Alternative: CPU sprites. The fire's flicker is re-rolled from the physics frame number at 12 fps, never from time.

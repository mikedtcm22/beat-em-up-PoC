# P0-SPIKE: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **Stages are cumulative and rendered in one process** (a, then b, c, d, e, nothing undone). Alternative: one process per stage. One process is 8 s a run and, with `TimeController.debug_scale = 0` freezing the world, a-d were pixel-identical between two runs (max difference 0). The LUT needs d.png first, so `video/spike/make_strip.sh` renders a-d, builds the LUT, then renders a-e again.
2. **The player holds his guard (block) for the fists.** Alternative: a jab mid-flight, or idle (idle shows no hands in first person). The guard is a stable pose and puts both gloves in the near foreground; the cost is two large forearms that fill the top corners.
3. **The lamp is an OmniLight3D at energy 1.0, not brighter.** The toon `light()` caps each channel of a light at 1, so a lamp above 1.0 loses its hue (at energy 3 the orange went cream). Alternative: SpotLight3D (a cone); omni gives the wall and floor pool a lamp has. Strength is set by range and attenuation (0.8, so the pool is a plateau with a hard band edge).
4. **`look_shadow_level` is lowered to 0.07 with the night light (Tuning runtime write, no preset).** The toon `light()` adds `shadow_level x light colour` inside the whole volume of every light, so a point light flooded its whole range orange. Alternative: replace the characters' toon shader with a copy (outside the four things the brief allows). The paint shader does the same job for the environment (a point light adds no shadow value there).
5. **The game's dusk grade is switched off (`look_grade_enabled = false`) from stage b.** Its tint is a dusk's, and stage e's LUT replaces it. Alternative: compose the LUT on the game's grade (`palette_lut.py --pre-lut`, kept as an option). Off is simpler and the LUT statistics are then the raw render's.
6. **The screen ink replaces the game's edge pass (its quad is hidden) instead of stacking on it,** and the characters' hull is thinned to 1.3 px (from 3.75) and tinted to the ink. Alternative: keep both. Stacked, the constant-width lines would hide the light-weighted ones. The hull stays because the screen pass cannot draw outside a silhouette.
7. **Line weight follows the light from three sources** (the lamp and the rim light as positions, the moon as a direction; the best of them), with the lamp's position taken per pixel from the depth. Alternative: one key direction as the brief literally says. Positions give the right answer on a surface near a lamp, where the direction differs across the frame.
8. **The palette LUT is Reinhard per L* band, split into a warm and a cool cluster, with a hue-turn cap of 10 degrees and strength 0.7.** Alternative: Pitie's distribution transfer (not built: the regularised iterations were costlier to make smooth, and Reinhard banded cleanly, see the report). A single mean per band turned the orange lamp magenta (the references' warm pixels are redder than a sodium lamp), hence the clusters and the cap. The L* curve is the histogram match blended 0.5, faded out toward white so the lamp's bloom has no cliff.
9. **The flat-paint shader is a full copy with its own `light()`,** not a posterise on top of the game's light. Alternative: posterise only the albedo. The painted terminator (a stroke noise shifted into the light term before the band step) needs the light function.
10. **Glow is tuned at 2560x1440, not at the window's 1280x720.** Godot's glow is not resolution independent (the halo is half as wide on screen at 2560). The SubViewport is the product, the window is only the check (see the report).
11. **LUT and capture paths:** the LUT lives under `captures/video/p0-spike/lut/` and is read with `Image.load_from_file` (captures/ is Godot-ignored, git-ignored). A later phase should commit a LUT under `assets/` through LFS once the look is chosen.

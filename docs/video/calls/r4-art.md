# R4-ART: judgement calls (round 4)

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **The terminator's noise is removed from the shader, not set to 0.** `term_wobble` stays declared (the ink piece writes it) and is unused; `paint_noise` and the noise on the light term are gone, so a test (`test_r4_art_data`) can assert the code. Alternative: ship `term_wobble = 0` in the ink look. Not taken: that is R4-INK's file, and a zero gain leaves the tell in the code.
2. **The designed shadow is a map, not a rule in the shader: `<id>_shade.png`, R = the hint.** The hint is `smoothstep(from, to, n . light)` with a designed light per bone group in `paint.json` "shade" (a top-front-left light for heads: the undersides of the nose, the brow, the cheekbone and the chin go dark; for arms, a light from up and outside, so the underside and the inner side go dark), plus the concave form (the same curvature as the paint) on heads, blurred over the island. The shader subtracts `shade_hint_gain * R` from the light term before the bands. Alternative: a shader rule from the normal in object space. Not taken: the skinned normal moves with the pose, and the arm's "underside" is a rest-space property. Cost: the arm's hint is a rest-space side, so it is the same side whatever the pose (judged in the two first-person shots only, where the lower side reads as shadow; a pose that rolls the forearm would put the hint on the top, which I did not check).
3. **Hatching is baked strokes (G coverage, B depth threshold), not a texture sampled triplanar.** `art_shade.strokes` cuts each stroke from the 3D position of the texel: planes parallel to a direction (the forearm's bone axis; vertical-ish for the face) at 3-4 mm, tapered segments with gaps, a wander along the axis, a random depth threshold per segment, and only inside the hint's zone. They run along the form and are continuous over UV seams. The shader draws a stroke only where `1 - lit / band_2` is deeper than its threshold, so strokes come in as the shadow deepens and never touch a lit face (depth 0), and it fades them with the pixel's distance to the lens (`hatch_near_m` 1.4 m, `hatch_far_m` 3 m) as well as the ink piece's `hatch_on`. Alternative: keep round 3's tonal art map and only orient it. Not taken: one density over everything was the tell. `hatch_gain` multiplies `hatch_on`; the ink piece's `hatch_*` values are untouched.
4. **Depth is measured from the mid band's edge (`band_2`), not the shadow band's (`band_1`).** First version used `band_1`: on the first-person arm the designed shadow lands in the mid band (the moon lights it), so no stroke ever showed (found by writing the strokes and the depth to the specular term). Strokes are in the directional light's pass only (a lamp's pass adds nothing in its shadow band).
5. **`hatch_floor_by_id` (player: 1).** The ink piece sets `hatch_on` from the nearest figure's distance, so the player's own arm got none in a shot with the Punk at 4 m. The floor lives in the art look. Alternative: ask R4-INK to exempt the player's art. Not taken: a request for a one-line floor is not worth a merge dependency.
6. **Ox's face: no surface-crease lines on the Head and Neck (`crease_skip_bones`), flat skin (cell 7: `shade_amount` and `light_amount` 0), and the drawn lines cut from 54 to 20 paths.** Removed: the secondary brows, the second furrow, the bags, the crease and the lower lid on the far eye, the far side of the nose, the corners, the far fold, the cheek lines, the dimple, and all 32 stubble strokes. Kept: both heavy brows, the lids, the irises and their paint, the catchlights, one furrow, the near side of the nose and the wings, the nostrils, the mouth, one fold and the lower lip. Added: five flat shadow shapes in the ink (the sockets, the far side of the nose, the cheekbone's crescent, the jaw), with the jaw's stubble coming from the baked hatching instead. `hint_gain` for Ox is 0.75 (the face fill lifts the shadow band, so the hint has to push harder).
7. **Ox's gloves differ by marks in `paint.json` "marks"** (`art_shade.marks`): the right glove has three bands of cloth tape at the wrist and light scuffs; the left has a stitched leather patch on the back of the hand and heavier scuffs, at another angle. Alternative: draw in `features.svg` over a blender view. Not taken: the glove's orientation in the front view is hidden, and a mark placed at "the extreme point of the bone in a direction" is robust. The player's two fists carry different scuffs on the glove cells for the same reason (the knuckle lines still come from the mirrored `features.svg`, heavier now: 2.7, 2.2, 1.6 mm).
8. **The first-person forearm's silhouette is made in Blender (`shape_limbs`, `paint.json` "shape"):** a radial scale about each forearm's bone axis, a swell of 6% near the elbow end (Gaussian, centre 0.3, width 0.3 of the bone), a taper to 0.78 at the wrist from 0.5 of the bone, and the hand's vertices easing back to 1 by 1.2 bones (the fist is wider than the wrist). Gated by the arm bones' weights. Before the Catmull-Clark, so it rounds. First try was a 12% swell: the arm was a bigger tube at the lens, so 6%. Alternative: a displacement in the shader. Not taken: the silhouette is the thing the judges read, and the outline hull follows the mesh only if the mesh changes.
9. **The arm's value:** `value_by_id` player 0.7 (night), 0.8 (dusk, the boss roof), the albedo multiplier on the shared material; `rim_gain_by_id` 1.4 so the warm rim from the lamp stays the arm's brightest part; the face-fill stays the lead's 0.15 at night. The arm is a mid-dark purple-brown with a warm top plane. The player's glove cells (32, 33, 35) were near-black navy and were lifted to mid lilac and mid blue, so the knuckle lines read.
10. **Shade map sizes:** 4096 for Ox and the player (strokes and close-ups), 2048 for the Punk (a hint only, no strokes). VRAM is about 90 MB a 4096 map with mipmaps. If memory presses on the shared machine, `paint.json` "shade" "size" 2048 halves it. Alternative: 2048 for all. Not taken: the strokes are 1 mm wide and need the texels.
11. **Test changes (old -> new, with the visible-path twin).** None of the existing assertions were weakened. The one test that checked the shader's source for the swatch fallback (`painted.a`) still passes. My own `test_r4_art_data.gd` asserts the absence of the noise terminator and the triplanar hatch in the shader source, with its visible-path twin (the shade maps carry a hint and strokes with several thresholds, and the rig sets the shade uniforms).
12. **The 75-degree lens judged with scratch shots** (`first_person_fov_deg = 75` copies of fp_punk and fp_kicker_lamp, kept out of git because `video/stills/` is counted by other packages' tests). `video/acts/r4_art_acts.gd` builds the same variants in code for the still stage.

## The tells (the round 3 blind check's eight), per shot: removed and remaining

- **Tell 4 (shading by shader):** removed in all five shots: no noise terminator, no triplanar crosshatch, a designed hint, strokes along the form only in close shadow. Remaining: the arm's terminator is a smooth curve across a smooth form (it reads as lit, not drawn) and the strokes on the arm are sparse.
- **Tell 5 (CG primitives and copies):** Ox's gloves differ (removed). The arm is still one big smooth tube with a mitten fist: partly removed (wrist taper, forearm swell, a darker value, the lilac glove), not gone. The fist is a ball from behind: the knuckles and fingers were drawn for the front and the outer face, not the camera's view, so they hardly show. The first thing I would do next.
- **Tell 6 (even outline weight):** not mine (R4-INK).
- **Others (1, 2, 3, 7, 8):** the world's and the ink's.

## Would a viewer who has not been told read it as drawn or as 3D?

- **fp_punk, fp_kicker_lamp (75 degrees):** the arm is now a foreground silhouette (mid-dark, a warm top plane, two or three flat values, a few strokes), and the Punk is small. Reads closer to drawn than round 3; a judge would still say 3D on the arm's perfect smoothness and the ball of a fist. Honest call: 3D, with less certainty.
- **ox_closeup:** the face is flat tones with a few designed lines and shadow shapes; the gloves differ and a tape wrap reads. It is a drawn head on a lit 3D hood and body; the old man's wrinkles are gone but the face is still an unusually realistic proportion. Honest call: drawn at a glance, 3D when looked at (the hood's and the gloves' smooth shading).
- **ox_low:** a dark silhouette against painted fire: drawn, as in round 3.
- **moving_punk, close frames:** the Punk's cap, shades and mask are drawn; his jacket and arms are lit by smooth shader bands. Probably 3D.

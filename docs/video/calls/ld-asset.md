# LD-ASSET: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why.

1. **The painted albedo is made from the game's palette swatches (the 6 to 7 materials of each character), not from the Tripo texture.** Each swatch becomes a material with a base, a shade and a light value, the shade in the concave places and the light on the convex ones, as hard-edged shapes. Alternative: bake the dense textured Tripo mesh into the layout (the pipeline's cache that registers it to the game mesh is not in the repo; rebuilding it is a multi-step run of the whole chain for three characters). The swatches are that texture's colours already, quantised per face, so nothing of the design is lost; the look gets the 2-3 values PLAN.md asks for.
2. **Shapes come from a curvature built from the shading normals (`form_curvature`), not from the mesh's dihedral angles.** The first version thresholded the dihedral curvature and drew ruler-straight polygons across the coarse triangles (the paint-over lesson: mechanical regularity reads as 3D). The normals are smooth over each triangle, so the thresholds wander. The dihedral curvature is kept for the crease LINES, where a sharp edge is what is wanted. Thresholds are percentiles per character, so a mesh's own curvature scale does not matter.
3. **Features are drawn in the front view's millimetres and projected onto the surface into UV space; the UV-space SVG is written out.** The brief says "SVG in UV space". The layout is scattered islands of a few hundred texels (see player_tex), where a face is in pieces and a hand-drawn line cannot be placed by eye. Alternative: draw straight on the UV layout. Instead `art_features.py` samples each stroke every 0.3 mm, finds the surface texel the view sees there (with the sub-texel offset solved from the neighbours), and draws in the layout with the width in millimetres turned into texels where it falls. `features_uv.svg` is the drawing as it sits in the layout.
4. **The ink mask is 4096 and the paint 2048.** The face's lines are 1.2 to 2.6 mm; a 4096 map has 0.8 mm texels. 8192 would be 64 MB a character. At the close-up distance a line is still soft-edged (bilinear magnification of about 3 screen pixels a texel): reported as a limit.
5. **Every line is warped by a smooth noise and faded in and out by another** (creases, borders, the wraps), and every stroke wanders by a seeded noise (0.2 to 0.4 mm). Alternative: clean lines. The Phase 0 blind check called ruler-clean lines a 3D tell.
6. **The face normals come from Blender's Data Transfer modifier from a UV sphere scaled to the head's box** (custom normals, nearest face interpolated), weighted by the Head bone's weight times the front of the head, at 0.85. Alternative: an analytic ellipsoid gradient in numpy. The brief names the proxy and the modifier. The weight keeps the hood's and the cap's own folds at the sides and the back. The player gets none (no head in first person).
7. **The video GLB is exported by `blender_export.export_glb` from the work file** (no import round trip), so an unchanged mesh is byte-identical to the shipped GLB (checked for the player). The player therefore has no video GLB: `CineArtRig` falls back to the game's mesh with the textures only. Alternative: ship a duplicate 2 MB file.
8. **`paint_tex` and `ink_tex` are read with UV2, by editing the loaded shader's source at runtime (`CineArtRig.ensure_layout_uv`).** `cine_toon.gdshader` (LD-INK's) samples them with `UV`, which is the palette's swatch coordinate (the UV2 is the layout the game's detail mask and mine are in). I may not edit that file; see "requests". The edit is idempotent, and a no-op once the shader is fixed. A static reference keeps the edited Shader alive (the cache would drop it and reload the original from disk).
9. **The player's wraps and arms get procedural lines, not drawn ones.** In first person the arm is large and near, and the layout cannot be drawn on by eye. Spiral lines around the forearm's bone axis on the wrap's material, and long lines along the arm on the skin, wander and fade like the others. Result: the wraps read; the upper arm is still the flattest surface in a first-person shot (reported in the risks).
10. **The sources of the drawings are in `assets/source/video_art/<name>/`, not beside the textures.** Godot imports an SVG under `assets/video/`. `assets/source` has a `.gdignore`.
11. **One variant per process for the acts' A/B.** The looks add nodes to the level that no act removes, so a second act in the same process applies them twice (the background got darker). `tools/video/shoot_still.sh "Asset: <shot> <variant>"` per variant; the same holds for LD-INK's acts.

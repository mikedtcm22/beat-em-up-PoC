# SHOTS: judgement calls

Each call: the decision, an alternative not taken, why. (The trailer's 14 shots, the beat script, the cue sheet, the camera sampling and `shoot.sh`: `video/shots/t*`, `video/cine/`, `video/stage/`, `video/acts/trailer_acts.gd`, `tools/video/`.)

## Calls
1. **A shot's script is a sequence of beats that wait for game state, not a list of hand-timed presses.** Each `CineBeat` fires in array order: it waits for its time (`at_s`, absolute, or `relative` to the beat before it fired), then for its `trigger` (the actor is `lead_ticks` from its strike, is staggered, is down, is free; the player is holding, is free, is at the top of a jump), for `timeout_s` at most. Alternative: absolute times only, as `moving_punk` had. The shots need a perfect block (a 14-tick window), a grab (only a staggered enemy, in a 1.3 m reach, after the jab's recovery), a spin kick at the jump's peak and a haymaker on an Ox who has got up. Every one of those moves when a number elsewhere changes (a tuning, a look piece that adds a light), and a re-time of 14 shots by hand costs more than the triggers do. The run is still deterministic: the triggers read the simulation, which `--fixed-fps 60` and the seeds fix. A trigger that never comes fires at its timeout and the cue sheet says so (`trigger X timed out`).
2. **The sequencer is `CineScript`, a plain class with no game in it** (`step(tick, trigger_met, on_fire)`), and the bot supplies the trigger reads. Alternative: the loop inside `cine_bot.gd`. It is tested without a level (`test_shots_script.gd`: the order, the relative waits, a trigger that holds the script, a timeout, several beats in one tick). `on_fire` runs each beat the moment it is decided: the first version collected the firings and fired them after, so a "wait for the player to be free" beat read the state before the press just before it, and a grab went out on the same tick as the hook it should have waited for.
3. **The cue sheet is a log of the combat events, in video frames.** `CineEventLog` subscribes to `CombatEvents` (the hits with their move, tier, blocked, perfect and killed flags; attacks and wind-ups; knockdowns, KOs, grabs, throws, weapons, jumps, landings, blocks, hazard KOs, the boss phase) and writes `<key>_events.log`: `EVENT f<video frame> t<shot-clock tick> <event> <detail>`. The frame is counted from the window's first tick (frame 0), the same pick rule the writer uses, so the sound placement needs no arithmetic. The beats are logged too (`beat #n PRESS P`), with the actors' end state. Alternative: the bot's own `log_line` of hits only. The edit needs the block, the whoosh (attack_started), the body fall and the weapon landing as well, and every shot needs them in one format.
4. **Slow motion is `TimeController.slowmo` through a `SLOWMO` beat; `CineShot.edit_speed` is only a note for the cut list.** Shots 7 and 12 (0.25x) slow the simulation, so the 24 fps picks of the slowed sim are smooth, the finisher's own 0.3x for 250 ms plays inside it, and nothing in `video/` writes `Engine.time_scale` for a shot (the bot only puts it back to 1.0). Alternative: render at 1x and slow the clip in the edit (a hold of each 24 fps frame: a stutter). The beat comes 0.1 s after the last punch's press, so it starts at the uppercut's contact.
5. **Shot 7 is on the catwalk's west edge, not the top roof's parapet.** Tried first: the top roof's west parapet (the still `roof_silhouette`'s place, with the sun behind it). The parapet is a solid block and the world stops a launch at it ("a launch the world stops right away"): the Kicker fell at its foot. Then the terrace's west edge, where the edge guard lets a launched body through and the hazard takes it: the sun is not behind it (a building is). The catwalk's west edge (`EdgeGuardCatwalk`, x 1.85, the `DropCatwalk` hazard past it) looks into the sun and the guard is a ghost for a launched body: a real `hazard_ko` of the Kicker, 1.3 s after the last hit, against the sun and its halo. Alternative: a synthetic launch like `knock_off()` (a `receive_hit` with a hand-set knockback). That is not the game's own action.
6. **The camera path is sampled at the output frame's time, `n / 24`, not at the tick** (`CineDirector.camera_time`). At 24 fps the picks of the 60 Hz ticks land on 0, 3, 5, 8, 10, 13: sampled at the tick's own time the path steps 3/60 then 2/60 s. Sampled at `floor(tick * 24 / 60) / 24` it steps 1/24 each frame (tested both ways in `test_shots_script.gd`). Within one output frame the camera time is constant, so the main window's other ticks see the same pose.
7. **`shoot.sh` fails on the shot's own failures.** Exit codes: godot's own; 3 no `SHOT ... frames=` line (the run did not reach the end), 4 the writer's `problems` (an ffmpeg encode or a PNG save) or `CineFrameWriter: ffmpeg exit` in the log, 5 neither `<key>.mkv` nor `<key>.png`, 6 ffprobe cannot read the mkv, 7 a `SCRIPT ERROR` or `SHADER ERROR` in the log. Godot's exit-time leak report (the `ERROR:` lines about RIDs and "never freed") is still not a failure: it prints on every run. The output key is read from the log's own `SHOT` line, so a `--shot-tag=` run is checked under its tagged name. `tools/video/shoot_trailer.sh` runs the list, goes on after a failed shot and exits 1 if any failed.
8. **`CineDirector.restore()` puts `show_center_dot` back** (`remember_overlays()` keeps it before the overlays' hiding turns it off).
9. **The shots are written by a generator, `tools/video/gen_trailer_shots.py`, from one table.** Alternative: hand-written `.tres` like the Gate 2 shots (about 150 lines each, dominated by sub-resource boilerplate). Fourteen shots re-timed by hand while the blocking changes would have cost more than the generator. The generator reads the enums of `cine_beat.gd` and `cine_camera_key.gd`, so the numbers in the files follow the scripts. The files are committed and are what the game reads (Godot never runs Python); edit the table and re-run `~/.venvs/beat-em-up-video/bin/python -I tools/video/gen_trailer_shots.py`. The Gate 2 shots (`moving_punk`, `crane_street`, the stills) stay hand-written.
10. **Shot keys are `tNN_name`, one scene and one `CineShot` each, with `number`, `edit_start_s` and `edit_speed`** (PLAN.md §5's number, time and speed) so `trailer_acts.cut_list()` gives the animatic's order from the data. The `crane_street` shot of Gate 2 stays as it was; `t01_crane_street` is shot 1 with the cast changed.
11. **The cast is varied in every shot with more than one figure** (blind tell 3). `t01` shows a Punk, a veteran, a Thug and a Kicker, no two in the same pose (a live idle, a held `taunt`, a live idle at another yaw, a held `brace`); `t08` a Punk and a Punk Veteran at different yaws. The tests: no shot shows one catalog id twice, and a group of three or more has at least three distinct poses. Only the Punk and Ox have the round 1 art, so the others (Kicker, Thug, Bruiser) are in with the game's art until LD-ASSET's second round.
12. **Every enemy type and the boss are in the list** (Punk, Veteran, Kicker, Thug, Bruiser, Ox), and every location. The list as written is 9 first-person shots and 5 cine (shot 14 is the title's backdrop): 24 s of 37 s of gameplay is first person if the windows are cut at the plan's lengths (65%, below the 70% the plan asks the lead to hold). Cutting shot 1 and the cine shots' lengths in the edit is the lead's call.
13. **Shot 11 is 8.1 s, not 6.** The plan's beats are all real and in order: the heavy (the shoulder charge) blocked, the next one perfect-blocked (a parry), P P · P (the spinning backfist, a knockdown), the haymaker held and released. Ox is down for about 1.9 s after the backfist and the haymaker needs him on his feet, so the shot waits for it (`ACTOR_DOWN`, then `ACTOR_FREE`), and the player steps 1.2 m in while he rises (the haymaker whiffs from 2.6 m). The release is at 7.2 s and the haymaker lands at 7.4 s: the edit cuts to shot 12 on the release, which is 7.2 s of this clip; it can drop the first, ordinary block (2.3 s) to land on the plan's 6 s. The log has every tick, so the cut is placed from the cue sheet.
14. **The haymaker's hold begins with a jab that whiffs** (`attack_whiffed jab_1`, then the haymaker on the release). That is the game: a press starts the jab and the hold converts on release. Kept, because the shots are real gameplay; shot 12 and 13 show it for 0.3 s before the fist comes back for the haymaker.
15. **The pipe and the bike chain are laid on the floor in front of the player** (`CineProp`, a weapon scene as `weapon_acts.gd` lays it, 0.2 m up on its side) and picked up by a real GRAB with the view tilted down, as the weapon act does. Alternative: spawn the player holding it (no pickup beat; the plan's "pick up his dropped pipe" is the pickup).
16. **Shot 8 shows the chain on two Punks as the game plays it: swings 1 and 2 on the first, and the third (the padlock, the finisher) launches him into the second** (`hit_landed ... impact_body_knockdown` on the Veteran). The chain's hits go to one target at a time; the third knocks both down.
17. **Shot 13: Ox has 14 health, so the haymaker (18) KOs him; a health of 20 left him alive and he got up.** Alternative: the real fight to his last hit. The cue sheet logs `ko` at the hit.
18. **Shot 14 has no actors: a free camera drifting across the skyline of the boss roof.** The title is the edit's. Its test case: only a free-camera shot may have no actor (`test_ld_stage_shots.gd`: "has an actor" -> "has an actor, or is a free-camera shot").
19. **Marks, not text.** Shot 10's name card ("OX PELLERIN"), shot 13's cut to black and shot 14's title are `MARK` beats in the cue sheet (`name_card`, `cut_to_black`, `title_in`): the HUD never reaches the writer's picture and there is no text in the video's render; the edit sets the type.
20. **The blocking pass is at 1280x720 with no character mask** (`--shot-size=1280x720 --shot-mask=0`; `shoot_trailer.sh` sets both), about 0.04 s a frame, 25 s a shot. The final renders say `TRAILER_SIZE=2560x1440 TRAILER_MASK=1`.
21. **Test changes (old -> new).** `test_ld_stage_shots.gd`: `test_the_moving_shot_is_five_seconds_of_first_person_punches` -> `..._that_end_with_the_action` (5.0 s / 300 ticks -> 3.4 s / 204 ticks; its twin on the visible path: the window ends within 1.3 s of the last punch, and a tilt-up beat exists); `test_every_shot_is_well_formed`'s "has an actor" -> "has an actor, or is a free-camera shot". Nothing else was weakened.
22. **`moving_punk` (Gate 2 finding 10): the head stays in frame and the shot ends with the action.** The Punk's cap left the top of the frame as the player closed in (first person, the player walks 1.5 m forward, a lunge adds 0.8 m). The player starts looking +2 degrees (was -3) and a PITCH beat tilts the view up to 12 degrees over 0.5 s from 0.5 s. The window is 3.4 s (was 5 s): the last punch is at 2.2 s, the launch lands at about 3.0 s, and the 1.6 s after that was a static tail.

## For each shot: drawn or 3D, as a viewer who has not been told would read it
Judged at 1280x720 blocking, beside the round 2 look (the pieces on `video/cinematic-trailer` at this commit; LD-WORLD3 and LD-ART2 land in parallel). The brief's question is the camera and the action; the look is the other packages'.

- **t07 (the roofs):** the sun disc with banded halos and the brick courses read as drawn. The player's body and the Kicker are the game's lit toon figures with one line weight: 3D in the figures, drawn in the world.

- **t08, t09 (roofs, first person):** the world reads drawn; the arm is the faceted game mesh with the black holes (LD-ART2's finding 1), which reads 3D.

## How to run it
- **The list:** `tools/video/shoot_trailer.sh [out dir] [keys...]` renders `video/shots/t*.tscn` one after the other (default: all 14, 1280x720, no mask, into `captures/video/shots_blocking/<key>/`, about 25 s a shot and 6 minutes in all). Each shot folder holds `<key>.mkv`, `<key>_events.log` (the cue sheet), `<key>_stats.json`, `<key>.sha256` and `shoot.log`. The final renders: `TRAILER_SIZE=2560x1440 TRAILER_MASK=1 tools/video/shoot_trailer.sh captures/video/shots`. One shot: `tools/video/shoot.sh t05_grab_throw` (`SHOOT_ARGS="--shot-size=1280x720 --shot-mask=0"` for the blocking size; the other `--shot-*` switches are in `cine_bot.gd`'s header).
- **A look:** `tools/video/shot_sheet.sh <shot.mkv> <out.png> [fps] [cols] [rows] [tile px]` (one shot's frames), `tools/video/shot_strips.sh <out.png> <tiles> <px> <mkv>...` (a row a shot).
- **Change a shot:** edit its entry in `tools/video/gen_trailer_shots.py` and run `~/.venvs/beat-em-up-video/bin/python -I tools/video/gen_trailer_shots.py`; read the cue sheet (`beat #n ... player (x, z) actor0 (x, z)` says where the action was at each beat, and `end_state` where it ended) before the sheets.
- **The cut list for the animatic:** `trailer_acts.cut_list()` (number, key, edit start, length, speed, location, FP or cine), or the shots' `number`/`edit_start_s`/`edit_speed`.
- **The tests:** `godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -s addons/gut/gut_cmdln.gd -gdir=res://video/tests -gexit` (114 now; 24 are SHOTS': `test_shots_list.gd`, `test_shots_script.gd`).
- **`shoot.sh` failing:** with `FFMPEG=/nonexistent` a run exits 5 (and prints the writer's problem), where it used to exit 0.

## The numbers (1280x720, no mask, this machine, the round 2 pieces)
- 14 shots, 1,180 frames in all (the windows add up to 49 s of 24 fps, against the plan's 41 s: the edit trims, mostly shot 11), 0.03-0.05 s a frame, 0.05-0.15 MB a frame in the mezzanine, about 25 s a shot with the process start. At 2560x1440 the Gate 2 figure was 0.31 s a frame: the whole list is about 5 minutes of render.
